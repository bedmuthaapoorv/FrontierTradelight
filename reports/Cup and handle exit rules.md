# Build cup-and-handle exits the code can execute

The data needed for a cup-and-handle `exit_rules.md` comes down to five inputs the exit recomputes for itself: the entry price (Open of T+1, not Close of T), the signal day T, the recomputed Pivot and HandleLow, a 22-bar ATR, and a 50-day SMA. Each exit rule must be written so that `resolve_exit(entry_price, entry_date, signal_history_df, future_df)` can evaluate it from those inputs alone. The recommended v1 design follows. Use a close-based initial stop at `max(0.99 × HandleLow, 0.92 × entry_price)`, filled at that day's close. A close below the pivot alone is not an exit, because 62% of cups throw back. A narrow "stalled breakout" exit applies in sessions 11-21. A trade that gains 20% within 15 sessions gets an 8-week (40-session) hold floor, during which only the hard stop or a 50-day-SMA break can close it. Otherwise a ratcheting chandelier trail of 3.0 × ATR(22) below the highest close governs (4.0 × for big winners). There is no breakeven move and no partial sale, a 30-session stagnation stop, and a uniform 90-session max hold. Two parts of the evidence base cannot be built as written. The backtester has no partial exits, so "sell a third at +2R" is impossible. The measured-move target needs the cup's left rim and low, which the live Sell tab's ~135 rows of history often cannot reach. The report gives the closest executable substitute for each. The evidence is also honest about its limits. Rigorous studies of individual stocks, and a long-only replication of the best-known momentum stop study, say stops cut risk rather than raise returns. Bulkowski's data favours wider trails and finds breakeven stops harmful. The one target-versus-trail backtest favoured targets, but with a different entry. No rigorous time-stop evidence exists. NSE gap and circuit-lock frequencies have never been published and must be measured from the project's own data. Two things must happen before any backtest: correct the entry spec's claim that the backtester buys at Close[T], and clear the stale, date-shifted backtest cache.

## Five code constraints decide which exit rules are legal

The compiler is a small model (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, temperature 0) that turns the markdown into `generated_exit_strategy.py` (`generate_exit_strategy.py:106-113`). The spec therefore has to name exact row positions, formulas and priority order. The parser reads only one line, `Exit Strategy Name: <name>`, which keys the leaderboard together with the entry name (`backup_utils.py:8-13`, `stats_utils.py:39-74`). Use a new name such as `Cup and Handle Exit Strategy v1`, and copy the existing section layout: Metadata, Data Availability, Rationale, Entry Reference, Pre-Trade Calculation, Daily State, Daily Update Logic, Exit Conditions with `Reason Tag`s, Priority Order, Fallback, State Inspection. The exit is global: every backtest trade uses the single compiled file, whatever the entry strategy (`backtest.py:57, 289`).

**Constraint 1: entry is at the next day's open.** The backtester passes `entry_price = future["open"].iloc[0]`, the Open of T+1, and `future_df` row 0 is that entry day (`backtest.py:279-284`). The entry spec says otherwise in three places. Rule 9's description says the backtester "enters at the signal day's close". Rule 11's description says "Entry happens at the signal day's close". The Output section says "the backtester itself enters at Close[T]" and defines `Initial Stop = max(0.99 * HandleLow, 0.92 * Close[T])`. Fix all three. Rule 9 should justify close-based signals only by non-repainting. Rule 11 should say the 5% chase cap is checked on Close[T], but the fill at Open[T+1] can gap above it. The Output's Initial Stop should be labelled an estimate that uses Close[T] as a proxy for the unknown next open. The earlier entry-side report had this backwards. It said the existing `exit_rules.md` wrongly assumed a next-day-open entry. In fact `exit_rules.md` was right and the entry spec is wrong. For the exit, this means every percentage is anchored to `entry_price`. The Open can gap above 1.05 × Pivot, which is why the 8% cap must exist. It can also gap below 0.99 × HandleLow, which puts the initial stop above the entry. A close-based check then fires on day 1's close unless price recovers. That is the intended behaviour: the structure failed before the trade began. The spec should say so explicitly, so the LLM does not add a guard.

**Constraint 2: one exit per trade.** The return is a single dict with `ExitDate`, `ExitPrice` and `ExitReason`, or `None` (`generate_exit_strategy.py:51-68`). There is no scaling out, no pyramiding and no risk-based sizing. Positions are equal-weight slots (`backtest.py:473-538`).

**Constraint 3: no screener values reach the exit.** Pivot, HandleLow, Rank Score and the initial stop computed by `evaluate_stock` never reach `resolve_exit` (`backtest.py:450-453`). The exit must recompute them from `signal_history_df` using the entry spec's Steps 1-2. **T is the last row of `signal_history_df` whose date is strictly before `entry_date`.** In the backtest every row qualifies, so T is the last row. In the live Sell tab, `signal_history_df` ends on the EntryDate itself (`sell_screener.py:120-121`), so the "strictly before" wording is what makes the rule work in both modes. With p = T's position:

- C = the latest position of max `high` over [p−22, p−5].
- `Pivot = high[C]`.
- D = the latest position of min `low` over [C+1, p−1].
- `HandleLow = low[D]`.

About 23 rows are needed, which both modes always have. Do **not** depend on A, B, K or SMA200. The left rim can sit 250+ bars back, and the live screener fetches only `entry_date − 200` calendar days, about 135 trading rows (`sell_screener.py:66-79`). If Pivot or HandleLow cannot be computed, the stop falls back to `0.92 × entry_price` alone. Tell users to record the actual fill day (T+1) as EntryDate in `holdings.csv`. With that convention, live `future_df` row 0 is also the entry day, so day counts match the backtest.

**Constraint 4: `None` or an exception means hold to range end, not skip the trade.** The caller closes the trade at the last close in range with the tag `RangeEnd` (`backtest.py:297-300`). Both existing exit specs wrongly say `None` lets the caller skip. Every failure path in the new spec should fall back to a defined stop. In live mode a silent `None` shows up as a HOLD.

**Constraint 5: the mechanics fix the defaults.** Triggers default to the daily close. Intraday high or low may be used only if the spec says so explicitly (`generate_exit_strategy.py:48-49`). The entry day is day 1, and its close is tested (`generated_exit_strategy.py:29`). Max hold is enforced only by the exit's own row count. No transaction costs are modelled (`backtest.py:307`). Equity is carried at cost, not marked to market, so a rule that holds losers longer is not penalised in MaxDD until the loss is realised (`backtest.py:511, 551`). The Sell tab adds three more quirks:

- `describe_hold_reason` displays only `TrailingStop`, `DistanceToStopPct`, `DaysHeld` and `DaysToMaxHold`, and hardcodes "/90 trading days" (`sell_screener.py:177-186`).
- The live frames duplicate the entry-day bar (`sell_screener.py:120-121`). Any rolling indicator that concatenates `signal_history_df` with `future_df` must drop duplicate index labels, keeping the last, or the live ATR and SMA will double-count that bar.
- A live-price bar has NaN volume (`sell_screener.py:82-107`). Any volume condition must treat NaN as "condition not met".

## The handle-low stop caps risk, and the evidence says that is all it does

The rigorous literature agrees on one point that should set expectations for the whole exit. **Kaminski and Lo prove that under a random walk a stop-loss always lowers expected return.** Their "stopping premium" is negative by exactly the risk premium earned while stopped out ([Kaminski & Lo, pp.10, 16-17](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). Stops can add value by two routes. The first is momentum strong enough that the serial correlation ρ is at least the per-period Sharpe ratio, a high bar of about 0.29 monthly for an annual Sharpe of 1. The second is a regime-switching market whose bad regime earns less than the safe asset, combined with a stop that identifies that regime accurately ([Kaminski & Lo, pp.19-24](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)).

Their rule is not a trade stop. It is a 0/1 switch out of an index when the rolling J-period cumulative return falls below −γ, with re-entry once a single period returns at least δ. They tested it on daily S&P 500 futures against 10-year Treasury-note futures from 1993 to 2011, with no transaction costs ([Kaminski & Lo, pp.9, 11-12, 25](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). The earlier notes' "monthly 1950-2004, 50-100 bp/month" figures are not in this version. Short-window stops (daily and weekly) had negative stopping premiums. Monthly-plus stops could raise return and cut volatility, by about +1.5% return, −5% volatility and up to +20% Sharpe in one calibration ([Kaminski & Lo, pp.5, 26-27](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). Part of that gain came from bond-futures momentum while stopped out, and a stopped-out backtest trade here earns nothing. The paper says nothing about individual stocks or breakouts. Grade: **rigorous, indirect**. Its only transferable lessons are two: hair-trigger stops on short windows destroy expected return, and a stop should be judged on volatility as well as return. **Lo and Remorov, testing individual US stocks from 1964 to 2014, found tight stops underperform buy-and-hold** on a mean-variance basis once a 0.2% half-spread is charged. Wide 10-day stops "generally perform about as well as buy-and-hold" while raising skewness and cutting maximum drawdown ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)). Dai et al. covered 25,997 US stocks from 1926 to 2016 and found trailing stops have lower gross returns but lower volatility and better tail behaviour than buy-and-hold ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/trailing-stop-loss-effectiveness-for-stocks)). Han, Zhou and Zhu's 10% stop on momentum portfolios, 1926-2013, is often quoted as the strongly positive exception. It cut the worst equal-weighted winners-minus-losers month from −49.79% to **−11.36%** (the earlier figure of −11.34% here was a typo), and it "more than doubled" the Sharpe ratio ([Alpha Architect, pp.2, 7](https://alphaarchitect.com/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)). That headline is a **long-short** result, and most of the return gain comes from the short leg.

The long (winners) leg alone tells a smaller story. Under the 10% stop, average monthly return moved from 1.24% to 1.27% and Sharpe from 0.160 to 0.211. Skew went from −0.29 to +0.69, and the worst month from −33.06% to −12.87%. A 15% stop hurt the winners leg ([HZZ Table 1, reproduced in Alpha Architect, p.2](https://alphaarchitect.com/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)). Vogel of Alpha Architect re-ran the long leg only on mid and large caps (above the NYSE 40th percentile), 1927-2013, gross of costs, using daily 5% and 10% stops. **The no-stop portfolio had the higher CAGR and Sharpe. The stop portfolios had lower drawdowns and higher Sortino ratios.** He judged that a 300-600 bp cost haircut for the extra daily trading "might eliminate all the benefits" ([Alpha Architect, pp.3-6](https://alphaarchitect.com/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)). Vogel does not address look-ahead or fill price. Grade: HZZ **rigorous** but contested on implementability; Vogel's replication **practitioner**, and its table did not survive the supplied PDF.

So even in the momentum context closest to a breakout entry, a stop on a long book buys tail control, not return. The stop is there to bound the left tail, and a backtest should judge it on drawdown, skew and net-of-cost expectancy, not on CAGR alone.

### Initial stop: handle low minus 1%, capped at 8% below entry

The recommended rule is `InitialStop = max(0.99 × HandleLow, 0.92 × entry_price)`, computed once at entry. Exit tag `"InitialStop"` fires when `Close ≤ InitialStop`, and the fill is at that day's close. The rule stays active every day, including during the big-winner hold. Bulkowski names the handle low as the cup stop ([Bulkowski, cup](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)). O'Neil's 7-8% loss cap is the backstop ([AAII](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/when-to-sell-and-nail-down-your-profits-while-you-still-have-them)). Grade: **practitioner**. The structural part is backed by Bulkowski's largest stop study: 3,068 upward breakouts, with a stop 2 cents below the lowest low of the prior N days ([Bulkowski, Stops2](https://thepatternsite.com/Stops2.html)).

| Reference low | Stop hit within a month | Average loss |
|---|---|---|
| Breakout-day low | 61.5% | 2.19% |
| Low from 1 day before breakout | 41.0% | 4.29% |
| Low from 10 days before breakout | 14.9% | 7.53% |

A handle low spans 5-22 sessions, so it behaves like the 10-day row. That is an extrapolation, since cups were not in the sample. The **1% buffer is proposed**. Bulkowski uses only "a penny" or "a few pennies" ([Bulkowski, Stoplocate](https://thepatternsite.com/Stoplocate.html)), and no study compares percentage buffers. The tighter-of-two construction works because the entry spec caps handle depth at 12%. With a shallow handle the structural stop governs. With a deep handle and an entry gapped high into the buy zone, the 8% cap governs. R is defined as `entry_price − InitialStop` and is at most 8% of entry. It is useful only for state reporting, since sizing cannot use it.

Two newly read sources point to tighter stops, and neither justifies changing the rule. On HZZ's winners leg, a 5% stop beat 10% (1.53% against 1.27% a month). But that is a gross, micro-cap-inclusive portfolio that re-buys fresh winners every month, and Vogel's mid/large long-only replication found no CAGR or Sharpe gain from either stop ([Alpha Architect, pp.2-5](https://alphaarchitect.com/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)). Minervini's "normal stop loss is 5%" is an illustration in a secondary article about a leveraged, quick-profit style, not a tested rule ([TraderLion, p.3](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management/)). Lo and Remorov's cost result and Bulkowski's hit rates still argue against a tighter stop. **The initial stop is unchanged.**

### Gap-down fills: trigger on the close, fill at the close

The recommended fill rule is close-trigger, close-fill for every exit, including gap days. Do not model intraday-low stops. The standard daily-bar convention is that a stop gapped through fills at the next open, not at the stop level ([arXiv 1412.5558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.5558); [AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/backtests-sl-mismatch/)). Slippage is asymmetric and always runs in the losing direction ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/775565)). A close-based rule already puts each gap inside the realised price, so it cannot flatter the loss side the way "fill at stop" does. Lo and Remorov also found that delayed execution did marginally better than immediate execution for tight stops, because of short-term reversal ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)). That argues against selling at a gapped-down open. Grade: **practitioner** (fill convention) and **rigorous** (reversal finding, not breakout-specific).

An intraday variant would trigger on `Low ≤ stop` and fill at `min(Open, stop)`. It is executable and could be a sensitivity run. Bulkowski's high hit rates on nearby lows suggest it will whipsaw more. Either way, have the analysis log realised loss divided by planned R. Bulkowski's average losses are measured to the stop price and ignore gaps ([Bulkowski, Stops2](https://thepatternsite.com/Stops2.html)), so NSE realised losses will be larger.

### Failed breakouts: tolerate the throwback, exit on a stalled one

Do not exit on a close below the pivot alone. Bulkowski reports a **62% throwback rate for cups** ([Bulkowski, cup](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)). Across 10,305 upward breakouts the throwback takes about 10 days to return to the breakout price. After returning, **35% keep falling and 65% recover**. Trades that held at or above the breakout price rose 40% on average, against 29% for those that dipped below it. High-volume breakouts, which the entry's 1.4× gate selects, throw back 70% of the time ([Bulkowski, Throwbacks](https://ThePatternSite.com/throwbacks.html)). The current exit's `Close < 0.98 × Pivot` within 10 days would cut most of the best trades. Hard structural failure, a close below the handle low, is close to Bulkowski's "busted pattern" definition: a move of no more than 10% before reversing through the opposite side ([Bulkowski, BustedPatterns](https://www.thepatternsite.com/BustedPatterns.html)). The initial stop already covers it.

The recommended additional rule, tag `"FailedBreakout"`, is **proposed**. On sessions 11 through 21, exit at the close if `Close < Pivot` and the highest close since entry is below `1.05 × entry_price`. The window opens after the ~10-day average throwback round trip and closes at the ~30-calendar-day throwback horizon, about 21 NSE sessions ([Bulkowski, Throwbacks](https://ThePatternSite.com/throwbacks.html)). The 5% progress test spares trades that ran first and are now retesting. No study tests any "close X% below pivot after N days" threshold, so this rule should be compared against a version without it.

### Breakeven move: none

The recommendation is **no breakeven move**. Bulkowski's only systematic test covered 2,333 double bottoms. Raising the stop to breakeven at half the target cut winning trades from 1,727 to 1,042 and net profit per share from $2.17 to $1.45. Raising it to the pattern midpoint also hurt ([Bulkowski, QStopAdjust](https://www.thepatternsite.com/QStopAdjust.html)). Grade: **practitioner**, and the sample is double bottoms, not cups. The opposing view, never let a 10%+ gain become a loss, is IBD folklore with no data behind it ([Motley Fool thread](https://discussion.fool.com/t/trading-ibd-stocks/104584)). Minervini does move his stop to breakeven once a trade gives positive feedback, now confirmed from the full TraderLion article ([TraderLion, p.6](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management/)). That source is **secondary**, has no data, and frames the rule within a leveraged style that "locks in gains" quickly and accepts "losing out on the longer-term trade" ([TraderLion, pp.3-4](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management/)). That is the opposite of this exit's 8-week-floor, wide-trail design, so the recommendation stays at no breakeven move. Dropping the move costs little: a 3 × ATR chandelier measured from the highest close passes breakeven by itself once the trade is up roughly 3 ATRs.

## Winners need a wide trail, an 8-week floor and no partial sale

### Trail: chandelier 3.0 × ATR(22), ratcheting, 4.0 × for big winners

The recommended trail is `Trail = HighestClose_since_entry − M × ATR(22)`, with M = 3.0. ATR is recomputed daily on the de-duplicated concatenation of `signal_history_df` and `future_df[:i+1]`. The effective stop is `max(InitialStop, highest Trail so far)`, so it only ever rises. Exit tag `"TSL"` fires when `Close ≤ effective stop` and the trail is above the initial stop. M becomes 4.0, irreversibly, when the big-winner flag fires. LeBeau's chandelier default is 22 periods and 3 × ATR ([Incredible Charts](https://www.incrediblecharts.com/indicators/chandelier_exits.php)). The current exit uses ATR(14). Moving to 22 matches the published default and Bulkowski's best volatility-stop lookback, 2 × the average 22-bar range ([Bulkowski, stops](https://thepatternsite.com/stops.html)). Grade: **practitioner** for the default. No head-to-head ranking of chandelier, MA and SAR trails on base breakouts exists, so the multiple is **proposed**.

The direction of the evidence is clear: wider is better. Bulkowski's lowest-low trail returns rose with every extra day of lookback, from 2.42% at 0 days to 5.66% at 10 days ([Bulkowski, Stops2](https://thepatternsite.com/Stops2.html)). In his 571-stock test, percentage trails tighter than about 36% lost money against buy-and-hold ([Bulkowski, CanStopsHurt](https://thepatternsite.com/CanStopsHurt.html)). Snorrason and Yusupov found 15-20% trails best and 5% worst on OMX Stockholm ([Lund](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/publication/1474565)). He also found that a tight initial stop handing over to a volatility trail beats the volatility trail alone ([Bulkowski, Stops2](https://thepatternsite.com/Stops2.html)). The `max(InitialStop, Trail)` construction does exactly that. Resist anything tighter than 3 × ATR. A 2 × ATR or 10-day-low trail would sit inside normal throwback noise. Bulkowski's ultimate high is by definition followed by a 20%+ decline, so a trail of roughly 20%, or 3-4 ATR for NSE volatility, is about the tightest that still captures the move ([Bulkowski, Time](https://www.thepatternsite.com/Time.html)).

### Big winners: hold 40 sessions, exit only on the hard stop or the 50-day SMA

The recommended rule sets the flag `BigWinner` the first time `Close ≥ 1.20 × entry_price` on or before session 15. While the flag is set and the session is 40 or earlier, the trail does not exit. Only `"InitialStop"` or a new `"BigWinner50DMA"` tag can close the trade, the latter when `Close < SMA(close, 50)` and `Volume > SMA(volume, 50)`. After session 40 the 4.0 × trail governs. O'Neil, via AAII, says to hold at least 8 weeks from the breakout if a stock gains 20% within 1-3 weeks ([AAII](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/when-to-sell-and-nail-down-your-profits-while-you-still-have-them)). Grade: **practitioner**. The 50-day break with heavy volume is IBD's distribution signal ([StockCharts ChartWatchers](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-chartwatchers-2021-02-one-key-signal-that-its-time-t-215/)) and is **secondary-only**. "Heavy" has no verified threshold, so above-average volume is a **proposed** reading. The disciples' version adds a "trades below the breach-day low next day" confirmation ([TradingView](https://in.tradingview.com/chart/VNINDEX/AgmKyPf9-Vnindex-Apr-2022-Rules-of-violation-of-the-50-day-MA)). That is executable, but it adds state for little evidence, so leave it out of v1.

### Climax tops: leave out of v1

O'Neil's climax signals are defined: the biggest one-day gain since breakout, the widest weekly range of the run, an exhaustion gap, and seven or eight up days out of ten ([AAII](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/when-to-sell-and-nail-down-your-profits-while-you-still-have-them)). He frames them as signals after "a run of many months". The commonly quoted "65-100% above the 200-day" extension figures are not confirmed as his ([TinTin Trading](https://tintintrading.substack.com/p/how-to-identify-signs-of-climax-runs)). With a 90-session cap, the precondition is rarely met, and no backtest of climax signals exists. The recommendation is to **omit it from v1**. A v2 candidate is executable: on session 20 or later, if `Close ≥ 1.25 × entry_price` and at least 8 of the last 10 closes were up closes, exit with tag `"Climax"`. The 8/10 count is **practitioner** (O'Neil). The other thresholds are **proposed**.

### Profit targets and scaling out: what the code forces

The evidence on targets cuts against the trail-only design, and the spec should say so. Arthur Hill tested S&P 500 stocks from 1997 to 2017. An **8 × ATR(22) profit target earned 8.99% CAR against under 7% for a 4 × ATR chandelier trail, at similar drawdown**. A 20% fixed target averaged about 9.5% CAR, and combining a trail with a target was worst ([StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2017-05-systemtrader---trailing-stops-versus-profit-targets-for-a-moving-average-crossover-system/)). Grade: **practitioner**. But the entry was a moving-average crossover on large caps, which have a thinner right tail than NSE breakout leaders. On the other side, cups average a 54% rise on perfect trades, yet 47% retrace substantially within two months and 23% never gain more than 15% ([Bulkowski, cup](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)).

The evidence-favoured hybrid cannot be built. Minervini's rule is stated two ways. The full TraderLion article says sell **half** at a gain equal to the original stop (+1R), then in the next sentence says the "best way" is half at **double** the stop (+2R), with the stop raised to breakeven. It also says to "sell into strength at a multiple of your risk" ([TraderLion, pp.6, 10](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management/); secondary, internally inconsistent). The "a third to a half at 2-3R" wording appears only in a Substack summary ([Stock Trading Approach](https://stocktradingapproach.substack.com/p/selling-into-strength-a-strategy)). The article contains no maximum loss per trade, position size, win rate, gain/loss ratio or trailing rule. That rule, and the "50% partial at +20-25%" from the entry report both need partial exits, which the backtester does not support (`backtest.py:293-320`). Three substitutes are executable:

- **Full exit at a fixed target.** This is O'Neil's own literal rule: "sell many of your stocks when they are up 20% to 25%", with the 8-week rule as the exception ([AAII](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/when-to-sell-and-nail-down-your-profits-while-you-still-have-them)).
- **Move the stop to breakeven at +2R.** This is the stop half of Minervini's rule, now confirmed from the article (p.6). It contradicts Bulkowski's breakeven finding.
- **Tighten the trail at +2R.** This contradicts the wider-is-better evidence.

**The measured move cannot be built reliably.** Bulkowski's actual measure rule is narrower than the "add the cup depth" shorthand used earlier: measure from the right cup lip down to the lowest valley, multiply by his 61% target-hit rate, and add the result to the breakout price, i.e. `Pivot + 0.61 × (High[C] − Low[B])` ([Bulkowski, cup page printout, p.2](<C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/Downloads/Cup with Handle.pdf>)). It still needs the cup low B, which can sit beyond the live history. When it is missing, `resolve_exit` returns `None` and the Sell tab silently shows HOLD. The closest substitutes are a percentage target or Hill's 8 × ATR(22) target, both of which need only ~23 bars.

The recommendation is to make **v1 trail-only**. Then run one paired variant, `Cup and Handle Exit Strategy v1-T`. It adds a full exit with tag `"Target"` at `Close ≥ 1.25 × entry_price` for trades without the BigWinner flag, so big winners stay exempt under the 8-week rule. Let the leaderboard decide. Hill's "combined was worst" result warns that the variant could lose. That is exactly why it should be run rather than assumed.

### Stagnation stop and max hold

The recommended time stop, tag `"TimeStop"`, is **proposed**. On session 30 or later, exit at the close if `Close < 1.05 × entry_price` and the highest close since entry is below `1.10 × entry_price`. No rigorous or systematic time-stop evidence exists. The nearest anchors are throwback timing and the 47% of cups that drop within two months ([Bulkowski, Throwbacks](https://ThePatternSite.com/throwbacks.html); [Bulkowski, cup](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)). Thirty sessions is beyond the ~21-session throwback window. The rule is a "no progress" exit, not a cap on winners. Bulkowski found 61% of upward breakouts take longer than the pattern's own width to peak ([Bulkowski, Time](https://www.thepatternsite.com/Time.html)). Treat 20/30/40 as a robustness check, not a tuning grid.

For max hold, the recommendation is **90 sessions for every trade**, tag `"MaxHold"`, counted as rows of `future_df` with the entry day as day 1. Grade: **proposed**. It matches the Sell tab's hardcoded "/90" (`sell_screener.py:184`). The entry report suggested 120 sessions for big winners. Do not adopt that until `sell_screener.py` reads the hold from the exit's own `describe_state`. Otherwise the Sell tab will display it wrongly.

## NSE closes, circuits and costs change what a backtest fill means

**The close a backtest "fills" at depends on the date.** Until 2 Aug 2026 the official NSE close was the 3:00-3:30 pm VWAP ([Equities India](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/closing-price-weighted-average)). **From 3 Aug 2026, F&O stocks close at a Closing Auction Session**: continuous trading ends at 3:15 pm and the auction runs 3:15-3:35 pm ([NewsOnAir](https://newsonair.gov.in/nse-to-introduce-new-method-for-closing-price-discovery-through-closing-auction-session-from-tomorrow/)). Non-F&O stocks keep the VWAP close. Under the auction, orders must sit within ±3% of the reference price, and **stop-loss orders are cancelled automatically** ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/closing-auction-session-cas-explained-nse-bse-closing-price-rules-2026)). The Sahi article is a single practitioner source, so verify it against NSE. The benchmark range, 2021-2026, is entirely pre-auction. Its close fills are approximations that a trader could only have tracked by spreading the order over the last half hour. That argues for a small adverse cost on close exits in post-processing. Live, a close-based exit should be executed as an auction order for F&O names, or by hand near 3:25 pm otherwise. A resting broker stop will not survive the auction. The decision should use data available around 3:15-3:25 pm, which matters most for the volume-gated 50-day rule.

**Circuit locks can make a close-based exit unfillable.** Non-F&O stocks carry fixed 2/5/10/20% bands. F&O stocks get a dynamic 10% band that flexes in 5% steps, so they rarely lock ([Rupeezy](https://support.rupeezy.in/support/solutions/articles/21000004845-what-are-circuit-limits-or-price-bands)). A stock locked at its lower circuit has no buyers ([Tradejini](https://www.tradejini.com/blogs/why-your-stoploss-doesnt-work-during-gap-opens)). GSM stocks trade with bands of 5% or lower, and at stage III only once a week ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/graded-surveillance-measure)). The recommended rule is executable but **proposed**. If an exit triggers on a bar with `High == Low` and `Close < previous Close`, treat that bar as locked and defer the exit to the next bar where `High > Low`, filling at its close with the original reason tag. If no such bar exists, the trade falls to `RangeEnd`. No published frequency exists for NSE lower-circuit locks, so **count `High == Low` down days in the project's own universe data** before deciding whether the rule is worth the complexity. The entry's ₹5 crore/day turnover floor already removes most lock-prone names. Point-in-time ASM/GSM lists are not stored, so surveillance exclusion stays a proxy.

**Gaps are the main driver of losses beyond the stop, and nobody has published their frequency.** Indian results mostly land outside market hours and hit at the next open ([Swastika](https://www.swastika.co.in/blog/post-market-earnings-shock-how-after-hours-results-move-stocks-before-opening-bell)). Options implied a 2.3% average results-day move for Nifty 50 members in October 2025 ([Bloomberg via BGov](https://news.bgov.com/international-trade/traders-see-calmest-india-earnings-season-as-they-look-past-tcs)). Mid and small caps move more, but no NSE single-stock gap distribution exists in the notes. The exit has no earnings calendar, so pre-results exits are not possible. Holding overnight is structural to the strategy: momentum abnormal returns accrue overnight ([LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119010)). Before trusting the stop distances, measure `Open / previous Close − 1` across the universe, the share of gap-downs beyond −3% and −5%, and how often the Open of T+1 lands below 0.99 × HandleLow.

**Costs belong in post-processing, not in the exit.** At a zero-brokerage broker, a delivery round trip costs about **0.238% on ₹1 lakh and 0.226% on ₹5 lakh**, and STT at 0.1% on each side is about 85% of it ([Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges)). Budget 2026 left delivery STT unchanged ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/what-is-stt)). The flat ₹15.34 DP charge adds about 0.15% on a ₹10,000 position. The exit could technically shade its returned `ExitPrice` to embed costs, but that corrupts the price log and the Sell tab. The better approach is to subtract about 0.24% per trade, plus slippage of 0.1-0.3% for large caps and 0.5-1% for small caps on stop exits, from the trades CSV. The slippage figures are the researcher's judgement, not sourced. Because the leaderboard is pre-cost, **a rule that trades more often looks better than it is**. That argues against tight stops and short time stops, and it should be stated in the spec's rationale. Vogel's verdict on daily-monitored momentum stops makes the same point. The extra trading "might eliminate all the benefits" ([Alpha Architect, p.6](https://alphaarchitect.com/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)). Short-term capital gains tax is a flat 20% inside a 90-day hold, so tax does not shape the rules ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/ltcg-sale-stocks)).

**Settlement adds a small edge case.** Under T+1, a sale on the day after purchase is BTST. If the original seller short-delivers, it can be auctioned with a penalty of up to about 20% ([Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/trending/consequences-of-short-delivery-nse-bse)). A day-1 close exit is a same-session round trip, which the notes do not cover. Keep the pipeline's day-1 checks, since a broken structure is real risk, but report how often day-1 and day-2 exits occur.

**Clear the stale cache before relying on any dates.** All 100 files in `data/backtest_cache/` were written on 2026-09-25, before the fix for the jugaad_data 18:30 UTC off-by-one bug. Their rows are stamped a day early. `ABB_2024-08-21_2026-09-25.csv` has 105 Sunday rows. The cache never expires (`backtest.py:202-209`), so it must be cleared by hand. Relative exit logic is unaffected because bars are counted by row. But SignalDate, EntryDate, ExitDate and the point-in-time `members_as_of` lookups are shifted, and mixing old and new files would misalign cross-sectional snapshots (`backtest.py:420`). The 2021-2026 benchmark range is not in the cache and will fetch corrected data.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "good exit rules" means for this project. The constraint that matters most is not the evidence. It is the four-argument function signature and a ~135-row live history. Those two facts rule out the measured-move target in live use and the scale-out rules that practitioner sources favour. They also force the exit to re-derive the pattern from raw bars on every call. Within what is executable, the evidence supports only a few firm choices: a structural stop well behind the breakout, tolerance for throwbacks, a wide trail and no early breakeven. Everything time-based is a researcher's guess. The stop's real job, per the rigorous literature, is shaping the loss tail, not lifting returns. The leaderboard's Composite Score is pre-cost, carries positions at cost, and never sees unrealised drawdown, so it can reward the wrong exit. Judge the variants on net-of-cost expectancy and the realised-loss-to-R distribution as well as the score.

Three measurements on the project's own data would do more than any further reading: NSE gap-down frequency through planned stops, lower-circuit lock frequency in the turnover-filtered universe, and how often the Open of T+1 lands outside the [0.99 × HandleLow, 1.05 × Pivot] band. With those numbers in hand, the v1 versus v1-T comparison becomes the first real Indian test of whether cup-and-handle winners should be trailed or harvested. Fix the entry spec's Close[T] wording and clear the cache first, or the test will start on the wrong prices and dates.
