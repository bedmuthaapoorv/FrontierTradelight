# KEC's bounce exposed a regime gap, not a bug

**The screener is not missing something about KEC. It is missing something about the market, and that gap is by design and now wider.** The user's data show KEC did not rise more than 1.5% on both of the last two sessions. It fell 1.53% on Tue 10-27 and rose 1.96% on Wed 10-28 close-to-close, and the only clear KEC short loss in the repo data is the 10-28 entry (-2.14% open-to-close, with the 1x ATR stop not hit). The "up on both days" pairing most plausibly matches Mon 10-26 and Wed 10-28 (+2.76% and +1.96%), or Wed 10-28 and today, Thu 10-29. An undated Groww quote shows previous close 338.15, which equals the local 10-28 close, and a +1.79% intraday move, so the quote is probably from today. KEC ranked 2nd of 13-14 passers on the 10-26 and 10-27 signal days because it is a bottom-1% 12-1 loser (about -61% over 12 months, 36% below its 200-day average) with Rs 78 crore of daily turnover, which alone adds about 23 rank-score points. The whole passer list lost on all three recent entry days (mean short P&L -1.14% to -1.72%) during a relief bounce from extremely oversold breadth (11 to 20). KEC also had a real, public catalyst: a record Rs 2,872 crore Power Grid order, with Q2 results possibly around 2 Nov (unconfirmed). The strategy is performing at its stated base rate (57.8% short win rate versus the ~59% claimed). The one real design question is market regime, which the breadth-gate removal made more pressing. No single-name filter is justified by this evidence.

## A bottom-1% loser at a 52-week low is exactly what the ranking selects

KEC passes every rule by wide margins and the scoring is deterministic, not noisy. On the 10-26 signal, momentum rank 0.249 gave a momentum score of 95.0 and liquidity gave 77.6, for a rank score of **89.80**. On the 10-27 signal, momentum rank 0.748 gave 83.13 (`local_diagnosis.md`, Q2). The rank percentile quantizes coarsely, since one position among about 409 names is 0.244 percentile points, so bottom 5% is only about 13-15 passers, and "top 3" means top 3 of about 14. KEC appeared on 117 of 220 backtestable signal days and in the top 3 on 29. Because the liquidity term pays 30% weight regardless of direction, a Rs 70-80 crore name with a bottom-1% rank lands near the top most days.

The "rallying through the filters" story does not fit KEC's multi-week data. It was down 14% to 18% over 21 days at each signal, below its 20-, 50- and 200-day averages, and only 4.5% to 6.6% above a fresh 52-week low set on 10-23 (intraday 317.25). What the rules cannot see is the last one to three days: no filter looks at the previous day's move, gap, relative volume, sector, news or market regime (the rules file makes breadth informational only). The 10-26 signal day had a +2.76% jump on 3.3x volume and the strategy still scored KEC second. That is a genuine blind spot, but a design choice documented in the rules, not a coding error. The notes did not find a bug in `calculate_momentum_ranks`, the liquidity calculation or the rank arithmetic, which the local researcher recomputed and reproduced to within one rank position of the saved report.

## The whole list lost together, in a market bounce

The more important finding is that KEC was not special. On the three most recent entry days the passer list's mean open-to-close move was +2.03%, +1.07% and +1.77%, with short win rates of only 20%, 36% and 21%, and mean short P&L of -1.72%, -1.14% and -1.58%. On 10-27, KEC was actually better than the list (its short won +0.70%, while the list lost). On 10-28 the top three lost mostly because KPITTECH gapped and ran +5.35% open-to-close and hit its stop (-3.50%), with KEC (-2.14%) and RPOWER (-2.41%) behind it. Breadth at the signals was 11.25, 14.18 and 19.80 (percent of stocks above their 50-day average), following universe-wide open-to-close drops of -1.80%, -0.25% and -2.14% on 10-21 to 10-23. In other words, the list had been an excellent short on those very days (vectorised mean short P&L of +1.74%, +0.58% and +3.72% by signal day from 10-19 to 10-22) and then lost three entry days running as the market snapped back.

This is the textbook pattern in the momentum-crash literature. Daniel and Moskowitz find that crashes occur in "panic" states, after market declines and high volatility, when the loser leg rebounds violently, and that loser betas can exceed 3 after declines ([NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). A one-day trade differs from their monthly holding period, so the evidence supports the regime concern but not any specific threshold. Two cautions on the local market proxy. The local researcher had no index data and used the mean open-to-close of today's 409-stock universe, which shows +0.37% on 10-28. Published closes for 28 Oct show the Nifty 50 down 0.22% and the Sensex down 0.48%, with the Nifty IT index down more than 2% ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/stock-market-closing-bell-sensex-falls-353-points-to-73246-nifty-ends-below-22700-as-it-stocks-drag)), and 27 Oct was reported flat ([IndiaIPO](https://www.indiaipo.in/news/detail/closing-bell-sensex-nifty-end-flat-on-expiry-day-pharma-realty-shine-it-drags)). Open-to-close versus close-to-close, index-heavy IT weakness and survivorship in the universe can all explain the difference, so "the market rallied" is solid for the beaten-down end of the market (the user's list) and weaker for the index. The honest summary is that the bounce was concentrated in oversold names, not that the market rose broadly.

## A real catalyst existed, but the news was public and the sample says it is variance

The market-context research found a company-specific cluster that a price-only screener cannot see: a record Rs 2,872 crore Power Grid 765 kV order, filed Saturday 24 Oct and traded on Monday 26 Oct, plus Rs 1,006 crore of orders on 23 Oct and about Rs 1,030 crore on 8 Oct ([IndiaIPO](https://www.indiaipo.in/news/detail/kec-international-secures-record-2872-crore-transmission-orders-from-pgcil); [Equitypandit](https://www.equitypandit.com/kec-international-shares-rise-after-securing-rs-2872-crore-power-grid-orders/); [DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/kec-international-wins-rs-1030-crore-orders-takes-ytd-intake-beyond-rs-8600-crore-id022)). The setting fits an oversold name after weak Q1 FY27 (PAT down 42% to Rs 73 crore, per [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/kec-international-q1-fy-27-results-pat-down-42-to-rs-73-crore/)) getting an order-win relief bounce. Q2 results are listed for 2 Nov on Groww, but the notes could not confirm this with a filing, and past Q2 dates were 4 Nov 2024 and 10 Nov 2025 ([IndiaInfoline](https://www.indiainfoline.com/company/k-e-c-international-ltd-share-price-board-meeting/corporate-action/27273)). The notes found no analyst action, block deal, index change, or F&O or ASM status issue, and no KEC news dated 27 or 28 Oct, but absence from searches is not proof of absence. Treat the 2 Nov date and a single-source claim that Power Grid lifted a tender restriction in June 2026 as unverified.

The base-rate evidence says none of this needs a rule change. Across 220 signal days and 3,023 pass-events, passers rose open-to-close 41.2% of the time and the short won **57.8%** with mean gross P&L of +0.17%, matching the claimed ~59% and +0.22%. Top-3 names did slightly better (61.0% win, +0.28%). KEC itself had a 65.8% short win rate over its 117 appearances, and consecutive double losses on KEC happened in 8.5% of consecutive appearances. List-level, mean short P&L was negative on 44.5% of days, two in a row 20.9% of the time, and three in a row 8.6%. Three straight bad days is uncommon but not exotic, and these losses cluster in bounce regimes rather than occurring independently, so independence arithmetic understates them. Caveats apply: this is one year of today's constituents (survivorship), without costs or slippage, and differs from the point-in-time Nifty 500 validation the user cites. Also note that a claimed edge of about 20 bps gross faces roughly 4-8 bps of estimated round-trip costs before slippage (an unverified own-calculation from Zerodha's published rates, [Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated)), so thin-edge filters that remove many trades can be net negative.

## What to change: nothing for KEC, one experiment on regime, three cheap diagnostics

Sorting the issues: there is **no bug** in the ranking arithmetic, and the stale `stock_report.csv` is a workflow issue (verify the file's timestamp against the last data bar before trading from it). The KEC losses and the three-day list slump are **expected variance concentrated in a bounce regime**. The **genuine design gaps** are two: no market-regime control, and no awareness of the most recent one to three days (news or gap bounces). The user's removal of the breadth gate today matters here, though I could not see the earlier gate's logic or test it; the diagnosis notes only that the current rules treat breadth as informational and that breadth never filters a stock. One nuance cuts against simply restoring it: the list earned its best returns on the same very-low-breadth days (11-14) that preceded the bounce, so a level gate such as "skip when breadth below X" would have forfeited those gains. The harm came from the turn, a sharp rise in breadth or a market rebound after a deep drop, which is what Daniel and Moskowitz describe.

The cheapest, least-overfit sequence is therefore as follows. First, run zero-parameter diagnostics on the existing 220-day trade log: split P&L by signal-day breadth bucket (for example tercile cuts) and by the 3-5 day change in breadth, and chart win rate and expectancy by rank bucket, since deepest losers are the highest-beta names. These add no rules. Second, if the breadth-change split is monotonic and holds in both halves of the year, test one gate (halve size or skip when breadth rose sharply over the prior days), with a single pre-declared threshold, and evaluate it on expectancy rather than win rate. Third, consider volatility scaling on the strategy's own trailing P&L, the best-evidenced risk control (Barroso and Santa-Clara found it nearly doubled the momentum Sharpe ratio, per the [paper abstract](https://ciencia.ucp.pt/en/publications/momentum-has-its-moments/)), though that evidence is for monthly momentum. A cap of one or two names per sector is a low-risk addition (sector rank is already computed), because correlated bounces hurt more than any single name. The ranked candidates in the filter research are plausible but weakly evidenced.

Single-name filters should wait. The local back-test of candidates found only 1-3 point win-rate differences, inside noise: signal-day return of +2% or more gave 58.3% versus 57.5% for down days, a positive 5-day return gave 56.3% versus 59.0% (and lower mean P&L), and signal-day volume of 3x or more gave **58.4% win and +0.39% mean**, better than average, not worse. A "skip if up 2% on the signal day" rule would have removed KEC only on the 10-26 signal. The theory is also ambiguous: Medhat and Schmeling find high-turnover stocks show short-term continuation ([CEPR DP15857](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP15857.pdf)), which would argue for avoiding shorts after high-volume up days, while the user's own data show the opposite sign for the 3x-volume subset. Entry gaps above +1% are costly (51.2% win, -0.36% mean) but are unknowable at signal time and only observable at the open. Most of the supporting literature is monthly-horizon and developed-market, from search summaries and abstracts, not full papers, and Lou, Polk and Skouras suggest momentum profits accrue overnight, so an open-to-close short earns a reversal or microstructure effect that academic momentum studies do not cover ([LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)). Any filter should require a prior mechanism, a same-sign effect in both halves of the data, loss of no more than 20-30% of trades, and a monotonic bucket pattern. A 3-5 point win-rate effect cannot be detected with fewer than several hundred trades per arm (the filter research's own arithmetic).

## Conclusion

The KEC episode is a clean illustration of a strategy whose edge lives in a regime-dependent reversal, not in any single stock. The screen worked as written: KEC really was a bottom-1% loser at a 52-week low, and the list really was a good short until the market turned. What changed was the regime, and the one decision that now deserves scrutiny is the breadth-gate removal, not KEC. Because the list's best days and worst days both came at breadth below 20, any gate must key on the rate of change, and the user's own trade log can answer that before a single new rule is written.

The notes leave several loose ends that limit confidence: the intended pair of dates, KEC's 10-29 data and its rank on the 10-28 signal, the 2 Nov results date, peer and sector-index moves, and the actual breadth and P&L split by regime. The most valuable next step is therefore to confirm the dates and run the breadth and rank-bucket splits. This is research, not personalized investment advice.
