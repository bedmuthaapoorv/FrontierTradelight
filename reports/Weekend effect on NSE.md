# Mondays on NSE bring bigger gaps, not losses

The classic weekend effect, negative returns from Friday's close to Monday's close, is not a reliable feature of the Indian market today. What the weekend does reliably change is **risk, not drift**. In the published Indian literature, the US-style negative Monday never clearly appeared. Where post-2000 studies find anything significant, Mondays are usually *positive*, and most studies find no day-of-week effect at all once T+2 rolling settlement arrived in 2003. In your own cached Nifty 500 data (Dec 2019 to Dec 2025), the equal-weight Monday return was 0.21 pp below other weekdays (p = 0.04). That gap comes almost entirely from a handful of 2020 crash Mondays and disappears once 2020 is excluded (p = 0.26), when returns are winsorised, or under a rank test. What survives every period is that the **Monday opening gap is about 27% larger in absolute size** (0.82% against 0.65%), Monday volatility is higher, and a stock is **about three times as likely to open 5% or more below Friday's close** as below a normal weekday close. Those tail gaps cluster on a few systemic crash Mondays, such as 7 April 2025 and 23 March 2020, so they cannot be diversified away. For your backtesting app this has three consequences. Friday signals filled at Monday's open need no special handling, because they perform in line with other days over 5 to 20 sessions. Your current exits are close-based, which is the conservative choice, but the engine itself does not stop a future exit rule from filling a stop at a price the Monday open had already jumped past. And the weekend's real cost shows up at the portfolio level, as correlated gap losses that the trade log does not currently surface.

Throughout, three kinds of claims are kept apart. **Published evidence** is labelled **[India]**, **[US]** or **[Global]**. **Cached-data results** come from the analysis of `Strategies/Default/data/backtest_cache`: 692 symbols, point-in-time Nifty 500 membership with a median of 495 members a day, 1,508 trading dates, Muhurat and special DR sessions removed. These are equal-weight stock averages, not the cap-weighted index. About **150 hypothesis tests** were run, so a Bonferroni-style bar is p < 0.0003, or roughly |t| > 3.6. Results that clear it are flagged. Everything else at p = 0.01 to 0.05 should be read as suggestive at best. **Inference** is reasoning from the two and is marked as such.

## The negative Monday faded in the US after 1987 and never clearly took hold in India

### What the weekend effect is, and where it historically lived

**[US]** Cross (1973) first documented the pattern that French (1980) named the weekend effect ([ASU](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20171204-research-debunks-myth-stock-market-weekend-effect)). French found that S&P Composite Monday returns for 1953 to 1977 were significantly negative, and that in every five-year sub-period the average Monday was the lowest of the week ([SAJBM literature review](https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/download/1064/1005)). Keim and Stambaugh traced negative Mondays back to 1928, across firms of all sizes ([FMG DP568](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/publications/DP568.pdf)). The most important finding for a backtester came from Rogalski (1984). He split the weekend return into Friday close to Monday open and Monday open to Monday close, and found the effect was **"really a Friday-close-to-Monday-open effect"**: the losses arrived while the market was shut ([SAJBM](https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/download/1064/1005)).

Four explanations have support and none dominates. **[US]** Firms tended to release bad news after the Friday close ([EFMA 2007 review](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0263.pdf)). DellaVigna and Pollet found that Friday earnings announcements draw a 15% weaker immediate response and a 70% stronger delayed response than other days, with a 45% higher chance of a negative surprise ([NBER w11683](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11683)). Individual investors trade relatively more on Mondays and tilt towards selling ([Lakonishok & Maberly 1990](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v45y1990i1p231-43.html)). Speculative short sellers closing positions before the weekend and reopening on Monday is a third explanation ([Chen & Singal 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i2p685-705.html)), though it is disputed by evidence that shorting explains no economically meaningful part of the gap ([JOIM](https://joim.com/do-short-sellers-cause-the-weekend-effect)). **[India]** The fourth is settlement mechanics. Under fixed-day weekly settlement, Nifty returns were inflated on Wednesday, the first day of the settlement cycle, and the effect vanishes once returns are adjusted for the settlement lag ([Badhani & Tripathy 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijbema/v2y2010i1p23-42.html)).

### The effect decayed, then reversed in large caps

**[US]** After about 1987 the negative Monday disappeared from large-cap indices and in several studies flipped sign. Mehdian and Perry found significantly negative Mondays in all five US indices before 1987 and significantly *positive* Mondays in the NYSE, S&P 500 and DJ Composite afterwards ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jbfnac/v28y2001i7-8p1043-1065.html)). The Boston Fed found **no weekend drift after 1987**, but weekend volatility stayed above that of ordinary weekdays ([Fortune 1998](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/research-department-working-paper/1998/weekends-can-be-rough-revisiting-the-weekend-effect-in-stock-prices.aspx)). That pairing of vanished drift and persistent variance is exactly what your own data shows. The weak Monday lingered longest in broad and small-cap indices such as NASDAQ, the Russell 2000 and CRSP ([Cho, Linton & Whang, FMG](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/publications/discussion-papers/are-there-monday-effects-stock-returns-stochastic-dominance-approach)), and in Mondays after down Fridays. **[Global]** Across 40 indices in 15 countries, conditioning Monday on the sign of the previous Friday produced a significant difference in 11 of 13 small-cap indices but only 4 of 14 large-cap ones ([Edinburgh thesis](https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstreams/bbda7f15-25e0-43d4-91f6-162e14acde23/download)). A 15-exchange study reports the effect gone in the US, UK, France and Germany ([Eunomia](https://ojs.akademiarac.edu.pl/index.php/eunomia/en/article/view/268)). Long-run US data show Monday losses that were substantial in 1926 to 1974 and statistically insignificant in 1975 to 2014 ([ASU](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20171204-research-debunks-myth-stock-market-weekend-effect)). More broadly, published return predictors lose roughly 58% of their in-sample return after publication ([arXiv 2105.01380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)).

### Indian studies point every direction, and mostly to nothing

**[India]** Before rolling settlement in 2001 to 2002, the dominant Indian day-of-week pattern was a settlement-driven Wednesday effect. On NSE data for 1995 to 2005, that Wednesday effect disappeared and Monday and Tuesday returns, though low, were no longer significantly different ([Badhani & Kavidayal](https://i-scholar.in/index.php/ijfm/article/view/40948)). On Nifty, the day-of-week return effect existed before T+2 and **vanished after it**, leaving only a volatility effect, mainly on Tuesday ([Sen, IJFM](https://publishingindia.com/archive/ijfm/an-investigation-of-the-day-of-the-week-effect-on-return-and-volatility-of-nse-nifty)). Later index studies disagree in sign. Over 1990 to 2013, the traditional negative Monday is "non-existent" and Sensex shows a *positive* Monday ([Aziz & Ansari, MPRA 46805](https://ideas.repec.org/p/pra/mprapa/46805.html)). Nifty 50, Midcap 50 and Smallcap 50 for 2005 to 2018 show a positive Monday and a negative Tuesday ([Panda, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2981633)). Four Nifty indices over 2011 to 2018 show no day-of-week effect at all ([Saxena, Purohit & Malhotra 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/gbusec/v24y2021i1p43-58.html)). Nifty and Sensex for 2011 to 2021 show a positive Wednesday ([Elangovan et al. 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/intjaf/v11y2023i3p181-201.html)). Only a COVID-window study (March 2020 to September 2021) finds negative Mondays, and only Sensex is significant ([Yadav & Kadian 2024](https://www.publishingindia.com/archive/jcar/day-of-the-week-effect-during-covid-19-pandemic-an-empirical-study-of-indian-stock-market)). No study of the 2023 move to T+1 settlement or of the 2025 move of Nifty expiry to Tuesday turned up.

**Inference.** Significant findings that change sign from study to study (positive Monday, positive Wednesday, negative Tuesday, a COVID-only negative Monday) are what data-mining across specifications looks like. The prior that a tradable weekday return effect exists on NSE large caps today should be low. No peer-reviewed Indian study shows a day-of-week rule surviving STT, stamp duty and slippage. Delivery STT alone is 0.1% each side ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/?p=391776)), already more than any plausible post-2010 weekday difference.

### What survives is variance and continuation after Friday

**Cached data.** Three Monday features hold up. The mean stock absolute daily move is 0.25 pp larger on Mondays (t = 4.02, **clears the multiple-testing bar**), and the variance difference is significant under Levene's test (p < 0.001). The larger absolute gap (+0.18 pp, t = 3.49) narrowly misses the bar on the full sample but appears in every sub-period, including 2021 to 2025 (t = 4.02).

The second feature is that **Friday's direction carries through Monday**. After an up Friday, the equal-weight Monday averaged +0.24% with 53% of stocks up. After a down Friday it averaged −0.49% with 41% up (t = 3.97, **clears the bar**, n = 146 against 130). Both the gap and the session contribute. On ordinary weekdays the prior day's direction carries into the next morning's gap and then reverses intraday. On Mondays the reversal does not happen. At stock level, demeaned by date, Friday's winners beat its losers on Monday by +0.12 pp close-to-close (t = 4.20), while mid-week winners and losers end the next day level (t = −1.06). **Inference.** The weak average Monday is mostly what Mondays after down Fridays look like. Conditioning on Friday explains more than the weekday label does, which matches the global small-cap finding above.

The third feature is that **the Monday session pattern is tilted to smaller and more volatile names**. Monday open-to-close runs from −0.20% in the largest-turnover tercile to −0.35% in the smallest, and from −0.18% in the lowest-volatility tercile to −0.35% in the highest. The tercile tests sit at t ≈ 2 and do not clear the bar. **[India]** This fits older NSE evidence that Monday volume was significantly lower and Monday volatility consistently higher over 1995 to 2005 ([Badhani & Kavidayal](https://gnanaganga.inflibnet.ac.in:8443/jspui/handle/123456789/10797)). In your cache, Monday relative volume is lighter only in the largest names (0.94).

## Two days of news land in one opening auction

### Weekend variance is small per hour but lumpy

**[US]** French and Roll showed that volatility comes mainly from trading, not the calendar. Average open-to-close variance is more than six times weekend close-to-open variance, even though the weekend is roughly eleven times longer ([French & Roll 1986](https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/~kan/3032/pdf/AssetPricingAnomalies/French_Roll_JFE_1986.pdf)). So a weekend does not carry three days of risk. Your cache agrees: the Monday absolute gap is about 1.27 times a weekday overnight gap, not three times.

**Cached data.** The *tail* is a different matter. Gaps of −5% or worse occurred on **91.6 Monday stock-days per 10,000 against 32.3 on normal overnights**, and −10% gaps on 18.4 against 4.7. For stocks in the highest-volatility tercile, Monday −5% gaps ran at 129 per 10,000 ([wk_results_app.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/wk_results_app.txt)). The excess is concentrated. Two Mondays, 7 Apr 2025 (375 stocks gapping −5% or worse) and 23 Mar 2020 (342 stocks), account for 57.8% of all Monday −5% events. Excluding the two worst days in each group, Monday still runs 1.7 to 2.2 times the overnight rate, and on calm days 1.4 to 2.3 times. A date-level test gives only p = 0.14, precisely because the effect lives in a few days ([wk_results_extra.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/wk_results_extra.txt)). When a stock's low touches 5% below the prior close, the open was already through that level 13% of the time on Mondays against 9% on other days. At −10% it was 19.5% against 10.9%. Given a gap of −5% or worse, the average gap was −8.5% on Mondays.

**Inference.** The weekend is a jump-risk event, not a diffusion one. The average Monday is only modestly noisier, but the rare Monday crash is market-wide, hits every open long position at once, and opens beyond any resting stop. That is a portfolio problem more than a per-trade one.

### Why Monday's open has the least warning

**[India]** Several large companies report on Saturdays. HDFC Bank's Saturday 18 July 2026 results were followed by a Monday fall of up to 5.41% intraday ([Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/hdfc-bank-q1-results-shares-fall-after-earnings/)), while earlier weekend results produced Monday rises above 2% ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/hdfc-bank-shares-climb-over-2-after-q4-earnings)). Weekend Budgets get a full special session instead: Sunday 1 Feb 2026 ran a normal 9:15 to 15:30 session in equities and F&O ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/business/stock-market-update-bse-nse-to-conduct-regular-trading-session-on-budget-day-enn26013103011)). Overnight Nifty moves partly reflect US daytime trading ([NSE Research Paper 39](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/research/Paper39.pdf)). GIFT Nifty trades about 21 hours on weekdays but **not on Saturday or Sunday** ([Kotak Securities](https://www.kotaksecurities.com/indices/global-indices/sgx-niftysgx-nifty); [MNCL](https://www.mnclgroup.com/gift-nifty-trading-hours)). Sources disagree on exact session splits. The most widely quoted ends around 2:45 am IST on Saturday and reopens at 6:30 am on Monday. **Inference.** GIFT Nifty's Friday night session captures most of the US Friday move, but Saturday and Sunday news gets only about 2 hours 45 minutes of price discovery before NSE's pre-open auction. No published study measures how well GIFT Nifty's Friday close predicts Monday's Nifty open. The one small study claiming about 95% directional agreement used January to June 2023 only and reports inconsistencies itself ([IMDR Journal](https://imdr.edu/public/IMDR_JOURNAL_2023_24_Updated/files/basic-html/page269.html)).

### Options price the weekend before it happens

**[India]** India VIX shows a positive Monday effect and lower readings later in the week ([VGTU BTP](https://journals.vilniustech.lt/index.php/BTP/article/view/8279); [IUP](https://www.iupindia.in/1701/Applied%20Finance/Day_of_the_Week_Effect.html)). Sensibull describes option sellers pushing premiums and implied volatility down into Friday, with IV recovering on the next trading day. In its worked example over the 2017 Christmas long weekend, a Nifty put lost 9.7 points against about 19 points of quoted theta, because a 2.3-point IV rebound returned about 10 points ([Sensibull](https://blog.sensibull.com/2017/12/26/the-weekend-effect/)). The practitioner conclusion is that a Friday-evening seller collects roughly one day of decay, not three. **[US]** Jones and Shemesh find equity option returns significantly negative over non-trading periods, mostly weekends, and attribute this to mispricing of weekend variance. In their working sample of US equity options for 1996 to 2007, Friday-to-Monday returns averaged −0.62% while every other day was positive, with little effect in S&P 500 index options ([Jones & Shemesh, JF 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v73y2018i2p861-900.html)). No Indian replication exists.

**[India]** Since 1 Sep 2025, NSE index derivatives expire on Tuesday and BSE's on Thursday ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/nse-receives-sebi-nod-for-tuesday-expiry-bse-thursday-480736-2025-06-17)), which makes Monday the Nifty expiry eve ([Navia](https://navia.co.in/blog/weekly-fo-expiry-insights/)). **Inference.** The "IV falls on Friday afternoon" pattern is largely a measurement artefact: quoted IV uses a calendar-day clock while the market prices trading-day variance. Under the Tuesday cycle, a short option carried over the weekend into expiry eve holds maximum gamma against the Monday gap. **Cached data.** In the cash market, the Thursday-expiry regime showed no expiry-day return effect and no relative-volume response to weekly expiry. Monthly expiry days carried about 7% more relative volume (p = 0.023). The Tuesday regime has only 18 expiries in the cache, too few to say anything.

### Stops guarantee a trigger, not a price

**[India]** On NSE a stop-limit (SL) order fills only inside its limit band, so a gap past the band can leave it unfilled while losses grow ([Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/charts-and-orders/order/articles/what-are-stop-loss-orders-and-how-to-use-them)). A stop-market (SL-M) order guarantees an exit but not a price. One trader reports an SL-M rejected when the stock opened below its trigger ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/gap-downs-causes-sl-m-order-to-get-rejected/69221)), though this is an anecdote, not documented policy. **Inference, using the cached gap rates above.** A Friday-held long with a resting stop exits at roughly the Monday open, not at the stop, in roughly one in eight to one in five cases where the stop is hit by a large Monday move. A backtest that fills at the stop price understates exactly those losses.

## Friday signals are fine; unguarded stops and correlated Mondays are the risk

### How the engine meets the weekend today

**Code review.** Every trade enters at the open of the first row after the signal day. Intraday strategies use only that row and square off at its close. The exit price is whatever `generated_exit_strategy.resolve_exit` returns, used verbatim; when it returns nothing, the trade closes at the last close in range ([backtest.py](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Default/backtest.py)). The current active exit, for the Momentum Loser intraday short, fills its stop at exactly entry + 1×ATR when the entry day's high reaches it ([generated_exit_strategy.py](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Default/generated_exit_strategy.py)). Because the trade enters at that day's open, there is no overnight gap between entry and stop, so this is sound apart from ignoring intraday slippage. The multi-day exit rules you have written, GARP rebalance v2 and Cup and Handle, are deliberately close-based. Cup and Handle's rules say an overnight gap below the stop "is realized at that session's close" and forbid intraday high/low fills ([cup and handle exit rules](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/ExitRules_backup/cup_and_handle_exit_strategy_v1_exit_rules_20261014_131140.md); [GARP exit v2](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/ExitRules_backup/garp_rebalance_exit_v2_exit_rules_20261014_163440.md)). **Inference.** Close-based exits cannot be gamed by a gap, because the close already contains it. The weakness is that this protection lives only in prose. Nothing in `backtest.py` checks that a returned `ExitPrice` was achievable on the exit day. A regenerated exit strategy that triggers on `low <= stop` and fills at `stop` would silently fill long positions at prices the Monday open had already gapped through. Separately, `market_calendar.py` correctly keeps full Budget weekend sessions (1 Feb 2020, 1 Feb 2025, 20 Jan 2024, and the Sunday session of 1 Feb 2026). Any future "is Monday" feature must therefore be defined by time since the previous session, not by `weekday() == 0`. Otherwise the Monday after a Sunday Budget session is mislabelled as a weekend reopen.

### Friday-signal, Monday-fill trades perform like any other

**Cached data.** This test used a simplified breakout: a close above the prior 20-session high on more than 1.5× average volume, entered at the next open, before costs. It is not Horizontal Breakout v9 or your other generated rules. Friday signals (4,921 signals, 284 dates) filled at Monday's open matched Mon to Thu signals over 5, 10 and 20 sessions. The excess return difference at 10 sessions was −0.13 pp (CI −0.45 to +0.22) ([wk_results_app.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/wk_results_app.txt)). The Monday entry day itself is better: the entry-day open-to-close excess was **+0.13% for Friday signals against −0.12% to −0.34%** for other days, a difference of +0.34 pp (t ≈ 5.3, **clears the bar**, and holds in all three periods). That fits the Monday continuation result above, where Friday's winners do not fade on Monday. The edge is gone by session 5. Skipping Friday signals would remove about 20% of trades and move the average 20-session return by at most 0.07 pp. Fridays before long weekends did not differ from normal Fridays (19 dates, CI −1.19 to +1.04 pp).

The finding that does matter is about the **fill convention, not the weekday**. For Mon to Thu signals, delaying entry from the next open to the next close improved raw 5 to 20 session returns by about 0.4 pp. Friday signals are the only exception: delaying them gains about nothing, because the Monday session does not fade. The Friday-minus-other difference in that delay gain is about −0.4 pp (t ≈ −2.2 to −2.6 full sample; −2.7 to −3.5 in 2021 to 2025, close to but not past the bar). This repeats the holiday study's overnight-up, intraday-down pattern, partly inflated because dividends are unadjusted in the cache.

GARP v1's trades in `backtest_trades.csv` enter on only 22 dates, about 10 real cohorts, so entry weekday is entirely confounded with cohort. Monday's +22.9% and Tuesday's −0.5% average returns say nothing about weekdays ([backtest_trades.csv](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Default/data/backtest_trades.csv)). For the Momentum Loser intraday short, Monday is on average the most negative open-to-close day (−0.27% equal-weight), and Mondays after a down Friday are worse (−0.50%). But Monday's open-to-close is indistinguishable from other days in 2021 to 2025, so this is a hypothesis to test, not a filter to add.

## Conclusion

The weekend effect on NSE has turned from a return anomaly into a risk-distribution fact. The drift that made the effect famous in the US was gone there by 1987. India's version was tied to a settlement calendar that ended in 2003, and your six years of data cannot separate the remaining Monday shortfall from a few pandemic crash days. What the weekend leaves behind is fatter, more correlated opening gaps, plus a Friday-to-Monday continuation that, unlike ordinary overnights, does not reverse during the session. Neither is a reason to change when your strategies trade. Both are reasons to change how honestly the engine prices exits and reports portfolio drawdowns.

The most actionable surprise is that the weekday matters less than the fill convention. Friday signals do well on Monday's open precisely because Monday breaks the usual gap-up, intraday-fade rhythm that costs every other next-open entry about 0.4 pp. If that rhythm survives dividend adjustment and your real v9 filters, choosing between next-open and next-close fills will move results more than any calendar rule. And the one weekend safeguard worth hard-coding is a guard on stop fills: it costs a few lines and stops a future exit rule from hiding exactly the losses the weekend is most likely to produce.
