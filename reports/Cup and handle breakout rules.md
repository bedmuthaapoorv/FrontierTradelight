# Anchor cup-and-handle rules in confirmed pivots

A cup-and-handle `rules.md` can be built entirely from daily OHLCV, SectorRank and MarketBreadth, and the research supplies a specific number for almost every component. The core spec: prior advance of 30% or more into the left rim; a base of at least 35 trading days including the handle, with the cup itself 30 to 250 days long and 12-35% deep; a U-shaped bottom tested by a parabola fit; right rim within 20% of cup height of the left rim; handle 5-22 days long, at most 12% deep, with its low above the base midpoint, above the 50- and 200-day averages and on lighter volume; breakout on a close above the handle high, on at least 1.4x 50-day average volume, and no more than 5% above that high. The weak point is the evidence behind those numbers. **No O'Neil/IBD primary source was read**, because investors.com and the IBD reprints blocked automated fetching. A user-supplied copy of TraderLion's cup-and-handle article now confirms several of the figures directly: the 7-week minimum, the 1.4-1.5x volume floor, the 10-week-MA and upper-half handle rules, and the 7-8% stop. But TraderLion is a practitioner restating O'Neil, cites no data, and does not contain the 5% buy zone. Bulkowski's famous **5% failure rate and 54% average rise come from 913 visually identified "perfect trades"**, measured to the hindsight "ultimate high" on liquid US stocks. They are not a benchmark for an automated detector. The only rigorous large-sample study, O'Neil Global Advisors' 2022 study of ~197,000 breakouts, found that breakout volume **scales the size of the move but not the hit rate, which stays near 57% in every volume bucket**. The one peer-reviewed cup-specific test, Ahlawat (2016), found cup-and-handle significant mainly for US indices and in bull markets. It rests on 5-14 trades per cell and on a 20-40-day shape, so it cannot carry much weight. **No Indian-market evidence exists at all.** For implementation, the riskiest part is look-ahead bias. Swing pivots and centered smoothers need future bars. The spec therefore has to state, in words the code generator cannot misread, which bars each anchor may use on signal day T. The paired exit should mostly keep the existing horizontal-breakout pipeline. It should change in three places: a structural stop at the handle low capped at 8%, a failed-breakout test that tolerates the 62% throwback rate, and an 8-week hold floor for early big winners.

## Most thresholds rest on practitioner and secondary sources, not tests

The evidence grade matters for every row below, so it helps to set the grades out first. Only one source is **rigorous**: OGA's "Breakouts: Pump up the Volume" (May 2022). It covers US daily breakouts from 1995 to Sep 2021, uses a survivorship-free universe and computes CAPM alpha. But it pools all O'Neil base types, so it contains **no cup-with-handle-specific result** ([OGA PDF](https://www.oneilglobaladvisors.com/documents/FG/oneil/research/617948_OGA_Breakouts-OGA.pdf)). The **practitioner** sources are Bulkowski's own pages and the preview of his *Encyclopedia* (3rd ed.), the StockCharts ChartSchool definitions, the TASC 1999 code, the canslimTechnical repo, the commercial Abiroid MT4 indicator, whose defaults are fully documented, and TraderLion's cup-and-handle article (user-supplied, read in full). TraderLion restates O'Neil's rules and says it is unaffiliated with IBD ([TraderLion, p.10](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/cup-and-handle-pattern/)). So an O'Neil figure confirmed there is upgraded to practitioner, not to IBD primary. Despite its author's "data-based research" bio, the article cites no statistics. The **secondary-only** sources are the remaining O'Neil/IBD numbers, which come via LuxAlgo, AAII, Amphibian Trading and search snippets, plus Minervini's cheat-entry concepts. One academic paper tests cup-and-handle directly, Ahlawat (2016, *Algorithmic Finance*). It is peer-reviewed but **low-power** for this pattern. It tested a 20-40-day template shape, not the O'Neil base, with no volume rule, 5-14 trades per result and no multiple-testing correction ([Ahlawat 2016, pp.5, 15, 23](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/AF-160059)). It is discussed below. The **proposed-by-researcher** rows have no source and are design choices the researchers made to turn qualitative rules into code. Typical examples are the handle volume dry-up ratio, the V-shape test and the breadth switch for cup depth.

Bulkowski's headline numbers need special care. On 913 bull-market "perfect trades" he reports a **5% break-even failure rate, 54% average rise, 61% target hit rate and 62% throwback rate**, ranking the pattern 3rd of 39 ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)). The often-quoted "95% success" is simply the complement of the 5% break-even failure rate ([Liberated Stock Trader](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/cup-and-handle-pattern/)). His method explains why these numbers cannot be reached mechanically:

- **Entry and measurement.** A close above the pattern top is the breakout, and entry is at the next day's open. He deliberately tests no exit rule, because that would test the stop or indicator rather than the pattern. Performance is measured instead to an "ultimate high" ([Bulkowski, *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns* 3rd ed., book p.4](<C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/Downloads/L-0016326199-pdf.pdf>)). The available preview stops before he defines it. The research notes give it as the highest peak before a 20% decline. Either way it is a hindsight-best exit.
- **His own warning.** He says outright that his average rises are for comparing patterns. A trader earns them only by trading the pattern "perfectly", every time ([Bulkowski 3rd ed., book p.xi](<C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/Downloads/L-0016326199-pdf.pdf>)).
- **The database.** It is 1,396 mostly NYSE/NASDAQ stocks from July 1991 to 2020. Stocks usually trade above $5 with daily volume above about 100,000 shares, and prices are split- but not dividend-adjusted. Patterns were picked out by eye but logged in real time, so the sample is not cherry-picked after the outcome. It is still discretionary, not algorithmic ([Bulkowski 3rd ed., book pp.3-4](<C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/Downloads/L-0016326199-pdf.pdf>)).

The 54% is therefore a ceiling for liquid US stocks above $5, not an expected return for NSE trades with fixed exits. The Cup with Handle chapter itself (book p.294) is not in the preview, so the cup-specific figures above still come from his website. His website's cup page also carries his later trading study of 300 patterns from 1990 to March 2024, which is the more realistic prior: **47% dropped substantially within two months of breakout, and 23% rose no more than 15% before declining** ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)). OGA's pooled baseline is about **57% positive at 63 days, with 1.1% alpha**. An NSE backtest that lands near those figures before costs is behaving normally, not failing. The researchers also flag that once Indian round-trip costs are paid, the low-volume buckets' modest alpha could be largely consumed. That cost estimate is an assumption, not a sourced figure.

Ahlawat (2016) adds an academic data point but little power. It covers the 30 Dow stocks (1990-2015) and SPY plus nine Russell ETFs (2000-2015). Patterns were found by probabilistic-neural-network template matching on 20-40-day windows, then validated by an H-L-H-L-H extrema order on LOESS-smoothed closes. Volume was ignored. Trades were entered only after a confirming close 3 days later, and held for 10-50 days ([Ahlawat 2016, pp.5, 15-16, 21-23](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/AF-160059)).

- **Results.** In bull markets cup-and-handle was significant (one-sided t-test, 95%) for **8 of 10 indices but only 2 of 30 Dow stocks** (AXP, JPM). After 0.5% costs this falls to **4 of 10 indices and 2 of 30 stocks**. It was **never significant in bear-market periods** (Tables 6-13, pp.24-53).
- **Sample sizes.** Every result rests on **5 to 14 trades**, re-tested at five overlapping horizons. Roughly 5,600 tests were run with no multiple-testing correction, and the bull/bear split was chosen ex post (p.16).
- **Author's verdict.** He concludes that no pattern earns significant profits across the cross-section (p.1). Cup-and-handle is "more reliable" for indices than for individual stocks (pp.44, 54).

For this spec, the study is weak support for a regime effect and says nothing about O'Neil-length bases, the handle rules or the volume gate. The index-versus-stock contrast is no reason to expect an edge in NSE single stocks.

## Geometry thresholds, component by component

The table below collects the recommended Mandatory Filter values. "A" is the left rim, "B" the cup low, "C" the right rim (the handle high), "D" the handle low and "K" the start of the prior advance. One week counts as 5 NSE sessions.

| Handle volume dry-up | mean(Volume over handle) < 1.0 × SMA(Volume, 50) at C | Qualitative "contracting volume" ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/cup-with-handle-base.md)); no numeric source | proposed-by-researcher |

Several of these choices involve real judgment. On **prior uptrend**, Bulkowski says he does not "pay much attention" to it ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)). The researchers still argue for keeping a floor. Without one, a detector accepts dead-cat bounces inside downtrends, which is the classic case where a pattern looks right to the code and wrong to a chartist. If 30% proves too restrictive on NSE data, relax to canslimTechnical's 1.15 rather than drop the rule. On **cup length**, the 7-week minimum covers the whole base, handle included ([TraderLion, p.3](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/cup-and-handle-pattern/)). The earlier rule applied it to the cup alone (35 ≤ bars(A→C)), which was stricter than the doctrine. The revised rule requires 35 bars from A to the day before the signal, and 30 bars from A to C, so that a 30-bar cup with a 5-bar handle qualifies. Keep bars(A→C) ≥ 35 instead if you read Bulkowski's 7 weeks as applying to the cup alone. On **cup depth**, the upper bound moves from 0.33 to 0.35, following TraderLion's directly stated 12-35% ([TraderLion, p.4](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/cup-and-handle-pattern/)) over the 12-33% that reached the researchers only through LuxAlgo. The two are close enough that either can be fixed before backtesting. Depth is measured off the left-rim high. StockCharts uses a different convention, a fraction of the prior advance (≤1/3 ideal, 2/3 extreme) ([ChartSchool](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/cup-with-handle)), and the two should not be mixed in one rule. A vendor backtest suggests an inverted U: **15-30% deep cups won 57% at 10 days, against 52% for shallower cups and 50% for deeper ones** ([Chart Library](https://chartlibrary.io/blog/cup-and-handle-pattern-success-rate)). Its methodology is opaque, so use the finding as a ranking hint, not a filter.

**Roundness** is the least validated part of the geometry. TraderLion restates "U, not V" without a number ([TraderLion, p.3](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/cup-and-handle-pattern/)). No source shows that R² or any other shape metric improves post-breakout returns. Fit the parabola on closes, not highs and lows, because one wick distorts a quadratic fit. Treat the R² threshold as something to fix before backtesting and then leave alone. A lenient 0.60 is the safer starting value, since the side-duration rule already catches most V-bottoms. On **rim alignment**, no authoritative tolerance exists. Bulkowski says only that rims should be "near the same price level but be flexible" ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)). The 1.02 upper bound matters because a right rim well above the left means the stock has already broken out.

Two further items are deliberately left out of the Mandatory Filters. **Base count**, meaning a penalty for third- and fourth-stage bases, is computable. But IBD's figures (67% success for stage-3 bases, falling to 20% at stage 4) come with no methodology ([IBD via itiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2490440090)), and the counting and reset rules were never verified, so defer it to a later version. **Cheat and low-cheat entries** (a pause in the lower or middle third of the cup) are a separate trigger with their own risk profile ([lilys.ai notes](https://lilys.ai/en/notes/notebooklm-20251211/minervini-favorite-chart-cheat)), as is the **cup-without-handle** variant, which is bought at the left-rim high ([Yahoo/IBD](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/cup-patterns-without-handles-still-182000010.html)). A first version should require a handle and exclude all of these.

## Breakout confirmation with a 1.4x volume gate and a 5% chase cap

The breakout block is where the backtester's mechanics (entry at the signal day's close) meet the pattern doctrine. The recommended rules follow.

| Component | Recommended Condition | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pivot | Pivot = High[C] = max(High) over [C, T−1] | O'Neil handle high + 10 cents ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/cup-with-handle-base.md)); Bulkowski: close above the right rim ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)) | secondary-only / practitioner |
| Breakout | Close[T] > Pivot × 1.001 | The 10-cent buffer has no INR equivalent; 0.1% is the researchers' proxy. A MarketSmith-based backtest used 1% ([Levelup](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/trading-cup-and-handles-with-marketsmith-pattern-recognition-3b869d0cfe2a)) | proposed-by-researcher |
| First breakout only | No Close > Pivot on any bar in [C+1, T−1] | Researchers' design | proposed-by-researcher |
| Breakout volume | Volume[T] ≥ 1.4 × SMA(Volume, 50) computed through T−1 | "At least 40% to 50% above the stock's average daily volume" ([TraderLion, p.4](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/cup-and-handle-pattern/)); IBD via [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/cup-with-handle-base/). Neither states the averaging window; 50 days is convention | practitioner |
| Buy-zone chase limit | Close[T] ≤ 1.05 × Pivot | IBD 5% buy zone ([TraderLion](https://www.traderlion.com/technical-analysis/the-cup-and-handle-pattern/), search snippet only; **not present** in the user-supplied full copy of TraderLion's article, whose FAQ answers printed collapsed) | secondary-only |
| Invalidation | Reject if any Close in [C+1, T−1] < Low[D] | AGPro's invalidation rule ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/script/Kg8nENSG-Cup-and-Handle-Quality-AGPro-Series/)) | practitioner |

A close above the pivot is stricter than IBD's intraday trigger. It is also the deterministic, non-repainting choice for a close-entry system. The 5% chase cap carries more weight here than in IBD practice: entry happens at the close, so a gap-and-run day that would have filled at the pivot intraday fills up to 5% higher in this backtester. Wider than that, the entry-to-handle-low risk blows through the 8% stop.

The volume gate deserves the most careful framing in the spec's Description field. OGA's table by volume bucket (under 50%, 50-100%, 100-150% and at least 150% above the 50-day average) shows **hit rates of 57.7%, 56.9%, 57.1% and 56.9%**, essentially flat. Meanwhile **63-day alpha rises from 0.67% to 2.79%, average gain from 12.9% to 19.9%, and average loss deepens from −11.4% to −14.5%** ([OGA PDF](https://www.oneilglobaladvisors.com/documents/FG/oneil/research/617948_OGA_Breakouts-OGA.pdf)). Volume therefore tells you how big the move will be, not whether the breakout works. This contradicts the practitioner claim that a breakout without the volume surge "is more likely to fail" ([TraderLion, p.4](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/cup-and-handle-pattern/)). TraderLion offers no data for that claim, so OGA's result stands. Keep 1.4x as a validity gate, because it is the doctrine and it screens out drift-ups with no participation. Rank by the volume ratio, with a full score at 2.5x or more. Bulkowski adds that high-volume breakouts throw back 70% of the time, against 58% for all upward breakouts ([Bulkowski, Throwbacks](https://ThePatternSite.com/throwbacks.html)). A strict volume filter will not reduce whipsaws, and the exit has to absorb them. The notes do not research delivery %. Any use of it, for example as a tie-breaker on breakout-day delivery, is untested.

**Market and sector filters** have the weakest support of all. O'Neil's "M" rule gates buys on a confirmed uptrend, because roughly three in four stocks follow the market ([Motley Fool, restating IBD](https://discussion.fool.com/t/ibd-follow-through-day-ftd/36704)). The only cup-specific regime number is a vendor result: **59% win with SPY above its 50-day MA versus 47% in a downtrend** ([Chart Library](https://chartlibrary.io/blog/cup-and-handle-pattern-success-rate)). Ahlawat (2016) points the same way: his cup-and-handle results were significant only in bull-market periods, never in bear ([Ahlawat 2016, Tables 6-13](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/AF-160059)). But his periods were chosen ex post and each result rests on a handful of trades. OGA's positive alpha across pooled regimes suggests a hard gate may cut sample size more than it improves expectancy. The researchers therefore recommend **MarketBreadth as a ranking weight first**, compared against a hard gate in backtest. One implementation catch affects the proposed breadth switch for deep cups. `market_breadth` reaches `evaluate_stock` as today's single value. It cannot tell you what breadth was when the cup bottomed months earlier. Write the deep-cup allowance against today's breadth, or drop it, and do not imply a historical lookup the function cannot perform. SectorRank stands in for O'Neil's "leading industry group" only partly, and nothing rigorous supports it. True IBD Relative Strength is not computable from this data. The usual practitioner proxy is the stock's own trailing 6-12 month return. Distance of the pivot from `HighestHigh(252)` is an alternative computable from `df` alone.

## Deterministic detection must date every anchor at confirmation

The detection procedure is where generated code most often goes wrong, so the spec should state it as an ordered algorithm. Every step must use only `df` rows up to and including day T, the last row. The guide's example convention treats `Volume[-1]` as "yesterday", and here the breakout rules must instead read the signal day itself. **State explicitly that the signal day T is the last row of `df` and that Close[T] and Volume[T] are included.** Otherwise the generator may shift everything by a day.

The core look-ahead hazard is well documented. Optuma's vendor staff warn that pivot labels "shift with incoming data" and must be ignored until enough bars have passed after them ([Optuma forum](https://forum.optuma.com/t/optuma-pivot-script-not-working-correctly/3761)). `argrelextrema` swings likewise register only after their order has elapsed ([MarketCalls](https://www.marketcalls.in/?p=63874)). Lo, Mamaysky and Wang avoided the problem by requiring the last extremum to sit d = 3 days before the detection date, with returns measured only after that lag ([LMW 2000](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/teaching/cis700/lo.pdf)). Translated into rules: a 7-bar fractal pivot at bar i is usable only when i + 3 ≤ T. An `argrelextrema(order=n)` pivot is usable only when i + n ≤ T, and it must be computed on the truncated array, because its default `mode='clip'` silently accepts end-of-array extrema that have no right-hand neighbours. **Never apply a centered rolling or kernel smoother to the full history.** It leaks future bars into past values. If smoothing is wanted, refit on data up to T each day and treat extrema near the right edge as unconfirmed, or use a one-sided EMA. LMW also showed that raw 3-bar extrema produce too many patterns that technicians reject ([LMW 2000](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/teaching/cis700/lo.pdf)). Use coarse pivots for the cup anchors and fine ones only inside the handle.

The anchor procedure below adapts the researchers' synthesis of TASC, canslimTechnical and Abiroid ([Traders.com](https://www.traders.com/Documentation/FEEDbk_docs/1999/01/TradersTips/Tips9901.html); [canslimTechnical](https://github.com/kanwalpreet18/canslimTechnical/blob/master/README.md); [MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/771350)). It yields at most one candidate per stock per day.

1. **Right rim C:** C is the bar with the highest High in [T−22, T−5]. That High must also be ≥ every High in [C+1, T−1]. On ties, take the most recent bar. Requiring C ≤ T−5 serves as both the handle minimum and the confirmation lag, so no separate pivot detector is needed here. A coarse pivot of order 10 at C would force a handle of at least 10 days and quietly contradict the 5-day minimum.
2. **Handle low D:** D is the bar with the lowest Low in [C+1, T−1]. D is not a pivot. It is "lowest since C", re-evaluated daily.
3. **Left rim A:** among bars in [C−250, C−30] that are confirmed swing highs with half-width 10 (all lie far enough back to be confirmed), take the most recent bar whose High equals the maximum High over [A, C), so that no higher peak sits inside the cup. Do not apply a separate price tolerance here. The rim-alignment filter decides acceptance, and the cup-length filter applies the 35-bar whole-base minimum (A to T−1).
4. **Cup low B:** argmin of Low over (A, C), with ties going to the later bar.
5. **Prior-uptrend base K:** the lowest Low in the 120 bars before A. If fewer than 60 bars exist before A, reject with reason "unverifiable prior trend" instead of silently passing.
6. Apply the geometry filters, then the breakout filters.

Two points about step 3 need care when drafting. If a price tolerance is added to the search, it must match the rim-alignment filter exactly, or the search will skip candidates the filter would accept. Keeping the tolerance out of the search, as written above, avoids the problem. The tie-break "most recent A that passes" keeps signals stable from one day to the next. The alternative, "highest R²", is equally deterministic but jumps around more.

The 400-day budget sets hard limits. Bulkowski's 65-week maximum (about 325 days) plus a prior-uptrend lookback plus a handle does not fit. With a 22-day handle and a 250-day cup, about 128 bars remain for the 120-bar K lookback, which is just enough. **Cap the cup at 250 bars.** O'Neil's range runs from 7 weeks to an unconfirmed upper bound, so this still covers most of it. The 65-week figure is Bulkowski's, and the notes could not confirm that it is O'Neil's. Long cups early in a stock's history will often fail step 5. That is the intended behaviour.

## Ranking weights reward volume magnitude and handle tightness

The ranking factors only order candidates that already passed, so weaker evidence is acceptable here. The weights below are the report's own proposal, built from the graded findings above. They sum to 100.

| Weight | Factor | Score formula (0-100) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | Breakout volume magnitude | min(100, (VolRatio − 1.4) / (2.5 − 1.4) × 100), where VolRatio = Volume[T] / SMA(Volume,50)[T−1] | rigorous: alpha scales with volume ([OGA](https://www.oneilglobaladvisors.com/documents/FG/oneil/research/617948_OGA_Breakouts-OGA.pdf)) |
| 20% | Handle tightness | 50% × (22 − HandleBars)/17 × 100 + 50% × (0.12 − HandleDepth)/0.12 × 100 | practitioner: short handles better ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)); vendor: shallow retrace better ([Chart Library](https://chartlibrary.io/blog/cup-and-handle-pattern-success-rate)) |
| 15% | Cup quality | 50% × R² scaled from 0.60→0 to 1.0→100 + 50% × (100 if depth in 15-30%, else 50) | proposed / vendor |
| 15% | Market breadth | MarketBreadth (already 0-100) | vendor regime evidence only |
| 15% | Relative strength proxy | Let P = Close[T] / HighestHigh(252). Score = clamp((P − 0.70) / 0.30 × 100, 0, 100) | practitioner proxy for IBD RS |
| 10% | Sector strength | 100 × (1 − (SectorRank − 1) / (NumSectors − 1)) | proposed |

Handle volume dry-up can replace part of the handle-tightness factor if the hard filter turns out too loose. Clamp every sub-score to [0, 100] in the spec text. Useful Output Columns beyond the defaults are Pivot, HandleLow, CupDepth%, CupBars, HandleBars, HandleDepth%, VolRatio, R², % above pivot and the initial stop level. Report HandleLow and Pivot in particular, because the exit needs them.

## Pair a handle-low stop with a throwback-tolerant failure test

The existing horizontal-breakout exit mostly transfers. Cups are long bases, and the 10-day failure window, the 8% backstop, the ATR trail and the 90-day max hold are all of the right order. Several changes are warranted, though, and one mechanical mismatch has to be fixed first. `exit_rules.md` states that entry is at the **next day's open**, while this backtester enters at the **signal day's close**. It also defines Pivot as `Highest_Close(20)`, which is not the cup's pivot. The cup exit should recompute, from signal-day history, Pivot = the highest High in [T−22, T−5] and HandleLow = the lowest Low between that bar and T−1. Those are the same definitions as entry steps 1 and 2, and they are deterministic and recomputable without passing state.

| Exit component | Recommended rule | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial stop | Exit if Close ≤ max(HandleLow × 0.99, Entry × 0.92) | Handle low as stop ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)); O'Neil 7-8% cap ([TraderLion, p.7](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/cup-and-handle-pattern/); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/cup-with-handle-base/)) | practitioner |
| Failed breakout | Days 1-15: Close < HandleLow (covered by the stop). Optional: on day 10 or later, Close < Pivot while the max gain so far is < 5% | Throwback timing ([Bulkowski, Throwbacks](https://ThePatternSite.com/throwbacks.html)) | proposed-by-researcher |
| Big-winner hold | If Close ≥ 1.20 × Entry within 15 sessions, suppress ATR-trail exits until session 40; exit only on the hard stop or a Close below SMA50 | IBD 8-week rule ([MarketSmith India](https://blog.marketsmithindia.com/use-rule-never-miss-big-profits/), snippet) | secondary-only |
| Trail | Keep the 3.0x ATR(14) Chandelier trail (4.0x after the override) | Existing exit; no pattern-specific ATR convention found | proposed-by-researcher |
| Profit-taking | Optional 50% partial at +20-25%; the measured move is a reference level, not a hard target. Bulkowski's rule is `Pivot + 0.61 × (High[C] − Low[B])`: the right-lip-to-valley height scaled by his 61% target-hit rate, not the full cup depth ([Bulkowski, cup page printout, p.2](<C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/Downloads/Cup with Handle.pdf>)) | IBD 20-25% rule (snippet via [Yahoo](https://hk.finance.yahoo.com/m/b4d8c322-9da9-3ad0-85c2-d97f2a8e897a/when-to-take-profits-in.html)); 61% target hit on perfect trades ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html)) | secondary-only / practitioner |
| Max hold | 90 sessions; up to 120 for trades that fired the big-winner rule | Researchers' proposal | proposed-by-researcher |

The failed-breakout change matters most. Bulkowski finds that **62% of cups throw back**. Across 10,305 upward breakouts, a throwback took about **10 days to return to the breakout price**, and **35% then kept falling while 65% recovered**. Trades that held at or above the breakout price rose 40% on average, against 29% for those that dipped below it ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/cup.html); [Bulkowski, Throwbacks](https://ThePatternSite.com/throwbacks.html)). A close below the pivot is therefore informative but not conclusive. The existing `Close < 0.98 × Pivot` test would cut many trades that go on to work, while a close below the handle low marks real structural failure. The tighter-of-two stop works because handle depth is capped at 12%. With a shallow handle the structural stop governs. When a deep handle combines with an entry high in the 5% buy zone, the 8% cap governs. The existing big-winner override already matches IBD's 20%-in-3-weeks trigger. Changing it from "widen ATR to 4.0x" to a 40-session hold floor is closer to the rule's intent, but whether that helps is a backtest question. A partial exit only makes sense if the backtester supports scaling out. If it does not, drop that row rather than approximate it.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the design problem away from finding the right pattern numbers and toward detecting the pattern honestly and judging it on realistic baselines. The geometry numbers are well established as convention, but none of them has been validated by a rigorous cup-specific test. The one rigorous study says the most-cited confirmation, volume, changes payoff size rather than odds. The practical baseline for an automated NSE detector is therefore roughly a coin-flip-plus hit rate, near 57%, with returns driven by the size of the winners, not Bulkowski's 95%. This argues for fixing every threshold in the spec before the first backtest. A small sensitivity grid should only check robustness, not pick the best parameters, following LMW's discipline of never revising their smoothing parameter after seeing results.

The highest-value engineering choices are the ones that keep signals honest. Anchor C at least 5 bars back so that the handle minimum doubles as pivot confirmation. Run all extrema detection on truncated arrays. Pin the signal day to the last row of `df`. Make the exit recompute Pivot and HandleLow from the same definitions the entry used. Because no Indian evidence exists, the first NSE backtest is the first real test of this strategy. The most informative experiments are likely the MarketBreadth gate against a ranking weight, and the 1.4x volume floor against no floor at all, given OGA's flat hit rates.
