# Muhurat bars, not short weeks, distort backtests

A holiday-shortened week on NSE does not behave like a different market. In your own cached Nifty 500 data (Dec 2019 to Dec 2025, 76 shortened weeks against 241 five-session weeks), shortened weeks show **no robust difference in weekly return, volatility, volume, turnover or breakout follow-through**. Published Indian studies mostly agree that the famous US pre-holiday premium is absent or weak on Nifty and Sensex. What does change is narrower and more mechanical. The reopening after a holiday carries a **larger overnight gap**, similar to a normal Monday. The session before a **long weekend** has been unusually strong (+0.63% equal-weight against +0.08% on normal days, n = 32). The derivatives calendar shifts: weekly Nifty expiry moves to the previous trading day, and T+1 settlement stretches to T+2 around settlement holidays. For your backtesting app, the biggest holiday-related problem is not week length. It is the **Muhurat and Saturday special sessions**, one-hour, thin-volume bars that the engine treats as full sessions. Each one depresses 20- and 50-session volume averages for weeks afterwards and can host unrealistic fills. Session-counted rules (MA lengths, MaxHold, 12-1 momentum) are internally consistent across holidays, and the GARP calendar rebalance already handles holidays correctly. The fixes that matter are tagging special sessions, labelling units (sessions against calendar days) honestly, and making overnight-gap costs visible in next-open fills.

Throughout, three kinds of claims are kept apart. **Published evidence** comes from papers, exchange circulars and broker or press sources, labelled by market. **Cached-data results** come from the analysis of `Strategies/Default/data/backtest_cache` (692 symbols, point-in-time Nifty 500 membership, about 488 members per day). Those tests are Welch t-tests with bootstrap confidence intervals, and roughly 60 were run, so a Bonferroni-style bar would be p < 0.001. Almost nothing below clears that bar. **Inference** is reasoning from the two, and is flagged as such.

## About one week in four is shortened, so it is routine, not an edge case

**Cached data.** The cache holds 1,516 trading dates. Over Dec 2019 to Dec 2025 there were **86 weekday non-sessions or Muhurat-only days**, giving **66 four-session weeks and 10 three-session weeks against 241 five-session weeks**, so about 24% of weeks are shortened. Regular weekday sessions run 245 to 250 a year. The cache also contains **8 abbreviated sessions**, detected as days when the median stock traded below 0.45x its trailing 50-row volume: the Diwali Muhurat sessions (14 Nov 2020, 4 Nov 2021, 24 Oct 2022, 12 Nov 2023, 1 Nov 2024, 21 Oct 2025) and the 2024 Saturday special live sessions (2 Mar and 18 May). It also has 3 full-volume weekend sessions, the Budget Saturdays of 1 Feb 2020 and 1 Feb 2025 and Saturday 20 Jan 2024 ([results.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results.txt)). Three-session weeks cluster around Diwali and the March and April holiday run (for example Mar 25 2024, Apr 14 2025, Oct 20 2025). Holiday names were assigned from general knowledge, not checked against an NSE circular, and the repo has no official holiday list to cross-check.

**Published calendar.** A broker compilation (not the NSE circular) lists 15 weekday equity holidays for 2026, including a late-March cluster (26 Mar, 31 Mar, 3 Apr) and two November holiday weeks (Diwali Balipratipada 10 Nov and Guru Nanak Jayanti 24 Nov). Muhurat trading is expected on **Sunday 8 November 2026**, with timings to come by NSE circular ([Sahi](https://sahi.com/blogs/nse-trading-holidays-2026-complete-list-of-stock-market-holidays)). One Angel One page gives a conflicting date, so confirm against the NSE circular.

**Inference.** Any rule that counts sessions meets a shortened week almost every month. Because the engine counts each stock's own rows, a holiday simply stretches calendar time. That is consistent for indicator maths, but it means "5 sessions" spans more than 7 calendar days **23% of the time** in your data ([results_eng.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results_eng.txt)).

## Returns barely move, except the day before a long weekend

### Published evidence: the global pre-holiday effect has faded and India never clearly had it

**[US]** The classic result is large. On 1963 to 1982 data, pre-holiday returns averaged **9 to 14 times** a normal day's, and the roughly 8 pre-holiday days a year earned over a third of the market's return ([Ariel 1990, IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v45y1990i5p1611-26.html)). A 2024 replication finds that after about 1990 the effect survives **only in small firms** and is insignificant for large caps ([Ko & Yang, Critical Finance Review](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0111)). For whole weeks, a practitioner tally shows S&P 500 holiday-shortened weeks averaging **+0.38% against +0.06%** for full weeks since 2000. The gain comes from weeks where Friday is the holiday, not Monday ([Schaeffer's 2023](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/analysis/2023/02/15/buy-these-20-stocks-presidents-day-week)). That source is not peer-reviewed and rests on a few weeks a year. Low returns on the one or two days after US holidays, and gains in the 3 to 4 days before, are documented for 1990 to 2020 and are strongest in small caps ([Dumitriu & Stefanescu, MPRA 100463](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/100463)).

**[India]** The Indian literature conflicts and mostly comes as abstracts without effect sizes. The pre-holiday effect is "completely missing" on BSE indices, while post-holiday effects are "very strong" and strengthened during the financial crisis ([Lu & Patel 2016, IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ebl/ecbull/eb-16-00286.html)). Over 1992 to 2018, **no holiday shows a significant pre-holiday return even at 10%**. The only significant effect is post-New-Year, and Diwali is insignificant on both sides ([Agarwal, Dahiya & Gupta, SRCC](https://www.srcc.edu/node/3960)). A thesis on Nifty, Sensex and the 500 indices for 2002 to 2014 finds no holiday effect in any period ([Inflibnet](https://sgbeta.inflibnet.ac.in/items/1960e11b-8db2-4cda-abc2-0605ba4cf29b)). Against these, a 2025 ARIMAX study across NSE and BSE indices calls the pre-holiday anomaly "significantly persistent" ([Tadepalli, Jain & Metri, FIIB Business Review](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/fbbsrw/v14y2025i2p173-183.html)). A short 2020 to 2022 sample finds pre-holiday returns for Christian and Jain holidays, post-holiday effects for Hindu and Sikh holidays, and nothing for Islamic holidays ([Indian Journal of Finance 2025](https://indianjournalofentrepreneurship.com/index.php/IJF/article/view/174848)). No Indian study of whole four-day against five-day weeks turned up, so your cache is the only evidence on that question.

### Cached data: weeks are a wash, long-weekend eves stand out

**Cached data.** Over the full sample, shortened weeks returned an equal-weight **–0.28 pp less** than five-session weeks, with p = 0.47 and a bootstrap CI of –1.05 to +0.46 pp. Per session, p = 0.84 ([results.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results.txt)). In 2021 to 2025 alone, four-session weeks averaged –0.40% against +0.54% (p = 0.038), but the gap vanishes per session (p = 0.125) and in the full sample. With 60 tests, this is the kind of result you would expect to find by chance. The three-session bucket's +2.46% mean comes from only 10 weeks and is dominated by the April 2020 COVID rebound.

| Week type | Weeks | EW mean return | Weekly std | Std per √session | % stocks up | Median rel. volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 sessions | 241 | +0.47% | 2.80% | 1.25% | 51.3 | 1.00 |
| 4 sessions | 66 | –0.16% | 2.78% | 1.39% | 45.6 | 1.03 |
| 3 sessions | 10 | +2.46% | 3.40% | 1.96% | 66.7 | 1.01 |

Pre-holiday days as a group (n = 76) are no different from normal days: +0.22% against +0.08%, p = 0.31. The exception is the **session before a long weekend** (Thursday before a Friday holiday, or Friday before a Monday holiday). It averaged **+0.63% equal-weight with 58% of stocks up, against +0.08% and 49%** on normal days (t = 3.06, p = 0.004, n = 32). Against ordinary Fridays it beats –0.02% with p = 0.002, and it holds in 2021 to 2025 (+0.52%, p = 0.011, n = 24). The session before a one-day mid-week holiday shows nothing (–0.09%, n = 43). The pre-Diwali session was +0.50% (p = 0.084, n = 11) ([results_eng.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results_eng.txt)).

**Inference.** The long-weekend eve is the one cached result that matches published patterns: the US finding that Friday-off weeks carry the holiday-week gain, and the global finding that residual effects live in broad and small-cap baskets, which is what an equal-weight Nifty 500 measures. It still fails a strict multiple-testing bar, rests on 24 to 32 events, and was measured before costs. Delivery STT is 0.1% on each side ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/?p=391776)), so a one-day delivery round trip costs about 0.2% before impact, roughly a third of the measured edge. Treat it as a hypothesis to test out of sample on 2026 data, not as a strategy.

## Reopening gaps grow with time away, and Muhurat is all gap

### Volatility scales with sessions, not calendar days

**Published evidence [US].** French and Roll found that variance per hour is far lower while exchanges are closed. Weekend close-to-open variance is a fraction of a trading day's, even though the weekend is about eleven times longer, and they used exchange holidays to show that most trading-hour variance comes from information revealed through trading itself ([French & Roll 1986](https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/~kan/3032/pdf/AssetPricingAnomalies/French_Roll_JFE_1986.pdf)). No NSE study splitting post-holiday variance into overnight and intraday parts was found.

**Cached data.** Your data fits this pattern. Weekly equal-weight standard deviation is about **2.8% whether a week has four or five sessions** (Levene p = 0.99). Per √session, shortened weeks are about 10% more volatile, but that is not significant (p = 0.26). Raw cross-sectional dispersion is lower in shortened weeks (p = 0.028 full, p < 0.001 in 2021 to 2025), but this is fully explained by fewer sessions: per √session, p = 0.12 to 0.34. Volume and aggregate turnover show no difference (p = 0.31 and 0.14) ([results.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results.txt)). This dispersion result is the only one clearing p < 0.001, and it is mechanical.

### Post-holiday opens behave like Mondays

**Cached data.** Gap size tracks time away from the market. A normal weekend gap (mean absolute 0.82%) is about 27% larger than a normal overnight gap (0.65%), with p < 0.001. Post-holiday reopenings look like Mondays, not like something larger.

| Reopen type | n | Mean gap | Mean \|gap\| | Open→close | Close→close |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal overnight (Tue–Fri) | 1,140 | +0.25% | 0.65% | –0.11% | +0.13% |
| Normal weekend (Mon) | 275 | +0.18% | 0.82% | –0.28% | –0.11% |
| After 1-day mid-week holiday | 43 | +0.20% | 0.76% | –0.36% | –0.16% |
| After long weekend | 32 | +0.50% | 0.93% | –0.11% | +0.40% |
| After Muhurat or Saturday special | 11 | +0.14% | 0.59% | –0.53% | –0.40% |

The absolute gap after a long weekend exceeds a normal overnight gap (p = 0.026) but not a normal weekend (p = 0.41). The **signed** gap after a long weekend has been positive, +0.50% against +0.18% (p = 0.058 full, p = 0.007 in 2021 to 2025, n = 24). Open-to-close after holidays does not differ from Mondays (p = 0.80), and volume on post-holiday days ran 5.6% above normal Mondays in 2021 to 2025 (p = 0.008). The largest holiday gaps came from stress periods, such as +3.62% on 7 Apr 2020 and +2.05% on 11 Apr 2025 during the tariff shock ([results.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results.txt)). Note that **open-to-close is negative in every category**. That is a general overnight-up, intraday-down pattern in the cache, partly inflated because dividends are not adjusted, and not a holiday effect.

**Inference.** On an India-only holiday, GIFT Nifty and foreign markets keep trading, so the NSE reopen is a catch-up to prices already formed elsewhere. That explains why post-holiday gaps look like weekend gaps rather than French-and-Roll quiet nights. The US-holiday mirror case (India open, US shut, as on Thanksgiving) should produce a smaller opening gap with no overnight US cue. That is a hypothesis: pre-market notes on Thanksgiving still quote GIFT Nifty as the opening guide ([DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/top-things-you-must-know-before-the-market-opens-id008-44476)), but nothing measures it.

### Muhurat: a symbolic gap, then a fade

**Published evidence.** Press and broker tallies put the Muhurat session's average move at about +0.5%, with 8 of 10 sessions positive in 2015 to 2024 ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/muhurat-trading-over-the-last-decade)). An unverified forum table claims Nifty gapped up in 13 of 14 sessions over 2008 to 2021 and closed below its open in 13 of 14 ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/nifty-always-forms-a-daily-red-candle-from-last-14-years-on-muhurat-session/138281)). Muhurat volume is lower and retail-dominated ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/the-significance-and-trends-of-muhurat-trading-at-bse-nse-on-diwali-news-232208)), and the session has no reliable six-month predictive power ([5paisa](https://5paisa.com/blog/can-stock-markets-sustain-diwali-rallies-heres-what-history-indicates)).

**Cached data.** All 8 abbreviated sessions closed up on an equal-weight basis, **+0.53% to +1.09% close-to-close, nearly all of it in the opening gap**. Open-to-close ranged from –0.18% to +0.32%, median relative volume was **0.07 to 0.23x**, and the next regular session ranged from –1.57% to +1.00% ([results_eng.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results_eng.txt)). With n = 8, this confirms the published picture of a token gap-up on token volume and nothing more.

## Derivatives and settlement shift by calendar rule, not by market mood

**Published evidence.** Since SEBI's October 2024 framework, each exchange may list only one weekly benchmark option, and the minimum index contract size rose to ₹15 lakh, with an extra 2% extreme-loss margin on short options on expiry day ([Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/updates/sebis-new-rules-on-weekly-index-derivatives-expiry)). A May 2025 SEBI circular confined all equity derivative expiries to Tuesday or Thursday ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-limits-equity-derivatives-expiry-to-tuesdays-and-thursdays-125052601285_1.html)). From September 2025, **NSE's Nifty weekly expires every Tuesday and its monthlies on the last Tuesday, while BSE uses Thursday**. When expiry falls on a holiday, the contract expires on the **previous trading day** ([AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/all-index-expiry-day/)). That blog's 2026 calendar moves Nifty weekly expiry to Monday 9 Nov (Diwali Balipratipada), and both the weekly and **all NSE monthlies** to Monday 23 Nov (Guru Nanak Jayanti). Verify these against NSE circulars. Theta is quoted in calendar days, so a holiday adds a full day of quoted decay ([Rupeezy](https://support.rupeezy.in/support/solutions/articles/21000005304-what-is-time-decay-theta)), but market makers price that decay into the last session before the closure by letting implied volatility fall ([Moontower Meta](https://moontowermeta.com/weekend-theta/)).

On settlement, NSE excludes bank holidays, exchange holidays and weekends when counting settlement days ([NSE](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-market-settlement-cycle)). A **settlement holiday** is a day when trading runs but clearing, depositories and banks do not. On the 26 Aug 2026 Id-E-Milad settlement holiday, for example, trades of 25 Aug settled on 27 Aug, and shares bought on 25 Aug could not be sold for delivery until credited ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/settlement-holiday-on-26-august-2026)). NSE Clearing bunches the missed settlements into the next business day ([NSE circular CMPT63955](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMPT63955.pdf)).

**Inference.** None of this touches your daily-bar cash backtests directly, since the engine fills at prices and ignores settlement. It matters in two places. First, for live execution of the screeners, a settlement holiday turns T+1 into T+2, so a rule that buys and then wants to exit the next session cannot deliver-sell the shares. Second, if the app ever adds F&O, an expiry calendar with the previous-trading-day rule, and implied volatility measured in trading days rather than calendar days, become mandatory. Without them, a shortened expiry week looks artificially cheap in calendar-day implied volatility.

## The engine is consistent, but special sessions and unit labels leak

**Cached data and code review.** `run_backtest` builds its calendar from the union of all symbols' dates and evaluates a stock only on days it has a row. Entries fill at the next row's open, and intraday trades square off at that row's close ([backtest.py](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Default/backtest.py)). Every lookback, MaxHold and time stop counts rows. No code resamples to weekly bars or references weekdays. Four concrete issues follow.

**Muhurat and special sessions are treated as full bars.** A Muhurat bar inside a trailing 20-row volume average **lowers that average by about 4.4%** for the next 20 sessions. That alone adds **3.8% more volume-qualified breakouts** (129 extra signals out of 3,492) ([mech.py](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/mech.py)). This touches Horizontal Breakout v9's `Volume >= 2.5 * MA(Volume, 50)` and the 20-session volume and turnover floors in Trend Continuation and Trendline Bounce ([horizontal breakout v9 rules](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Rules_backup/horizontal_breakout_momentum_o_neil_volume_rules_20261022_095002.md); [trend continuation rules](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Rules_backup/trend_continuation_pullback_mean_reversion_strategy_rules_20261022_094318.md)). On the Muhurat day itself, `Close > Close[-1]` and new-high checks pass easily on a near-certain gap-up. A signal on the pre-Diwali session enters at the Muhurat open, and the Momentum Loser intraday short would both enter and square off inside a one-hour auction-driven session, which is not a realistic fill. Each such bar also counts as a session towards MaxHold and the 20-row near-miss horizon.

**Week-to-session conversions assume five sessions per week.** The cache averages about 4.75 to 4.8 sessions per week. Cup and Handle's "7 weeks" became 35 sessions, which spans 46 to 59 calendar days (median 51, about 7.3 weeks). O'Neil's "20% in 3 weeks, then hold 8 weeks" became 15 and 40 sessions, and 40 rows is about 8.3 calendar weeks. Trendline Bounce's 25 sessions is described as "roughly 5 calendar weeks" ([cup and handle rules](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Rules_backup/cup_and_handle_breakout_v1_rules_20261006_133828.md); [horizontal breakout exit](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/ExitRules_backup/horizontal_breakout_exit_strategy_v1_exit_rules_20261006_144834.md); [results_eng.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results_eng.txt)). The drift is small and harmless for pattern geometry. But O'Neil's rules are calendar-based in the original, and session counts quietly lengthen them in the holiday-heavy March to April and October to November windows.

**GARP's rebalance is correct, but its state labels mix units.** The entry rule `P < T <= D` fires on the first row on or after 15 Jun or 15 Dec. In the cache, Saturday 15 Jun 2024 correctly mapped to Tuesday 18 Jun, because Monday 17 Jun was a holiday. There are 122 to 129 sessions between rebalances, so the **150-session MaxHold safety net is never reached** ([generated_rules.py](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Default/generated_rules.py); [results_eng.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results_eng.txt)). In `describe_state`, however, `DaysHeld` counts rows, while `DaysToMaxHold` counts calendar days to the next rebalance and has nothing to do with the 150-session MaxHold ([generated_exit_strategy.py](file:///D:/git-repos/FrontierTradelight/Strategies/Default/generated_exit_strategy.py)). A stock with missing rows enters on its first available row after the rebalance date: a late entry, not a missed one.

**Calendar-day warm-ups convert to thin row margins.** `WARMUP_DAYS = 400` yields 269 to 276 rows, against the 253 rows needed for 12-1 momentum. That leaves only about 16 to 23 rows of slack, so a stock with more than about 16 missing rows in its first year gets no momentum rank early in a backtest. In the cache, 1.3% of member stock-days are missing ([results_eng.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results_eng.txt)).

**Breakouts do not care about holidays, but they do pay the gap.** Using a simplified breakout (close above the prior 20-session high on more than 1.5x average volume), shortened weeks produce slightly fewer signals (3.17% against 3.50% of stock-days, not significant) and **no worse follow-through**: excess five-session return differs by –0.09 pp, CI –0.42 to +0.25 pp. A holiday inside the holding window does not hurt either (–0.38% against –0.55% excess). Across every group, though, these breakouts **underperform the equal-weight universe by about 0.5% over 5 and 10 sessions** after a next-open entry ([results_brk.txt](file:///C:/Users/apoorv.bedmutha.MEDIASSISTINDIA/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/D--git-repos-FrontierTradelight/3a82b646-45e4-46f0-a082-e044ceba7faa/scratchpad/results_brk.txt)). That definition is far simpler than Horizontal Breakout v9 and ignores costs. **Inference:** the underperformance fits the gap-up, intraday-fade pattern above and is a property of the next-open fill convention, not of holidays.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that holidays matter to this app as a **data-quality and fill-realism problem, not an alpha source**. Neither the Indian literature nor six years of your own Nifty 500 data supports trading the week's length, and the one promising calendar pattern, the long-weekend eve, is a small-sample, pre-cost result that should earn its place out of sample before it becomes a rule. The real leakage sits in a handful of one-hour bars that quietly reshape volume averages for weeks afterwards, and in the habit of writing "weeks" while the engine counts rows. Fixing those makes every strategy's backtest slightly more honest at once.

The largest open question is not about holidays at all. The cache shows a persistent overnight-up, intraday-down split and a roughly 0.5% excess-return penalty on next-open breakout entries. If that survives dividend adjustment and the real v9 filters, it is worth more attention than any calendar effect. It argues for testing alternative fill conventions, such as entering at the signal-day close or using limit entries, ahead of further holiday research.
