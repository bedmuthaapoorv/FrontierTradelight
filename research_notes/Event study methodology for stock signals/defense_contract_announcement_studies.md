# Stock Price Reactions to Government/Defense Contract-Award and Order-Win Announcements

## Is there published research quantifying the average abnormal return and its persistence/decay after a government contract award is publicly announced? What magnitude and over what window?

### Takeaway
Yes — there is a modest but real academic literature, concentrated in the US, showing a statistically significant but economically small positive abnormal return to contract-winning firms on the announcement day (roughly 0.5%–1.4% for corporate-government contracts, with most of the reaction concentrated in a 1–2 day window), while the contract-granting party (the government/customer) shows no significant reaction. A newer, large-sample proprietary study of the defense sector specifically finds a much smaller (single-digit basis points) *broad* average reaction, with the effect concentrating heavily in subsets defined by relative contract size, branch of service, and contract duration.

### Cited Findings
- Elayan, Pukthuanthong & Roll (UCLA Anderson, "The Valuation Effect and Determinants of Corporate Contracting," working paper, sample 1990–2000), studying 1,963 corporate-government contract announcements (largely US federal/local government, mostly military) find a statistically significant average abnormal return (AAR) for contractors of **0.54% on the day before/day of announcement (t=-1)**, versus **1.13%–1.43% for purely inter-corporate contracts** — i.e., government contract winners gain *less* than winners of private-sector contracts, on average. — [Source](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf)
- In the same study, the abnormal return is concentrated almost entirely in the two-day window t=-1 to t=0 (CAAR = 1.94% for the sub-sample of inter-corporate contracting-party pairs, highly significant); returns in the days after (t=+1 to +5) are statistically indistinguishable from zero, and the contract-granting firm (contractee/customer) shows **no significant abnormal return at any point**, including on its own first-ever contract award — suggesting reactions are essentially resolved within the announcement window with no discernible post-announcement drift in this data. — [Source](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf)
- Cross-sectionally, this study finds announcement returns are *higher* for: longer contract terms, larger contract size relative to firm assets, smaller contractor firm size, low pre-contract growth (Tobin's q), higher historical profitability, more industry competitors, and higher pre-contract stock return volatility (consistent with a "winner's curse"/quasi-rent story and a cost-efficiency signaling story). — [Source](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf)
- A 2026 practitioner/academic white paper (TenderAlpha × University of Hong Kong), using a large, timestamped, minute-precision global defense-contract-announcement dataset mapped to ~183 US-listed defense equities, 2010–2025 (16,529 unique contracts), finds the **overall average post-announcement reaction is very small**: 3-day value-weighted return of **+0.6 bps (not significant, t=0.18)** and equal-weighted **+4 bps (t=1.2)**; 5-day value-weighted **+3.4 bps (t=0.76)** and equal-weighted **+7.5 bps (t=1.75)** — i.e., the broad/average effect across all defense contract announcements is economically negligible and not reliably distinguishable from zero. — [Source](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)
- The same white paper finds the reaction is strongly concentrated in specific subsets: contracts in the **top 5% by size relative to the firm's market cap** show a 3-day VW return of **+30 bps (t=2.35, significant)** and 5-day **+23.1 bps (t=1.43)**; contracts in the **top 5% relative to total assets** show 3-day **+28.1 bps (t=2.24, significant)**. Absolute dollar contract size alone shows only a weak, mostly insignificant monotonic pattern — economic materiality relative to firm size matters much more than headline contract value. — [Source](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)
- Elayan et al. also report average abnormal returns are larger for smaller firms, and that the market appears to price the news efficiently and quickly: no meaningful cumulative drift is detected beyond the 2-day announcement window in the base full sample. — [Source](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf)
- A separate pharmaceutical-sector event study (327 event-days, 12 NASDAQ-listed pharma companies, 2010–2021, US federal contract awards) using a 20-day window (±10 days) found **cumulative abnormal returns were statistically significant**, but **individual daily average abnormal returns within the event window were positive but not statistically significant** — the authors concluded "winning a government contract has a limited impact on the stock market" in that sector, and suggested the effect may show up cumulatively/with a lag rather than as a sharp one-day jump. — [Source](https://www.anserpress.org/journal/jea/3/1/43)

### Inferences
- The two rigorous US studies that quantify magnitude (Elayan et al.'s 1990–2000 sample and the TenderAlpha/HKU 2010–2025 sample) point to a genuinely modest average effect for corporate-government/defense contract awards — well under 1% on average, and in the TenderAlpha sample, often statistically indistinguishable from zero when pooled across all announcements. The effect is real but small, and highly conditional on contract characteristics (see next question).
- The pharma-sector finding of significant *cumulative* but insignificant *daily* abnormal returns hints that for some announcement types the market's absorption of the news may not be a single sharp jump but a more gradual repricing — this is suggestive of a "small daily drift adding up," though the authors did not frame it explicitly as PEAD-style drift, and this should be treated as a weak, single-sector signal rather than a general finding.

### Gaps
- No study found directly measures decay/half-life of abnormal returns beyond a ~5-10 day window for defense-specific contracts (i.e., no month-long or quarter-long post-announcement drift study specific to defense contract awards was found).
- No article-level effect size was found comparable across the various studies with a common event-window definition, since studies use different windows (2-day, 3-day, 5-day, 20-day) and different weighting schemes (equal- vs. value-weighted), which limits precise magnitude comparison.

## Do these studies find a difference in market reaction between the initial "award" announcement vs. subsequent contract-execution milestones (e.g. groundbreaking/keel-laying/first-delivery)?

### Takeaway
The evidence base on this specific distinction is thin, but what exists points the opposite direction from a naive assumption: the one study that explicitly separates "awards" (brand-new contracts) from "modifications" (which are closer in spirit to execution-stage announcements, e.g., scope/budget changes on existing contracts) finds modifications produce a *stronger*, more statistically reliable post-announcement drift than the initial award announcement itself. No study was found that specifically studies shipbuilding milestones (keel-laying, steel-cutting, commissioning) as a distinct announcement-type category with its own abnormal-return estimate.

### Cited Findings
- The TenderAlpha/HKU white paper explicitly separates "Awarded" (brand-new contracts) from "Modification" (changes/expansions to existing contracts) and finds: 3-day VW return for Awarded = **-0.3 bps (t=-0.07, insignificant)** vs. Modification = **+3.8 bps (t=0.76)**; 5-day EW return for Awarded = **+3.2 bps (t=0.67, insignificant)** vs. Modification = **+12.8 bps (t=2.07, statistically significant)**. The paper's explicit conclusion: "modifications show stronger post-announcement drift than new awards, particularly in equal-weighted returns." — [Source](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)
- Elayan et al. test an "Initial vs. Subsequent Contracts" variable (whether the announcement is the firm's first contract win in the sample period) as a cross-sectional predictor of abnormal returns, and find it **statistically insignificant** for both contractors and contractees — i.e., being a "debut"/first announcement vs. a later, presumably more expected announcement did not measurably change the size of the reaction in their sample. This is described by the authors as "somewhat unexpected." — [Source](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf)
- No study located specifically analyzes shipbuilding lifecycle milestones (steel-cutting, keel-laying, launching, sea trials, commissioning) as separate announcement-type events with their own return statistics; searches for "shipbuilding order announcement stock reaction event study" returned only general event-study methodology papers and non-academic market-news coverage of real-world stock moves (e.g., a 20% single-day surge in Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers (GRSE) shares tied to a record FY26 revenue announcement, which is a financial-results reaction, not a contract-milestone reaction). — [Source](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/defence-shipbuilding-stocks-surge-cmnflip6r5iyho00jo59gwdg0)

### Inferences
- Taken together, the "modification vs. award" finding and the "initial vs. subsequent contract" insignificance finding both suggest that being the *first* or *initial* announcement in a contract's lifecycle is not obviously the announcement type carrying the largest market-moving information content — later-stage announcements (modifications, and by extension, plausibly execution milestones) may carry incremental new information (e.g., confirmation of scope, schedule progress, or scaled-up committed value) that the market has not yet fully priced in from the original award.
- This is only an inference by analogy, however — "modification" (scope/budget changes to an existing contract) is not the same event type as a physical milestone (keel-laying, commissioning) tracked in the user's Event Watcher, so this should not be read as direct confirmation that shipbuilding milestones generate comparable or larger abnormal returns than the initial award.

### Gaps
- No dedicated empirical study of physical/ceremonial milestone announcements (keel-laying, steel-cutting, launch, commissioning, first flight, first delivery) as a distinct event-study category was found in the defense, aerospace, or shipbuilding literature. This is a genuine evidence gap, not just a search-failure — the literature search terms used ("shipbuilding order announcement stock reaction event study," "keel-laying stock price," analogous aerospace-delivery-milestone phrasing) surfaced no academic hits, only real-time market commentary.
- No study was found that measures whether the market treats a "certainty-resolution" milestone (e.g., commissioning, meaning the ship is now complete and operational) differently from a "scope-expansion" milestone (e.g., contract modification adding budget) — these plausibly carry different information content but neither was isolated in the literature reviewed.

## Is there research on Indian defense-sector stocks specifically (e.g. around Ministry of Defence/PIB announcements), or is the evidence base mostly US/European?

### Takeaway
The evidence base is overwhelmingly US-centric (essentially all rigorous event-study/academic literature identified studies US corporate-government or US military contracts); no peer-reviewed or credible academic event study specific to Indian defense PSU/private shipbuilder stock reactions to Ministry of Defence (MoD) or Press Information Bureau (PIB) announcements was found. What exists for India is real-time market/news commentary describing large observed stock moves around MoD/DAC (Defence Acquisition Council) approvals, not rigorous abnormal-return studies with statistical testing.

### Cited Findings
- The Elayan et al. sample (1990–2000) is drawn from Dow Jones/CRSP US data, and the authors explicitly note that "a large majority of government contracts are awarded to American firms contracting with the U.S. federal and local governments." — [Source](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf)
- The TenderAlpha/HKU white paper is global in contract-announcement coverage but restricted to *US-listed equities* for the return analysis; it separately reports returns for "US vs. Non-US contracting entities" (i.e., US company reacting to a non-US government's contract), finding non-US-government contract announcements to US-listed firms actually show *negative* mean returns in the 3-day window (VW: -6.8 bps, t=-1.3; EW: -6.9 bps, t=-1.35) versus near-zero for US-government contracts — but this is not a study of Indian-listed stocks reacting to Indian government announcements, it is US-listed stocks reacting to any government's contract. — [Source](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)
- Real-time market reporting (not an academic event study) describes Indian defense PSU shares rallying up to 3% after the Defence Acquisition Council approved ₹52,000 crore of capital acquisition proposals, with the Nifty India Defence index rising 1.70% on the news — illustrating that Indian markets do visibly react to MoD-level procurement approvals, but with no formal abnormal-return/statistical-significance testing behind the reported figure. — [Source](https://www.thehawk.in/news/science/defence-psu-stocks-rally-up-to-3-pc-after-rs-52000-crore-procurement-nod)
- Search for academic or NSE/BSE-affiliated research specifically studying Indian defense-sector stock reactions to PIB releases returned no matching academic papers; the closest hits were (a) a general event-study methodology paper on Indian PSU disinvestment announcements (a different event type — privatization, not contract awards), and (b) an arXiv paper on Indian stock market reactions to Union Budget announcements (a different, macro-level event, not company-specific contract awards). — [Source](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/emeeco/v6y2014i2p181-202.html); [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15787)

### Inferences
- The complete absence of Indian-specific academic event-study literature on defense contract/PIB announcements, combined with clear real-world evidence that Indian defense stocks do move meaningfully (multi-percent single-day moves) around MoD/DAC announcements, suggests this is an under-researched area — practitioners and retail-facing commentary track it, but no rigorous, peer-reviewed quantification of average magnitude, significance, or decay exists that we could locate.
- Given the near-total absence of Indian evidence, any expectations about magnitude/persistence for Indian PIB-driven announcements must be extrapolated from US evidence with substantial uncertainty, and adjusted for known Indian-market differences not addressed in any study reviewed (e.g., generally lower institutional coverage of small/mid-cap defense PSUs, different disclosure/leak dynamics around PIB releases vs. US press releases, and different market depth/liquidity).

### Gaps
- No Indian academic finance journal article, SSRN paper, or NSE/BSE-affiliated research note specifically analyzing event-study abnormal returns around MoD/PIB defense contract or shipbuilding milestone announcements was found. This appears to be a genuine, not merely search-limited, gap in the literature as of this research.
- No comparative study contrasting the magnitude/speed of Indian market reactions to defense announcements against the well-documented US baseline was found.

## What do these studies conclude about optimal trade timing — do they find the market reacts efficiently within a day or two, or is there documented underreaction/drift specific to this announcement type?

### Takeaway
The dominant finding across the US-focused literature is fast, largely efficient incorporation of contract-award news within a 1–2 day window, with the abnormal return concentrated on the announcement day and the day before (suggesting some information leakage/anticipation), and little-to-no reliable drift thereafter in the base case. However, the TenderAlpha/HKU study found statistically significant continued drift specifically in three conditional subsets — contract modifications, Army-awarded contracts, and short-duration (≤1 year) contracts — where 5-day returns were larger and more significant than 3-day returns, implying underreaction/drift is present but concentrated, not universal.

### Cited Findings
- Elayan et al. find the largest average abnormal return occurs on day t=-1 (the day before the public announcement), which they attribute to the announcement often actually occurring a day earlier than reported in the media (information leakage/reporting lag), with returns on days t=+1 through t=+5 not showing a persistent, systematic pattern in the base full samples. — [Source](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf)
- The TenderAlpha/HKU paper directly compares 3-day vs. 5-day windows across subsets and finds that for short-duration (≤1 year) contracts, the 5-day equal-weighted return (**+28.6 bps, t=2.84**) is nearly triple the 3-day return (**+11.5 bps, t=1.44**) — indicating continued positive drift over days 4-5, not a one-day, fully-efficient repricing, for this specific subset. Similarly, for contract-size-relative-to-assets in the top 5%, the 5-day return (+29 bps, t=1.84) remains elevated versus 3-day (+28.1 bps, t=2.24), roughly persisting rather than decaying. For "modification" announcements specifically, 5-day EW return (+12.8 bps, t=2.07) is both larger and more significant than the 3-day EW return (+8.2 bps, t=1.68), reinforcing a drift pattern for that announcement type. — [Source](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)
- Conversely, for the pooled "All announcements" baseline in the same paper, the 3-day and 5-day reactions are both small and statistically insignificant (3-day EW +4bps t=1.2; 5-day EW +7.5bps t=1.75) — suggesting that for a generic, unconditioned defense contract announcement, there is no strong evidence of exploitable drift; the drift is present mainly within specific, identifiable sub-categories (large-relative-size, Army, short-duration, modifications). — [Source](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)
- The pharmaceutical-sector paper's finding — significant cumulative abnormal returns but insignificant single-day abnormal returns within a 20-day window — is also consistent with a "slow diffusion of information" pattern rather than a single-day efficient jump, though this is a different sector (pharma federal contracts) and a small sample (327 event-days). — [Source](https://www.anserpress.org/journal/jea/3/1/43)
- General event-study/post-earnings-announcement-drift (PEAD) literature (not specific to contract announcements) documents underreaction as a broad, well-established market anomaly when trading frictions (transaction costs, short-sale constraints) limit aggressive same-day trading on news, providing a generic theoretical mechanism that could plausibly extend to contract-announcement drift, though this is generic event-study theory, not a contract-specific finding, and is offered here only as context per the user's own framing of "generic event-study theory" as a lower-confidence category. — [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214635020303750)

### Inferences
- The practical implication for a signal that spans "the initial award announcement" through later "contract-execution milestones": the US evidence suggests capturing the announcement-day/next-day reaction is important since much of the average effect is front-loaded and resolves within 1-2 days for a generic announcement, arguing against very late entry for the *initial award* in the base case.
- However, the same evidence suggests that a multi-day holding window (3-5 days) is empirically justified — not as a universal effect, but specifically for economically material contracts (large relative to firm size/assets), Army-type procurement, short-duration contracts, and contract modifications — where statistically significant continued drift beyond day 1 was documented. Since shipbuilding/defense PSU announcements in India are often large relative to the (frequently mid-cap) contractor's market cap, the "large-relative-size" and "short-duration" drift findings are the most transferable analogs to the Indian shipbuilding/defense-PSU use case, even though they come from a different market.
- Because no Indian-specific evidence exists, and the shipbuilding-milestone-specific evidence gap is total, any conclusion about optimal Indian PIB-announcement trade timing must be treated as an extrapolation with meaningful uncertainty, not a validated finding.

### Gaps
- No formal statistical test of drift specifically for the "initial award vs. execution milestone" comparison the user cares about was found (see prior section's gap).
- No study using Indian trading-day return data to test 1-day vs. multi-day holding windows around MoD/PIB defense announcements was found — this would be the most directly applicable study and does not appear to exist yet.
- The TenderAlpha/HKU paper acknowledges its own t-statistics are "approximate" because of overlapping 3-day/5-day event windows across adjacent entry dates, and results are gross of transaction costs, which the paper itself flags as a caveat on how confidently the drift subsets can be traded on in practice.
