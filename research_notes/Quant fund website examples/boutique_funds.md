# Boutique / Small / Emerging-Manager Quant Fund Websites

## What small or boutique systematic/quant trading funds, prop-turned-fund managers, or emerging CTAs have public websites worth studying?

### Takeaway
A workable set of real, currently-live examples spans the size spectrum from a $5M-AUM pre-launch shop to ~$1B boutique CTAs — useful as graduated templates. Most are plain, text-heavy, compliance-driven sites rather than flashy consumer-style marketing pages; the notable exception is CARL, which markets systematic strategies directly to retail/accredited individuals with app-style UX.

### Cited Findings
- **Episteme Capital Partners** (epistemecap.com) — systematic global macro CTA founded 2009, offices in London and West Palm Beach, flagship program "Episteme Systematic Quest" (futures + FX forwards) plus a "Global Liquidity Opportunities" short-term fixed income program. — [About](https://www.epistemecap.com/about/), [Investment Programs](https://www.epistemecap.com/investment-programs/)
- Episteme's site requires accepting a legal/regulatory disclaimer banner before the visitor can see marketing content — a friction point common to funds marketing non-public offerings. — [About](https://www.epistemecap.com/about/)
- **Systematic Strategies** (systematic-strategies.com) — founded 2009 by Dr. Jonathan Kinlay, NY-based, markets itself on "over fifteen years of proven performance" across equity, fixed income, currency and commodity futures, serving HNW/family office/institutional clients via managed accounts and a hedge fund vehicle. — [Site](https://systematic-strategies.com/)
- Systematic Strategies' site has Home, Investment Strategies, Research & Development, Management, Careers, and an externally-hosted Blog, but **no dedicated performance page, no team bios/headshots beyond the founder, no investor login, and non-functional social-media icons** — a genuinely bare-bones, dated small-shop site despite 15+ years in business. — [Site](https://systematic-strategies.com/)
- **Drury Capital** (drurycapital.com) — CTA founded 1993 by Bernard Drury, flagship "Drury Diversified Trend-Following Program" trading since 1997, plus a "Drury Multi-Strategy Program." Site nav: Firm (Overview, Team, Awards, Careers), Our Approach, Investment Products, News, Contact Us. — [Investment Products](https://www.drurycapital.com/investment-products/)
- Drury's site leads with risk disclaimers ("significant risk of loss in futures trading... past performance is not indicative of future results") ahead of specific return figures, and has a "Meet the Team" page and a footer "Need Assistance?" CTA block. — [Investment Products](https://www.drurycapital.com/investment-products/)
- **Quantica Capital AG** (quantica-capital.com) — Swiss (Schaffhausen), FINMA-regulated, founded 2005 (incorporated 2003), systematic medium-term trend-following; now ~$1B AUM, 13 professionals, 2 offices, 21-year track record, trades 100+ markets. Sections: Home, About Us, Approach, How to Invest, Publications, News, ESG, Careers, Contact, and a separate **Investor Area login**. — [Site](https://quantica-capital.com/)
- Quantica's design is notably more polished than the other boutiques found: illustrative hero artwork, light/dark logo variants, publication cards with imagery, clean modern typography — closer to an institutional/mid-size-manager aesthetic than a true micro-fund, useful as an "aspirational" template for a fund that has scaled past the earliest stage. — [Site](https://quantica-capital.com/)
- **Crabel Capital Management** (crabel.com) — LA-based, founded by short-term trading pioneer Toby Crabel, 30+ years systematic futures/FX trading; has dedicated `/programs/` and `/who-we-are/` pages, suggesting a similar Home / Programs / Team / Contact structure to the others. — [Site](https://crabel.com/), [Programs](https://crabel.com/programs/), [Who We Are](https://crabel.com/who-we-are/)
- **CARL** (investwithcarl.com) — markets systematic/quant hedge-fund-style strategies ("15%+ targeted returns," monthly liquidity) directly to individual, institutional, and IRA investors; site has Investor Types pages, "How CARL Works" (strategy/methodology), Investments, Fund Services (capital raising, verification, administration, marketing, custom development), a Learning Center with podcast, and an "About CARL" page with a "Talk to CARL" contact option and a "CARL Club" community. — [How CARL Works](https://investwithcarl.com/how-carl-works/systematic-trading)
- CARL is mobile-first with prominent iOS/Android app-download CTAs, a three-step onboarding flow (account setup → investment analysis → funding), a "Get Started" primary CTA, and gates its downloadable performance report behind accredited-investor status. — [How CARL Works](https://investwithcarl.com/how-carl-works/systematic-trading)
- **MPP&E Capital** — newly established (2023) quant hedge fund, London, 8-person team (5 with advanced math/physics degrees), launching with SMAs in 2024 ahead of a planned institutional commingled fund in Q1 2025; systematic strategies across US equities, commodities, macro futures, and volatility. No AUM disclosed; actively courting multi-managers, bank fund-of-funds, and family offices. — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/new-quant-hedge-fund-mppe-capital-preps-for-launch/)
- **Astant Global Management** — London-based macro/AI-driven manager preparing to launch a new quant strategy; currently manages only ~$5.44M (mostly for one Moroccan agro-industrial backer), targeting ~$38M AUM ahead of the new fund's launch — a genuinely micro-scale, pre-institutional example. — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/astant-to-launch-ai-powered-quant-hedge-fund/)
- Other named small/independent systematic shops surfaced in research but not directly verified via their own sites in this pass: **Qbasis** (co-founded by Philipp Polzl, in-market ~95% of the time, short-bias-capable trend variant) and **Dunn Capital Management** (still trading a trend-following system largely unchanged since the 1980s). — [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/the-life-outside-of-trend/)

### Inferences
- There's a clear size/polish gradient: sub-$50M pre-launch shops (Astant, MPP&E) have essentially no public marketing site yet (press coverage substitutes for a website); ~$0-ish-to-low-hundred-million solo/small-team CTAs (Episteme, Systematic Strategies) have plain, static, compliance-first brochure sites with minimal design investment; firms in the few-hundred-million-to-$1B range (Quantica, Drury, Crabel) invest in a cleaner but still conservative institutional design with a real Team page and sometimes an investor login; CARL is the outlier, applying consumer-fintech UX to a systematic-strategies offering aimed at a broader (including non-institutional) investor base.
- A near-universal skeleton across the boutique/small end is: Home → Strategy/Approach → Team/Firm → (sometimes) Track Record/Performance (often gated or absent) → News/Research → Careers → Contact, with an optional password-protected Investor Area/Login for existing LPs. A newly-launched or solo fund emulating these should expect to omit or gate the Performance section early on, consistent with what was observed.
- Regulatory caution visibly shapes design choices at every size: disclaimer walls before content (Episteme), risk-disclaimer-first copy ahead of return figures (Drury), and gating performance reports behind accredited-investor verification (CARL) are all different implementations of the same compliance instinct, and a new fund's site should budget for this friction rather than treat it as a design error.

### Gaps
- Could not access live, current team-bio and performance-page content in detail (most WebFetches returned only partial/cached summaries of long pages) — a follow-up should browse each site directly (not via summarizing fetch) to capture literal bio text, headshots, and any specific performance-table formatting.
- Dunn Capital Management's and Qbasis's own websites were not directly fetched/verified in this pass — only secondary reporting was found; their actual site structure is a gap.
- Did not find a true "under $50M, currently operating with a public marketing site" example with a visible track record — the smallest examples found (Astant, MPP&E) are pre-launch/pre-track-record, while funds with visible track records (Episteme, Systematic Strategies, Drury) are likely larger than $50M even though exact AUM isn't disclosed on-site. This is itself a finding (see below).
- No BarclayHedge/Preqin/NFA CTA-registry browsing was performed (would require authenticated or paid access); the NFA public CTA list (nfa.futures.org) was identified as a lead but not crawled for additional candidate names.

## How do these smaller funds balance looking credible and professional with a much smaller marketing budget/team?

### Takeaway
Most boutique/small quant managers substitute design spend with (a) heavy use of credentials, tenure, and track-record-length claims in copy, (b) conservative, template-like layouts rather than custom illustration or animation, and (c) compliance-forward framing (disclaimers, risk language) that doubles as a credibility signal to institutional allocators; a minority (e.g., CARL) instead spend on consumer-grade UX to win trust from individual investors.

### Cited Findings
- Systematic Strategies substitutes a sparse, dated design with repeated emphasis on "over fifteen years of proven performance" and founder credentials (Dr. Jonathan Kinlay) in place of visual polish, team bios, or a performance page. — [Site](https://systematic-strategies.com/)
- Drury Capital leads product pages with regulatory risk disclaimers before any performance claim, a pattern that signals institutional-grade compliance awareness even on a modestly-designed site. — [Investment Products](https://www.drurycapital.com/investment-products/)
- Quantica Capital, now at ~$1B AUM, invests in more custom illustration, publication/thought-leadership content, and an ESG section — signaling that design/content investment scales with AUM and institutional investor expectations. — [Site](https://quantica-capital.com/)
- Episteme's site gates meaningful content behind a disclaimer click-through, prioritizing regulatory caution over smooth UX — a trade-off smaller managers accept rather than invest in a more sophisticated gated-content system. — [About](https://www.epistemecap.com/about/)
- Agencies specializing in this space (see next section) explicitly pitch "cost-effective" or "custom, professional" sites as their value proposition for funds that can't afford large in-house marketing teams — e.g. Emerson Ward offers everything from "simple, inexpensive brochureware sites" up to complex secure investor portals, scaled to budget. — [Emerson Ward](https://www.em-ward.com/websites)

### Inferences
- The dominant credibility strategy at the small end is substituting "years in business" / founder pedigree / regulatory rigor for visual design spend, since compliance (NFA/SEC-aware copy, risk disclaimers, accredited-investor gating) is a credibility signal institutional allocators actually weight, whereas slick design is not a prerequisite for an allocator doing real diligence.
- Funds marketing to retail/individual investors (CARL) instead need to invest in UX/app polish because that audience evaluates credibility through consumer-software cues (app store presence, onboarding flow, community features) rather than through operational-diligence documents.

### Gaps
- No direct interview or published commentary from a boutique fund's own marketing/IR staff was found explaining their budget trade-offs explicitly; the above is inferred from observed site characteristics, not stated motivations.

## What calls-to-action do boutique/emerging funds use to attract prospective investors?

### Takeaway
The dominant CTA across boutique/institutional-facing managers is simply "Contact Us" / an email or phone number, sometimes paired with an Investor Login for existing clients; a gated "download performance report" (accredited-investors-only) and "request more information" patterns also appear. Only the retail-facing example (CARL) used app-download and "Get Started" account-creation CTAs, and no example in this research literally used "request a pitch deck" as site copy, despite that phrase being common in hedge-fund marketing practice generally.

### Cited Findings
- Drury Capital uses a persistent "Contact Us" link throughout navigation plus a footer "Need Assistance?" prompt. — [Investment Products](https://www.drurycapital.com/investment-products/)
- Quantica Capital uses "More about us," "More about our approach," "More news," "More publications" as secondary content-expansion CTAs, with phone/email contact details prominently displayed as the primary conversion point, plus a separate "How to Invest" page and Investor Area login for existing clients. — [Site](https://quantica-capital.com/)
- Episteme Capital's primary CTAs are an "Investor Login" for existing LPs and a general contact email — no visible lead-capture form or newsletter signup was found. — [About](https://www.epistemecap.com/about/)
- Systematic Strategies' only real CTAs are its Contact page link and a Careers page link — no investor-specific lead capture at all. — [Site](https://systematic-strategies.com/)
- CARL's primary CTAs are "Get Started" (account creation), iOS/Android app-download buttons, a three-step onboarding funnel (account setup → investment analysis → funding), a gated performance-report download for accredited investors, and a newsletter subscription prompt in the footer. — [How CARL Works](https://investwithcarl.com/how-carl-works/systematic-trading)
- Benjamin Ball Associates (a presentation coaching service, not a fund) confirms that hedge fund "investor presentation"/pitch-deck creation is itself a service industry, implying pitch decks are typically shared directly with prospects/allocators during outreach rather than offered as a public "request a pitch deck" website CTA — consistent with not finding that literal CTA on any fund site researched. — [Benjamin Ball Associates](https://benjaminball.com/blog/hedge-fund-investor-presentation/)

### Inferences
- Institutional-facing boutique/CTA managers treat their website as a credibility/informational layer, not a lead-generation funnel — the real sales motion (sending a deck, scheduling a call) happens off-site via direct outreach or placement agents, which is why "Contact Us" is sufficient as the on-site CTA rather than a gated-content funnel.
- A new small fund emulating these sites should not expect (or need) aggressive on-site lead capture; a simple contact form/email plus an optional gated one-pager or fact sheet matches observed practice.

### Gaps
- Did not find any boutique quant fund site with an explicit "Request a pitch deck" or "Schedule a call" scheduling-tool (e.g., Calendly) integration; this may exist on sites not captured in this research pass, or may simply be rare in this subsector compared to VC/PE marketing sites.

## Are there common website builder platforms, templates, or agencies specializing in fund/asset-manager websites?

### Takeaway
Yes — a small cluster of boutique agencies (Emerson Ward, Typza, Flycast Media, HEDGE Digital, MVP Design, Collateral Partners-listed agencies) specialize specifically in hedge fund / asset manager / PE website design, with Webflow and Squarespace as the two most commonly cited platforms for this segment (as opposed to fully custom builds used by larger institutions).

### Cited Findings
- **Emerson Ward** (em-ward.com) — boutique creative agency "focused exclusively on providing custom, professional web, collateral, and content solutions to the asset management and fintech ecosystem," offering everything from simple brochureware sites to complex secure investor portals with document/data management, built on Webflow; also offers branding, pitch books, fact sheets, business cards, digital marketing, and video production. Cited client types include a placement firm (Avec Capital), a female-led advisory team (2X Wealth Group/Ingalls & Snyder), and a fund consulting/market-access firm (Zagros Strategic Advisors). — [Websites](https://www.em-ward.com/websites), [Design Support](https://www.em-ward.com/design-support)
- **Typza** (typza.com) — agency specializing exclusively in **Squarespace** websites for hedge funds and financial services, founder holds "Squarespace Certified Designer" and "Circle Member" credentials; builds custom (non-template) sites structured around investment philosophy explainers, team/PM credentials, operational infrastructure details (prime broker, administrator, auditor), investor segmentation pathways, market commentary/blog, password-protected documentation areas, and contact forms aimed at converting allocator research into meeting requests. Pricing is quote-only via a "book a call" flow. — [Hedge Fund Website Design](https://www.typza.com/hedge-fund-website-design)
- **Flycast Media** — UK agency doing "bespoke financial website design" and finance branding for hedge fund managers, IFAs, and wealth managers. — [Flycast Media](https://www.flycastmedia.co.uk/web-design/)
- **HEDGE Digital** (hedgedigital.com) — positions itself specifically around "hedge fund, asset management website design and alternative investment websites," emphasizing document/chart/data management integration. — [HEDGE Digital](https://www.hedgedigital.com/)
- Darien Group and Collateral Partners both publish curated lists/rankings of "top branding agencies for investment managers & private equity firms" and "best private equity web design agencies," indicating an established, documented market of specialist agencies in this niche beyond the few named above. — [Darien Group](https://www.dariengroup.com/insights/top-branding-agencies-for-investment-managers-private-equity-firms-2025), [Collateral Partners](https://www.collateral.com/blog/best-private-equity-web-design-agencies)

### Inferences
- Squarespace and Webflow (rather than WordPress, Wix, or fully bespoke code) appear to be the two dominant platforms specifically recommended/used for this fund-website niche among boutique agencies — likely because both offer a credible, design-forward look with low ongoing engineering overhead, which matches small funds' budget constraints while still needing password-protected/gated sections (a feature both platforms support natively or via light integration).
- There is no single widely-known "fund website template marketplace" equivalent to e.g. generic SaaS landing-page template sites; instead, the market is served by a handful of boutique agencies doing semi-custom builds on general-purpose website builders, each positioning on "understands regulatory/compliance needs of fund marketing" as their differentiator.

### Gaps
- No specific pricing figures were found for any of these agencies (Typza, Emerson Ward, Flycast Media, HEDGE Digital, MVP Design) — all direct prospects to a "book a call" / quote request instead of publishing rates.
- Did not independently verify whether any of the funds researched above (Episteme, Systematic Strategies, Drury, Quantica, Crabel) were themselves built by one of these named agencies — this connection (which specific agency built which fund's site) was not established and would need direct inquiry or footer/credits-page inspection.
