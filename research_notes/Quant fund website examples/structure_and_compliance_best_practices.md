# Fund Website Structure, Legal Constraints, and Best Practices (General Reference, 2026)

## What are the standard pages/sections a fund marketing site needs?

### Takeaway
Top hedge fund / asset manager sites converge on a small, disciplined set of sections — Home, Strategy/Approach, Team, (gated) Performance/Track Record or Investor Relations portal, Research/Insights, Contact, and a Legal/Disclosures footer — and deliberately avoid cramming in more; the standard advice is "say less, assume visitor sophistication."

### Cited Findings
- "The home page should tell a qualified visitor within eight seconds what the firm does, for whom, and why it is credible," including a headline naming the strategy type and asset class, a specific (falsifiable) two-sentence philosophy statement, and one clear path to investor relations/contact — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- "The team page consistently receives more engagement than any other section" and should include complete bios: undergrad/grad institutions, prior firms with dates, specific roles, credentials (CFA, CAIA), and ideally links to verifiable public profiles, cross-referenced against SEC EDGAR Form ADV disclosures — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- The Strategy/Approach page should name the specific strategy (global macro, long/short equity, event-driven, etc.), explain the informational/structural edge in plain language, and be explicit about risk profile and the investor type the strategy suits — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- "Investor portals hold performance data, offering documents, and detailed materials that belong in controlled environments, not public pages" — a password-protected Investor Relations portal is the standard home for pitch decks, DDQs, audited financials, PPMs/offering memoranda, and monthly letters, with functioning logins and current documents — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- Trust-signal elements allocators look for: clear legal entity name and regulatory registration (Form ADV or equivalent), named team with verifiable career histories, precise (not vague) strategy description, and disclosed operational infrastructure — prime broker, fund administrator, auditor, legal counsel — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- Visual/design conventions: dark navy, deep charcoal, off-white, muted gold palettes signal stability; serif type signals tradition while clean sans-serif signals precision; avoid full-screen autoplay videos, animated tickers, and generic rotating stock photography; mobile responsiveness and HTTPS/page-speed are treated as baseline requirements, not differentiators — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- Overview articles of hedge fund site design/examples from agencies (Founderjar, Easify, Brand Glow Up, Nexaflow, Collateral Partners) consistently frame the site's job as removing friction/doubt for an already-interested allocator rather than generating cold leads — [Collateral Partners](https://collateral.com/blog/top-hedge-fund-websites), [Founderjar](https://www.founderjar.com/inspiration/hedge-fund-websites/), [Easify Technologies](https://easifytechnologies.com/14-best-hedge-fund-website-examples/)

### Inferences
- A fund site's public-facing IA should be deliberately "thin" (Home / Strategy / Team / Insights / Contact / Legal) with a clearly separated, access-controlled Investor Relations layer for anything resembling performance data or offering documents — this split is as much a legal necessity (see compliance section below) as a UX choice.
- "Research/Insights" and "News/Press" sections function primarily as credibility/SEO content and should stay informational (market commentary, thought leadership) rather than performance-promotional, to stay outside Marketing Rule "advertisement" scrutiny where possible.

### Gaps
- None of the sources enumerated a canonical "Privacy Policy" or "Terms of Use" page explicitly for hedge funds (as opposed to disclaimer content generally) — this is standard web-compliance boilerplate covered by general data-privacy law (e.g., CCPA/GDPR) rather than fund-specific guidance found in this research pass.

## What legal/regulatory constraints apply to what a fund can say publicly about performance or soliciting investment (US, with UK note)?

### Takeaway
The core US constraint is the Reg D general-solicitation rule: a fund relying on Rule 506(b) cannot have ANY public-facing solicitation (including an open website) and must gate everything behind a pre-existing relationship or password wall, while a fund relying on Rule 506(c) may advertise and market publicly (including performance claims, subject to the separate Investment Advisers Act Marketing Rule) but must take documented "reasonable steps" to verify every purchaser is actually an accredited investor before accepting their money. The UK's FCA regime achieves a similar "gate everything to qualified recipients" result through financial-promotion restrictions on high-risk/non-mass-market investments.

### Cited Findings
- **Rule 506(b) (no general solicitation):** "they cannot use any form of general solicitation or public advertising. This means no website marketing, social media campaigns, mass emails, or public presentations. All investor communications must happen through pre-existing relationships or private, one-on-one communications," and the relationship "must be substantive — not merely a name on a mailing list — and must be established before the offering begins" — [Databento / search synthesis of Procopio, Trenam, PipelineRoad, PPM Lawyers](https://www.procopio.com/resource/website-mistake-hurting-private-offering)
- "Content that isn't secured by password protection can qualify as general solicitation" under 506(b); SEC staff has accepted that a password-protected site built around policies/procedures designed to establish a substantive pre-existing relationship before disclosing the offering did not itself constitute impermissible general solicitation — [Procopio / Trenam summary](https://www.procopio.com/resource/website-mistake-hurting-private-offering)
- **Rule 506(c) (general solicitation permitted):** "Rule 506(c) permits issuers to broadly solicit and generally advertise an offering, provided that all purchasers in the offering are accredited investors" — a 506(c) fund "can describe itself and its offering in public: a website, social media, press, conference pitches, and cold outreach to allocators you have never met" — [SEC.gov](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c), [Repool](https://repool.com/fundpedia/rule-506c/)
- Under 506(c), issuers "must take reasonable steps to verify purchasers' accredited investor status" based on factors other than mere self-certification (unlike 506(b), where self-certification is generally sufficient) — [SEC.gov](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c), [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/04/02/sec-guidance-on-accredited-investor-verification-in-general-solicitation-securities-offerings)
- A March 2025 SEC staff letter clarified that a fund with a minimum investment of at least $200,000 for individuals or $1,000,000 for entities can generally satisfy verification based on written investor representations alone, without additional documentation — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/sec-provides-bright-line-test-for-investor-verification-under-rule-506c/), [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/04/02/sec-guidance-on-accredited-investor-verification-in-general-solicitation-securities-offerings)
- 506(c) issuers must also file Form D with the SEC within 15 days of first sale and comply with "bad actor" disqualification rules; securities sold remain restricted — [SEC.gov](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c)
- **Investment Advisers Act Marketing Rule (206(4)-1), separate from Reg D and applicable to SEC-registered advisers' "advertisements" regardless of solicitation method:** all advertisements are prohibited from containing untrue/misleading statements, from making claims the adviser cannot substantiate on demand, from presenting benefits without "fair and balanced treatment of any associated material risks," and from omitting material facts that would otherwise make the ad misleading — [SEC.gov Small Business Compliance Guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing)
- Performance-specific Marketing Rule requirements: must show **net performance alongside gross performance**; cannot show performance without specified time periods (with limited exceptions); must present performance of all "substantially similar" portfolios (no cherry-picking); cannot imply SEC has approved the calculations; hypothetical/backtested performance requires documented policies ensuring relevance to the specific intended audience — [SEC.gov Small Business Compliance Guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing)
- **Testimonials/endorsements** (now permitted under the Marketing Rule, a reversal from the pre-2021 blanket ban): must disclose whether the endorser is a client, whether/how much they were compensated, and any material conflicts of interest; promoters receiving over $1,000 in compensation in a trailing 12 months require a written agreement; "bad actors" cannot be used as compensated promoters; the adviser must oversee ongoing compliance by promoters — [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/the-secs-new-marketing-rule-practically-speaking-testimonials-and-endorsements/), [SEC.gov](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing)
- Recordkeeping: advisers must retain advertisements, internal approvals, disclosures, and supporting substantiation documentation for at least five years, including performance data, testimonial/endorsement records, third-party ratings, and related compensation arrangements — [search synthesis, multiple compliance-vendor sources](https://www.smarsh.com/regulations/sec-marketing-rule-206-4-1)
- Marketing Rule compliance deadline was November 4, 2022 (adoption permitted starting May 4, 2021); as of January 2026 SEC staff further clarified that certain SRO disciplinary orders do not automatically disqualify a person from providing compensated testimonials/endorsements, provided other eligibility/disclosure conditions are met — [search synthesis](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2020-334)
- **UK (FCA) comparison:** financial promotions for higher-risk/non-mass-market investments may only be made to certified high-net-worth investors, certified sophisticated investors, self-certified sophisticated investors, or certified "restricted" investors, and must be communicated or approved by an FCA-authorised firm; mass-marketing of such investments to the general public is not permitted — [FCA PS22/10](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps22-10.pdf), [FCA Handbook COBS 4.12B](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/cobs4/cobs4s18)
- A certified high-net-worth individual in the UK is someone whose accountant/employer certifies income ≥£100,000 in the last year or net assets ≥£250,000; HM Treasury has proposed requiring firms to "reasonably believe" an investor qualifies rather than rely solely on a signed self-certification statement, tightening the regime further — [search synthesis of FCA/HM Treasury consultation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/61b9c0d8e90e070448c521b0/Financial_Promotion_Exemptions_Con.pdf)

### Inferences
- The choice of Reg D exemption (506(b) vs 506(c)) is the single biggest determinant of site architecture: a 506(b) fund effectively cannot have a public marketing site with any solicitation content at all (it needs an entirely gated/password site, or a purely "about us, no offering talk" public shell plus a private portal reached only via pre-existing relationships), whereas a 506(c) fund can run a fully public marketing site but must still comply with the separate (and stricter in substance) Advisers Act Marketing Rule on anything that counts as performance advertising, and must build a robust accredited-investor verification flow (documentation, not just a checkbox) before accepting any subscription.
- Because the Marketing Rule applies independently of which Reg D exemption is used, even a 506(c) fund that can legally show performance publicly will usually still choose to gate detailed track-record data behind a login — both to limit who can act on it and to simplify "fair and balanced" / net-vs-gross / substantiation recordkeeping obligations to a known, verified audience.
- The UK's regime is functionally similar to US 506(b)-style gating by default (restrict to defined qualifying categories unless promotions are approved by an authorised firm) — there isn't a direct UK equivalent of "506(c) with verification" that cleanly opens the door to full public advertising of fund performance.

### Gaps
- This research did not find fund-specific case law or enforcement actions illustrating exactly where a "gray area" public website (e.g., generic firm description with no performance numbers) crosses into general solicitation under 506(b) — the Procopio/Trenam sources describe the standard but a reader should get specific counsel review of site copy against *Citizen VC* no-action-letter-style analysis before launch.
- Could not confirm current (2026) specifics of exact EU equivalent rules (e.g., AIFMD marketing passport / national private placement regimes) — out of scope per the US-primary focus, but worth flagging as a gap if the fund ever markets into the EU.

## What standard disclaimer/disclosure language commonly appears on fund websites?

### Takeaway
Fund websites use a recurring, largely formulaic set of disclaimer elements — performance disclaimers, "not an offer to sell" language, accredited-investor-only warnings, and (if applicable) hypothetical/backtested-performance caveats — typically centralized in a footer-linked "Legal/Disclosures" page rather than scattered across the site.

### Cited Findings
- Standard performance disclaimer phrasing variants found in the wild: "Please remember that past performance may not be indicative of future results" and "past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results" and "Investors may get back less than they invested" — [ClearRock Disclaimer PDF via search](https://www.etf.com/docs/ClearRock_Disclaimer.pdf)
- Extended institutional phrasing: "Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results and there can be no assurance that the fund will be able to achieve comparable results... Actual results may vary, may be materially lower and may involve a complete loss of investment" — [search synthesis of fund disclaimer pages](https://www.fssaim.com/uk/en/institutional/config/disclaimers/price-performance-disclaimer.html)
- Hypothetical/backtested performance requires its own disclaimer under the Marketing Rule: documented policies must ensure the hypothetical performance is relevant to the likely financial situation and investment objectives of the intended audience, and sources note hypothetical results "have many inherent limitations" with no representation that similar results will be achieved in the future — [SEC.gov Marketing Rule guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing); phrasing pattern corroborated by general search of fund disclaimers
- A dedicated "General Marketing Disclaimer" PDF pattern exists among funds, titled around "Not an Offer to Sell Securities" plus other important information, though the exact clause text could not be extracted from the PDF retrieved in this research pass (binary/compressed content) — [Post Capital example, PDF](https://www.postcp.com/pdf/General-Marketing-Disclaimer-2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- A compliant fund "Legal/Disclosures" page typically needs, at minimum, four standard clauses: (1) a "this is not an offer to sell / solicitation to buy securities, which can only be made via a confidential offering memorandum to qualified recipients" clause; (2) a past-performance disclaimer; (3) if any backtested/hypothetical numbers appear anywhere on the public site, a hypothetical-performance limitations disclaimer; and (4) an accredited/qualified-investor eligibility statement gating access to any offering materials.
- Because exact boilerplate text is proprietary/counsel-drafted and varies by fund structure (506(b) vs 506(c), US vs offshore feeder, RIA vs exempt reporting adviser), this is an area where the report should recommend the fund have final disclaimer language reviewed/drafted by securities counsel rather than copying any single example verbatim.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve verbatim, fully-sourced disclaimer boilerplate text from a live example (PDF extraction failed, and Alpha Architect's disclosures page returned HTTP 403). The specific legal phrasing patterns above are paraphrased/aggregated from search snippets rather than directly quoted from a verified primary source — treat exact wording as illustrative, not as counsel-approved boilerplate to copy directly.

## What UX patterns are common for gating sensitive content (e.g., accredited-investor click-through before performance data)?

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is a two-tier site: a fully public informational layer (home, strategy, team, insights, contact, legal) plus a password-protected or login-gated "Investor Relations" / "Investor Portal" layer holding performance, DDQs, audited financials, and offering documents — access to the gated layer is typically preceded by a self-certification step (an accredited-investor attestation / click-through) and, for actual subscriptions, a more rigorous documented verification process, often outsourced to specialized verification vendors.

### Cited Findings
- "Investor portals hold performance data, offering documents, and detailed materials that belong in controlled environments, not public pages," requiring "functional login systems and current documents" and "direct contact to real personnel" — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- Dedicated third-party verification platforms exist specifically to serve this gating need: VerifyInvestor.com offers "Rule 506(c) accredited investor verification and Rule 506(b) accredited investor review services along with AML/KYC checks"; iCapital offers an embeddable "Investor Passport" allowing an investor to verify accredited status once and reuse that verification across partner sites via a single line of JavaScript/API integration — [VerifyInvestor](https://www.verifyinvestor.com/), [iCapital](https://icapital.com/insights/practice-management/a-guide-to-the-accredited-investor-verification-process/)
- For 506(b) funds, SEC staff guidance (via no-action analysis) supports using password-protection plus a process designed to establish a substantive relationship (e.g., a qualifying questionnaire) before an investor ever sees offering-specific content, rather than a simple one-click "I am accredited" checkbox — [Procopio/Trenam synthesis](https://www.procopio.com/resource/website-mistake-hurting-private-offering)
- For 506(c) funds relying on the 2025 SEC staff bright-line test, the investor-facing verification step can be as simple as a written representation combined with a qualifying minimum investment size ($200k individuals / $1M entities), reducing (but not eliminating) the need for heavier documentary proof (tax returns, bank/broker letters) at the gate — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/sec-provides-bright-line-test-for-investor-verification-under-rule-506c/)

### Inferences
- A pragmatic implementation for a new fund site: (1) a lightweight, non-binding "I am an accredited/qualified investor" self-certification interstitial or login wall in front of the Investor Relations section, to limit casual public access to performance/offering materials and reduce Marketing Rule / general-solicitation exposure; (2) a separate, more rigorous verification step (documents, third-party verification service, or the $200k/$1M bright-line representation) only at actual subscription time, not at the point of merely viewing marketing materials — these are two distinct gates serving two distinct legal purposes (limiting advertising exposure vs. actually admitting a purchaser).

### Gaps
- Could not find a named, widely-cited UX pattern/term of art (akin to "age-gate") specifically for the accredited-investor click-through; the practice is described functionally in sources but not branded or standardized the way COPPA age gates are. Specific conversion/engagement data on click-through gates for fund sites was not found.

## What do branding/design consultants recommend for tone, trust signals, and conversion on fund sites?

### Takeaway
Consultants converge on "restraint as a credibility signal": fund sites should favor sober, information-dense, low-flash design over consumer-marketing polish, foreground verifiable facts (regulatory registration, named team with checkable histories, named service providers) over persuasive copy, and route all serious engagement toward a single, simple contact/IR path rather than multiple competing calls-to-action.

### Cited Findings
- "Professional design is baseline, and what separates top funds is the discipline to say less and assume visitor sophistication" — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- "Hedge fund websites need to feel measured, credible and controlled, with design supporting confidence rather than creating distractions" — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- Trust-signal checklist allocators implicitly run through a site against: legal entity name + regulatory registration, named team with verifiable histories, precise (non-vague) strategy language, and disclosed operational infrastructure (prime broker, administrator, auditor, legal counsel) — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- "A well-built website removes barriers" rather than directly generating capital — i.e., the site's conversion job is to confirm credibility a relationship/introduction has already suggested, and poor execution actively undermines that credibility rather than merely failing to add to it — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- Visual identity recommendations: dark navy / deep charcoal / off-white / muted gold palettes to signal stability; serif type for a "tradition" register, clean sans-serif for a "precision" register — either can work if used consistently — [Thomas Digital](https://thomasdigital.com/industry/hedge-fund-websites)
- Multiple design-agency roundup articles (Founderjar, Easify Technologies, Brand Glow Up, Collateral Partners) independently catalog real/example hedge fund sites as a genre, reinforcing the same pattern of minimal navigation, large whitespace, muted palettes, and a single prominent "Contact / Investor Relations" CTA rather than multiple competing CTAs — [Founderjar](https://www.founderjar.com/inspiration/hedge-fund-websites/), [Easify Technologies](https://easifytechnologies.com/14-best-hedge-fund-website-examples/), [Collateral Partners](https://collateral.com/blog/top-hedge-fund-websites)

### Inferences
- Because this fund site will be targeted at sophisticated allocators rather than retail, the recommended conversion flow is "one clear low-friction path to a human" (a named IR contact or a short qualification form) rather than lead-gen tactics (pop-ups, email captures, chatbots) more common in consumer fintech — those patterns would likely read as a negative trust signal in this market segment.
- Team-page depth (named individuals, dated career history, credentials, cross-referenced against Form ADV) is worth prioritizing relative to other content, since it is independently identified as the highest-engagement section and the easiest place to establish or undermine credibility.

### Gaps
- Sources found were general hedge-fund-website design roundups/agency marketing content rather than rigorous UX-research studies (e.g., no first-party usability testing data, time-on-page statistics, or A/B test results were available) — treat the "team page gets most engagement" and similar engagement claims as agency-reported practitioner experience rather than independently verified quantitative research.
