# Market Breadth: Indicators and What Low Breadth Signals About Market Regime

## What are the standard market breadth indicators and how are they computed?

### Takeaway
Practitioners rely on a small family of breadth indicators — the Advance-Decline (A/D) Line, percent of stocks above a moving average, the McClellan Oscillator and Summation Index, new highs vs. new lows, and breadth-thrust signals — all built from the same raw ingredient (counts of advancing vs. declining/rising vs. falling stocks) but smoothed or transformed differently to capture short-term momentum vs. longer-term trend health.

### Cited Findings
- **Advance-Decline (A/D) Line**: a cumulative breadth indicator. Formula: `AD Line (current) = AD Line (previous) + Net Advances (current)`, where Net Advances = advancing issues − declining issues. The starting value is arbitrary; only the shape/direction matters. — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Advance-Decline Line](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/advance-decline-line)
- An A/D Line that rises and records new highs alongside the underlying index shows strong participation (bullish); a narrowing/lagging A/D Line while price makes new highs is a bearish divergence. — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Advance-Decline Line](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/advance-decline-line)
- **Percent of stocks above a moving average**: measures the share of stocks in an index trading above a given moving average (commonly 50-day for shorter-term, 150-day/200-day for medium-to-long-term). The 50% level is the key threshold for the indicator; above 50% = bullish bias, below 50% = bearish bias. More specifically, >70% above the 200-day MA signals broad strength/bullish trend, while <30% signals market weakness/bearish trend. — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Percent Above Moving Average](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/percent-above-moving-average)
- As of market close Sept 18, 2026, 43.2% of US stocks were above their 200-day moving average (illustrative current reading, not itself historically load-bearing but shows the indicator in practical use). — [TheTrading.Tools: Market Breadth Today](https://www.thetrading.tools/market-breadth)
- **McClellan Oscillator**: derived from Net Advances converted to Ratio-Adjusted Net Advances (RANA) — net advances as a percentage of total issues traded, ×1,000 — then computed as the 19-day EMA of RANA minus the 39-day EMA of RANA. Positive = advancing issues gaining strength; negative = declining issues dominate. — [StockCharts ChartSchool: McClellan Oscillator](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/mcclellan-oscillator)
- A "breadth thrust" — the Oscillator surging from below −50 to above +50 (especially swings exceeding ±100) — signals a surge in bullish breadth that can mark important market bottoms and lead to an extended advance. — [StockCharts ChartSchool: McClellan Oscillator](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/mcclellan-oscillator)
- **McClellan Summation Index**: the cumulative sum of daily McClellan Oscillator values (`Summation Index = previous Summation Index + current day's Oscillator value`, starting from an initial value typically zero). Because it's cumulative, it's a slower, smoother version of the Oscillator, crosses zero less often, and is used for medium/long-term breadth timing rather than short-term signals. — [DefinedGe Securities: McClellan Summation Index](https://www.definedgesecurities.com/library/mcclellan-summation-index/)
- **Zweig Breadth Thrust**: computed as a 10-day exponential moving average of (advancing issues) ÷ (advancing + declining issues). It is a rare signal — only ~19-20 occurrences since WWII (through the search date). — [Investing.com: Zweig Breadth Thrust](https://www.investing.com/analysis/zweig-breadth-thrust-is-it-a-trustworthy-technical-indicator-for-stock-market-200660043)
- Historical performance after Zweig Breadth Thrust signals (a *high-breadth surge* signal, useful as the opposite pole from "low breadth"): average S&P 500 gains of 5.4% after 1 month, 8.3% after 3 months, 17.2% after 6 months, and 23.3% after 1 year, with a "perfect" track record of positive 6- and 12-month returns since 1950 across the ~20 signals. Separately, Bulkowski (2013) found classic Breadth Thrust signals preceded market advances in 83% of occurrences since 1950, averaging 22.4% gains over the following 12 months. — [Yahoo Finance: This Stock Market Indicator Has Been 100% Accurate Since 1957](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/stock-market-indicator-100-accurate-080000872.html); [Investing.com: Zweig Breadth Thrust](https://www.investing.com/analysis/zweig-breadth-thrust-is-it-a-trustworthy-technical-indicator-for-stock-market-200660043)
- **New highs vs. new lows**: a bearish divergence develops when an index keeps rising while the count of stocks making new highs declines, indicating fewer stocks are participating and market leadership is narrowing. Such conditions often appear late in market cycles when a small group of large stocks keeps pushing the index up while the broader market weakens. — [source article on breadth divergence/new highs, from search aggregation of StockCharts/RIA/AAII content](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/stock-market-breadth-warning-or-opportunity/)
- India-specific breadth toolkit: the Advance-Decline Line, the Advance-Decline Ratio (advances ÷ declines, with 1.0 the critical threshold; >1.5 considered healthy, i.e., roughly 60% of stocks advancing), percent of Nifty 500 stocks above 50-day/200-day MAs, and NSE 52-week highs vs. lows are the standard indicators used for Indian equities. — [OneTradeJournal: Advance Decline Line — Reading Nifty Breadth and Divergence](https://onetradejournal.com/indicators/advance-decline-line); [Ventura Securities: Market Breadth Indicator Meaning Explained](https://www.venturasecurities.com/share-market-glossary/market-breadth-indicator/)

### Inferences
- All breadth measures are essentially transformations of the same underlying advance/decline (or above/below-MA) count data at different smoothing horizons: A/D Line and % above MA are more "levels/trend" oriented; McClellan Oscillator and Zweig Breadth Thrust are "momentum/thrust" oriented (fast changes in participation); the Summation Index and long-horizon % above 200-day MA are the slow, regime-level view.
- Because a breadth thrust (rapid *expansion* from low to high participation) has historically been a strong bullish signal, it follows that the depressed, pre-thrust low-breadth state it emerges from is being implicitly treated by practitioners as an oversold/washed-out condition that historically preceded — not followed — strong up-moves, which is a nuance to reconcile against the "low breadth = bearish/late-cycle" framing found elsewhere in the research (see next section).

### Gaps
- No single source in this research directly gave the exact numeric formula/threshold levels practitioners use for the McClellan Summation Index's own bullish/bearish extremes (only the calculation, not standard reading bands).

## What does persistently low breadth typically indicate about market character?

### Takeaway
Persistently low breadth (few stocks above their own moving averages, decliners dominating advancers) is generally read by practitioners as narrow/concentrated leadership and weakening underlying market health — a warning sign associated with late-cycle tops, deteriorating internals beneath a still-rising index, or a choppy/rotational regime where the "market" is really just a handful of large caps carrying the index while most stocks languish.

### Cited Findings
- A rising index accompanied by a deteriorating A/D Line — more stocks declining than advancing even as the index climbs — is described as "a classic breadth divergence, signalling a narrow, unsustainable rally driven by only a few large-cap stocks rather than broad market participation," and "this narrow breadth often precedes a correction." — [OneTradeJournal: Advance Decline Line — Reading Nifty Breadth and Divergence](https://onetradejournal.com/indicators/advance-decline-line)
- Narrow, bearish-divergence conditions (declining new-highs counts while price rises) "often appear late in market cycles, when a small group of large stocks continues pushing indexes higher while the broader market begins to weaken." — [aggregated from search: RIA/StockCharts commentary on breadth divergence](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/stock-market-breadth-warning-or-opportunity/)
- Example of a recent narrow/low-breadth reading used as a market-character signal: "S&P 500 Breadth Narrowest Since Dot-Com, Goldman Warns" — Goldman Sachs flagged current S&P 500 breadth concentration as comparable to the dot-com era, implying a narrow-leadership regime. (Full article text could not be retrieved — WebFetch was blocked with HTTP 403 — so only the headline/framing is confirmed, not Goldman's specific statistic.) — [Benzinga: S&P 500 Breadth Narrowest Since Dot-Com, Goldman Warns](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/economic-data/26/05/52210245/sp-500-market-breadth-narrowest-since-dot-com-goldman-sachs-warns)
- During the dot-com bubble (1997-2000), the share of US market cap held by the 10 largest stocks rose from 15% to 24%, and the S&P 500's Herfindahl-Hirschman concentration index more than doubled between 1995 and 2000, peaking months before the bubble burst — a period of extremely narrow leadership (Microsoft, Cisco, Intel, AOL, Yahoo dominating gains) while the average stock lagged. — [Bloomberg: Dot-Com Bubble Flashbacks Triggered by Lack of S&P 500 Breadth](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-05/dot-com-bubble-flashbacks-triggered-by-lack-of-s-p-500-breadth) (headline/framing from search snippet; full text inaccessible)
- "The lesson across these decades is consistent: concentration doesn't cause corrections on its own, but it magnifies them once they arrive" — narrow breadth/concentration is framed as an amplifier of downside risk rather than a standalone trigger. — [search-aggregated summary of dot-com/concentration analyses](https://redlotuscapitals.medium.com/historical-analysis-of-the-us-stock-markets-dot-com-bubble-era-1995-early-2000-4b4ffb18768d)
- A market with high dispersion (individual stocks moving more than the index) and low correlation reflects "a lack of a unified market trend," pointing to diverse individual stock performance independent of overall market movement — i.e., a rotational, "market of stocks not a stock market" character, which is one specific character type low breadth/participation data can indicate (as opposed to an outright broad correction). — [Nationwide: Market dispersion is back—why stock selection matters more now](https://www.nationwide.com/financial-professionals/blog/markets-economy/articles/market-dispersion-is-back-why-stock-selection-matters-more-now); [SpotGamma: Dispersion, Correlation, Volatility and the Stock Bubble](https://spotgamma.com/dispersion-correlation-volatility-and-the-stock-bubble/)
- A counter-perspective exists: an article titled "'Narrow' Markets Are Not a Bad Sign for Stocks" appeared in search results, indicating some practitioners dispute that narrow breadth is reliably bearish. — [ETF Trends/ETF DB: 'Narrow' Markets Are Not a Bad Sign for Stocks](https://etfdb.com/etf-strategist-channel/narrow-markets-are-not-a-bad-sign-for-stocks/) (title/framing only; full content not fetched)
- In the current (2026) Indian market, the number of Nifty Total Market stocks trading near 52-week lows climbed to 14%, up from 2% in April, described as "a noticeable shift signalling that fewer stocks are participating in the market's upside and more are losing value" — an example of deteriorating breadth signaling weakening market character in the Indian context. — [Whalesbook: Market Breadth Softens: 14% of Nifty Stocks Near Yearly Lows](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/brokerage-reports/Market-Breadth-Softens-14percent-of-Nifty-Stocks-Near-Yearly-Lows/6ab1fb735aacb956d07c28dc)

### Inferences
- The evidence points to at least three distinct "flavors" of low breadth that a report should probably distinguish: (1) narrow/concentrated leadership with index still rising (a divergence/late-cycle-warning pattern, e.g., dot-com 1999-2000, 2015 FAANG, current 2026 concentration flagged by Goldman); (2) broad-based weakening breadth with index also falling or stalling (an outright, broadening correction/bear signal, e.g., rising 52-week-low counts in Nifty); and (3) low breadth from high dispersion/low correlation with no clear index trend at all (a choppy/rotational regime — "market of stocks" rather than "stock market"). These have different implications for which trading strategies work.
- The existence of a credible dissenting view ("narrow markets are not a bad sign") suggests low breadth should be treated as a probabilistic/contextual warning sign rather than a deterministic bearish signal — the report should avoid over-claiming certainty here.

## What is "breadth divergence" and what has historically followed it?

### Takeaway
Breadth divergence — the index making new highs (or holding up) while breadth measures (A/D Line, new-highs count, cumulative volume breadth) fail to confirm — has historically preceded significant market weakness in several documented US cases (2007 pre-GFC, 2015 pre-2016 correction), while bullish divergences (breadth holding up while price makes new lows) have preceded market bottoms (2002-2003, mid-2004, 2009).

### Cited Findings
- **2007 (bearish divergence → 2008 bear market)**: "Chart 3 shows two bearish divergences in the NYSE AD Line from June to November 2007. The NYSE Composite moved to new highs in July, but the AD Line peaked at the beginning of June." These two consecutive bearish divergences preceded "the January support break and the bear market of 2008." — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Advance-Decline Line](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/advance-decline-line)
- **2002-2003 and 2004 (bullish divergence → market lows)**: Bullish divergences from October 2002 to March 2003, and May-August 2004, foreshadowed "important lows in the stock market." — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Advance-Decline Line](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/advance-decline-line)
- **June-July 2009 (bullish divergence → rally)**: A bullish divergence preceded a 10% rally from the July low to the August high. — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Advance-Decline Line](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/advance-decline-line)
- **2015 (bearish divergence → 2015-16 weakness)**: In July 2015, the NASDAQ Composite and cumulative volume breadth lines both made all-time highs; subsequently a negative divergence developed as participation lagged, especially visible in cumulative volume charts — a pattern of narrowing participation ("early FAANG" stocks driving the index) while the broader market weakened. — [search-aggregated summary citing StockCharts "A New Breadth of Fresh Air" and related Dec 2015 commentary](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-dancing-2015-12-a-new-breadth-of-fresh-air/)
- **2024-2025 (bearish divergence, recent)**: While the S&P 500 continued rising from October 2024 into early 2025, net new highs began declining, signaling weakening participation beneath the surface; the source states "narrowing breadth like this often precedes market pullbacks." — [search-aggregated summary, RIA/AAII breadth-divergence commentary](https://realinvestmentadvice.com/resources/blog/stock-market-breadth-warning-or-opportunity/)
- General definition: "narrowing participation is often identified with a bearish divergence" when prices hit new highs but the A/D Line lags; conversely, bullish divergences occur when prices decline to new lows while the A/D Line holds up, suggesting fewer stocks are actually declining than the price action implies. — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Advance-Decline Line](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-indicators/advance-decline-line)

### Inferences
- Across the documented US cases (2002-03, 2004, 2007, 2009, 2015, 2024-25), breadth divergence functions symmetrically: bearish divergence (price up, breadth down) has consistently preceded corrections/bear markets (2007→2008, 2015→2016 weakness, 2024-25→subsequent pullback), while bullish divergence (price down, breadth flat/up) has consistently preceded bottoms/rallies (2002-03, 2004, 2009). This symmetry supports using breadth divergence as a two-sided regime-timing tool, not just a bearish-only warning.
- The 2007 and 2015 cases both show the divergence appearing months (not days) before the eventual market move, suggesting breadth divergence is a leading-but-noisy signal with a multi-month lag rather than a precise timing tool.

### Gaps
- No non-US (e.g., Indian, European, other Asian) historical breadth-divergence case study with documented before/after price behavior was found in this research; the India-specific findings retrieved were about current breadth softening (2026), not a historical divergence-then-outcome case study. This is a notable gap given the assignment's interest in Indian markets.
- Quantitative statistics (e.g., average magnitude/duration of decline following a confirmed bearish divergence, hit rate) were not found — the sources describe qualitative "preceded" relationships without backtested hit-rate/magnitude statistics.

## Is there quantitative/backtested evidence on individual-stock trend-following or breakout strategy performance in low- vs high-breadth periods?

### Takeaway
Direct backtests comparing individual-stock trend-following/breakout strategy performance specifically conditioned on breadth regime were not found; available evidence is indirect, drawn from trend-following/momentum research on regime-dependence, choppy-market whipsaw effects, and dispersion/correlation dynamics that are closely related to breadth but not the same metric.

### Cited Findings
- Trend-following investing across global asset classes since 1880 has delivered strong long-run positive returns, but "the outperformance of the trend-driven process is not stable over time: periods with exploitable trends alternate with long-lasting trendless periods" — i.e., regime-dependence is well documented for trend-following generally, though not tied specifically to a breadth metric. — [Trend Following: A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing (whitepaper)](https://www.trendfollowing.com/whitepaper/Century_Evidence_Trend_Following.pdf)
- Research examining 66,000+ simulated long-only trend trades found "a highly skewed profit distribution, with less than 7% of trades driving the cumulative profitability" — implying most trend/breakout trades are unprofitable or marginal and returns depend on a small number of large trending moves (consistent with breakout strategies needing a genuine, broad trend/thrust to pay off, which low-breadth/choppy conditions would suppress). — [search-aggregated summary of quant trend research](https://medium.com/@writeronepagecode/quant-trading-unlocked-from-crisis-alpha-hedging-to-algorithmic-backtesting-2f924b6903e0)
- "Some researchers have replaced the usual crash protection through trend following on the asset level by breadth momentum on the universe level instead" — indicating some quant researchers explicitly link breadth-based measures to trend-following strategy design, though the specific study/results were not surfaced in full. — [Quantpedia-adjacent search summary, exact paper not identified](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23150v2)
- In choppy/sideways markets, momentum/breakout signals "fail more" and require reduced position sizing or stronger confirmation; moves that occur "usually fade just as fast," and most breakouts fail with traders "stopped out extensively in bad, sideways or bear markets." — [search-aggregated summary of momentum/breakout-in-choppy-markets commentary](https://bookmap.com/blog/momentum-vs-mean-reversion-which-dominates-in-a-choppy-market)
- Momentum crashes are often driven by sector rotations (e.g., technology collapsing while financials recover); constructing momentum portfolios to be sector-neutral has been proposed as a way to reduce exposure to these rotation-driven crashes — directly relevant to the "choppy/rotational regime" character of low breadth. — [search-aggregated summary citing momentum crash/rotation research](https://paretoquant.substack.com/p/monthly-close-4-momentum-crash-rotation)
- High dispersion combined with low correlation among stocks (a market-character signature that overlaps with low-breadth/rotational regimes) is associated with conditions where "selectivity matters and active management may have greater scope to add value beyond benchmark returns" — implying individual-stock selection strategies (as opposed to broad trend-following on the index) may be relatively favored when breadth is low and dispersion is high. — [Nationwide: Market dispersion is back—why stock selection matters more now](https://www.nationwide.com/financial-professionals/blog/markets-economy/articles/market-dispersion-is-back-why-stock-selection-matters-more-now)

### Inferences
- The available (indirect) evidence suggests low-breadth/choppy/rotational regimes are structurally adverse for broad breakout/trend-following approaches (more false breakouts, faster fades, sector-rotation-driven momentum crashes) but potentially more favorable for careful individual-stock selection/dispersion-aware strategies, since low correlation and high dispersion — a common feature of low-breadth markets — is exactly the condition under which stock-specific factors (rather than the market factor) drive returns.
- No source provided a controlled backtest with breadth as the explicit conditioning variable (e.g., "breakout win rate when % stocks above 50-day MA < 30% vs. > 70%"), so any claim of a precise, quantified performance differential would not be well-sourced.

### Gaps

This is the weakest-evidenced key question in this research; the report should flag this as an area where the underlying report may need to rely on inference/analogy (dispersion/correlation regime literature, momentum-crash/rotation literature) rather than a direct breadth-conditioned backtest.

## Are there other known historical low-breadth periods with documented before/after market behavior (beyond those in the divergence section)?

### Takeaway
Beyond the 2007 and 2015 breadth-divergence cases already documented, the clearest recurring historical example of a "low breadth / narrow leadership" period is the 2000 dot-com top, where extreme concentration in a handful of tech/internet mega-caps preceded a severe, broad decline; a comparable concentration level is being flagged again in 2026 by Goldman Sachs, though the eventual outcome of that current episode is of course not yet knowable.

### Cited Findings
- Dot-com top (1999-2000): top-10 US stocks' market-cap share rose from 15% (1997) to 24% (2000); S&P 500 Herfindahl-Hirschman concentration index more than doubled 1995-2000, peaking months before the crash; by June 2000 the S&P 500 had a 33% weight in Information Technology vs. 20% for the Dow. — [Morningstar / Vontobel / Redlotus-aggregated search summary of dot-com concentration data](https://redlotuscapitals.medium.com/historical-analysis-of-the-us-stock-markets-dot-com-bubble-era-1995-early-2000-4b4ffb18768d)
- Outcome after the dot-com top: the Nasdaq Composite peaked in March 2000 and lost roughly 78% of its value by October 2002, with even quality technology names suffering severe drawdowns. — [search-aggregated summary of dot-com bubble aftermath](https://redlotuscapitals.medium.com/historical-analysis-of-the-us-stock-markets-dot-com-bubble-era-1995-early-2000-4b4ffb18768d)
- Current (2026) episode: Goldman Sachs has characterized S&P 500 breadth as "narrowest since dot-com," explicitly invoking the dot-com precedent as the historical comparison for today's concentrated leadership. — [Benzinga: S&P 500 Breadth Narrowest Since Dot-Com, Goldman Warns](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/economic-data/26/05/52210245/sp-500-market-breadth-narrowest-since-dot-com-goldman-sachs-warns)
- 2015 "FAANG-led narrow market" (also covered under the divergence section above): NASDAQ and cumulative volume breadth diverged from July 2015, with early FAANG stocks driving index performance while the average stock lagged — offered here again as a second concrete historical narrow-leadership example distinct from 2000 and 2007. — [search-aggregated summary of StockCharts Dec 2015 commentary](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-dancing-2015-12-a-new-breadth-of-fresh-air/)

### Inferences
- The two clearest "textbook" low-breadth/narrow-leadership episodes with well-documented before/after outcomes are 2000 (severe, ~78% Nasdaq decline over ~2.5 years) and 2007 (bearish A/D divergence preceding the 2008 bear market); 2015 is a documented divergence case but with a comparatively milder subsequent outcome (a correction/volatility episode in 2015-16 rather than a multi-year bear market), suggesting the severity of the eventual outcome is not uniform across low-breadth episodes and likely depends on concurrent valuation/macro conditions rather than breadth alone.

### Gaps
- Could not access full Bloomberg/Goldman text on the 2026 "narrowest since dot-com" claim (403 error), so the specific current breadth statistic underlying that comparison, and any explicit before/after prediction Goldman made, remains unconfirmed.
- No pre-GFC (2007) concentration/HHI-style statistic (analogous to the dot-com 24%/HHI-doubling data) was found — the 2007 case is documented via the A/D Line divergence dates only, not via a concentration statistic, so the two examples (2000 vs. 2007) are documented with different types of breadth evidence and are not directly comparable on the same metric.
- No additional non-US historical low-breadth episode (e.g., a specific Indian market historical narrow-leadership period with before/after data, comparable to 2000 or 2007 in the US) was found within the research budget.
