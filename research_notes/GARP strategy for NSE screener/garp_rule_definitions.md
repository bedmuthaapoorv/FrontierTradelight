# GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price): Quantitative Rule Definitions for an NSE / Nifty 500 Screener

Research notes compiled 2026-10-13. The scope covers every way GARP is specified, including rules that need analyst consensus (forward EPS, forward PEG, estimate revisions, earnings surprise). Each rule is tagged with the data it needs so the cost is visible: **[STMT]** = historical financial statements, **[PRICE]** = price/volume, **[CONS]** = analyst consensus estimates (forward EPS, long-term growth, revisions, surprise), **[OWN]** = shareholding/ownership data, **[PROP]** = proprietary vendor score (e.g. Zacks Rank). Note on access: the S&P DJI methodology PDF (Sept 2025 version), the S&P education papers, and the Indexology blog posts all returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch. The S&P details below come from search-engine extracts of those documents, not a full read. The NSE Multi-Factor methodology (Sept 2019) was downloaded and read in full.

## Q1. Core GARP metrics and their exact formulas (PEG, P/E, growth floors, quality filters)

### Takeaway
There are three formula families. (a) The **Lynch/PEG family** uses PEG = P/E ÷ EPS growth rate (in % units). Lynch's own variant adds dividend yield to the growth rate. The growth input can be trailing (5Y historical EPS growth) or forward (3–5Y consensus long-term growth), and P/E can be current-year or next-year estimate. (b) The **consensus-driven retail/quant family** (Fidelity, Zacks) combines forward P/E (F1), forward PEG, projected quarterly and annual EPS growth, estimate revisions (e.g. F1 revisions over 4 weeks > 5%), earnings surprise and Zacks Rank. (c) The **index family** (S&P DJI) avoids PEG altogether. It builds a Growth z-score from trailing 3-year EPS CAGR and 3-year sales-per-share CAGR, then a Quality/Value composite from ROE, financial leverage and E/P, using only [STMT]+[PRICE].

### Cited Findings
**Consensus / forward-estimate based GARP rules** (all need [CONS])
- **Fidelity GARP screen** (exact criteria): EPS growth last quarter vs same quarter prior year ≥ 5% [STMT]; TTM EPS growth vs prior TTM ≥ 10% [STMT]; **projected** this-quarter EPS growth vs same quarter prior year ≥ 5% [CONS]; **projected** this-year EPS growth vs last year ≥ 10% [CONS]; **P/E on this year's estimate ≤ 15** [CONS]; **PEG ≤ 1.0** [CONS]; price ≥ $5 and 90-day average volume ≥ 10K [PRICE]. Fidelity's description says the thresholds are meant to sit relative to market medians, but the screen lists fixed numbers. — [Fidelity: Growth at a Reasonable Price (GARP) screen](https://research2.fidelity.com/fidelity/screeners/commonstock/strategy.asp?simpleStrategy=%7BF61E8D5C-32C9-499B-8F6D-DD6DAF53EB78%7D)
- **Zacks GARP screens** (recurring 2024–2026 articles): PEG < industry median; P/E (F1, current-fiscal-year consensus) < industry median; % change in F1 EPS estimate over the last 4 weeks > 5% (**estimate revision** rule) [CONS]; Zacks Rank 1 or 2 [PROP, itself revision-driven]. Earlier Zacks GARP screens required both last-5Y historical EPS growth and projected 3–5Y EPS growth between 10% and 20%, ROE > industry average, and market cap > $1B. Earnings surprise appears as a descriptor (e.g. "beat consensus in each of trailing four quarters") rather than a hard screen. These are promotional articles; treat thresholds as starting points. — [Nasdaq/Zacks: Pick These 5 Lucrative GARP Stocks Using PEG Ratio](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/pick-these-5-lucrative-garp-stocks-using-peg-ratio); [Yahoo/Zacks: 4 GARP Stocks That Investors Can Scoop Up](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/4-garp-stocks-investors-scoop-142800512.html); [Yahoo/Zacks: 4 PEG-Driven GARP Stocks for 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/4-peg-driven-garp-stocks-190000713.html)
- **Zacks GARP Composite 35 Trust** (SmartTrust UIT, 15-month fixed portfolio, 35 stocks). Universe: NYSE/NASDAQ ranked by market cap and 3-month average daily volume. Then a sequential ranking tree: (1) **PEG**, defined as price-to-earnings ÷ the 12-month EPS growth rate of the **last 5 years** (trailing) [STMT]; (2) **share buyback** = shares outstanding one year ago ÷ current shares outstanding [STMT]; (3) **CFO-to-price** = TTM cash flow from operations ÷ price [STMT]; (4) **earnings surprise** = (actual EPS of last completed quarter − consensus estimate for that quarter) ÷ actual EPS [CONS]. — [SmartTrust: The World According to GARP (Jan 2015)](https://www.smarttrustuit.com/content/uploads/2015/01/SmartTrust_The-World-According-to-GARP_White-Paper.pdf)
- SmartTrust also notes that PEG may use current-year or next-year P/E. GARP strategists prefer PEG near 1 and generally below 2, and **PEG below ~0.5 may signal over-optimistic growth projections**. It suggests an absolute P/E band of 10–20 (US context, S&P 500 25-year average P/E 18.9) and positive TTM FCF (CFO − capex, summed over the last 4 quarters). It cites a 1998 Morgan Stanley study (1,000 largest US/Canada stocks, Jan 1986–Mar 1998): the 100 lowest-PEG stocks returned 18.7%/yr vs ~16.8% for the market. — [SmartTrust white paper](https://www.smarttrustuit.com/content/uploads/2015/01/SmartTrust_The-World-According-to-GARP_White-Paper.pdf)
- Practitioner guidance favours forward estimates because GARP buys future growth. "Most GARP investors" target PEG 0.8–1.5 (single practitioner blog). — [Growth Stock Investor: GARP strategy](https://www.growthstockinvestor.com/growth-stock-valuation/garp-strategy/)
- **Data availability in India [CONS]:** I found no authoritative statement of consensus coverage depth for the Nifty 500. One low-reliability syndicated item screened Nifty 500 stocks for coverage by ≥ 5 analysts, implying that coverage is uneven. — (no reliable source; see Gaps)

**Trailing / statement-based rules**
**PEG definitions**
- Standard PEG = P/E ÷ EPS growth rate. Lynch's modified version: P/E ÷ (earnings growth rate + dividend yield). AAII describes it as a "dividend-adjusted PEG". — [AAII: Lynch Screening Strategy](https://www.aaii.com/latest/article/11683-aaii-stock-ideas-lynch-screening-strategy); [AAII: Former Wall Street Investment Manager's Screening Strategy](https://www.aaii.com/latest/article/27126-former-wall-street-investment-managers-screening-strategy)
- Worked example: P/E 20 with 20% growth gives PEG 1.0, which Lynch treated as fair value. Lynch's checklist asks "whether the stock is selling at a P/E ratio at or near the growth rate". Per summaries of the book, he also said a 20% grower at 20x beats a 10% grower at 10x, all else equal. — [Novel Investor: One Up on Wall Street rules](https://novelinvestor.com/the-one-up-on-wall-street-rules/); [Old School Value: Lynch checklist](https://www.oldschoolvalue.com/tutorial/peter-lynchs-final-investment-checklist/) (secondary summaries, not the book text)
- Zacks defines PEG = P/E ÷ EPS growth, where growth is the **projected EPS growth over the next 3–5 years**, i.e. analyst consensus. Zacks itself says PEG ignores changing growth rates, such as high growth for 3 years followed by a lower sustainable rate. — [Zacks: Understanding PEG](https://www.zacks.com/stock-education/31/Understanding-PriceEarnings-to-Growth--PEG-)
- Zacks GARP screens use industry-relative cutoffs rather than absolute ones: "PEG Ratio less than X Industry Median" and "P/E Ratio (using F1) less than X Industry Median". F1 is the current-fiscal-year consensus EPS estimate. Some screens add Zacks Rank 1 or 2. — [Zacks GARP screen article via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/4-garp-stocks-discounted-peg-190000701.html); [TradingView/Zacks](https://www.tradingview.com/news/zacks:4136e3f58094b:0-4-low-peg-garp-stocks-that-perfectly-balance-growth-and-value/)

**Index-style (S&P DJI GARP) metric formulas** (from search extracts of the 2024 "A Systematic Approach to GARP" paper and the methodology)
- **3-Year EPS Growth** = CAGR from the EPS three years ago to the current fiscal-year EPS, with the **absolute value of the starting EPS in the denominator**. This lets negative-to-positive turnarounds produce a defined number. **3-Year SPS Growth** = 3-year CAGR of sales per share. — [S&P DJI: A Systematic Approach to GARP](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf)
- **Financial leverage** = latest total debt ÷ book value (a later formula version scales BVPS by common shares outstanding). There is special handling when BVPS is negative. **ROE** = trailing-12-month EPS ÷ latest BVPS. **Earnings-to-price** = TTM EPS ÷ price. — [S&P DJI: A Systematic Approach to GARP](https://spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf)
- Growth score = average of the z-scores of 3Y EPS growth and 3Y SPS growth. If one z-score is missing, the other is used alone. The QV composite = average of quality (ROE, leverage, inverted) and value (E/P) z-scores, with the same fallback when a quality z-score is missing. — [S&P DJI GARP methodology (Sept 2025)](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-garp-indices.pdf)
- S&P 500 Innovation GARP variant: adds R&D/EV, FCF/revenue and FCF/debt to its growth and quality sets. — [S&P DJI: A Systematic Approach to GARP](https://spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf)

**Indian (NSE) quality and value factor formulas** (useful building blocks; NSE has no GARP index)
- NSE Quality Z = (1/3)·Z(ROE) + (1/3)·(−Z(D/E)) + (1/3)·(−Z(EPS growth variability over previous 5 years)). For financial services companies: 0.5·Z(ROE) + 0.5·(−Z(EPS growth variability)), so D/E is dropped. — [NSE Multi-Factor Indices Methodology, Sept 2019](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf)
- NSE Value Z = 0.3·(−Z(P/E)) + 0.2·(−Z(P/B)) + 0.4·Z(ROCE) + 0.1·Z(Div Yield). When Quality is also in the index, the weights change to 0.3 / 0.3 / 0.2 / 0.2. — [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf)
- NSE converts a z-score into a positive factor score: Score = 1 + Z if Z > 0, and 1/(1 − Z) if Z < 0. Stocks are then ranked by percentile. — [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf)
- Eligibility data conditions: Quality requires positive EPS in each of the previous 6 financial years. Value requires positive PAT in the previous 1 financial year. — [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf)
- Nifty500 Multicap Momentum Quality 50 uses the same quality trio: ROE, D/E, and EPS growth variability over the previous 5 years. — [NSE factsheet: Nifty500 Multicap Momentum Quality 50](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/Factsheet_Nifty500MulticapMQ50.pdf)
- Nifty200 Value 30 value score uses E/P, B/P, S/P and dividend yield. — [NSE factsheet: Nifty200 Value 30](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/Factsheet_Nifty200_Value30.pdf)
- An NSE methodology excerpt reproduced by Mirae AMC defines YoY EPS growth = ΔEPS ÷ prior-year EPS, with a sign adjustment when prior EPS is negative. I could not confirm which index the excerpt belongs to. — [Mirae Asset index methodology document](https://www.miraeassetmf.co.in/docs/default-source/marketing/index-methodology.pdf?sfvrsn=da1e13d5_2)

**Lynch balance-sheet guidance**
- Debt "determines which companies will survive". Short-term debt is not a worry if cash covers it. Lynch described a normal balance sheet as 75% equity and 25% debt (≈ D/E 0.33). — [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/the-one-up-on-wall-street-rules/); [Old School Value](https://www.oldschoolvalue.com/tutorial/peter-lynchs-final-investment-checklist/) (secondary)
- AAII Lynch screen: total liabilities/total assets below the industry median. — [AAII](https://www.aaii.com/latest/article/27126-former-wall-street-investment-managers-screening-strategy)

### Inferences
- Every PEG variant in use can be written as PEG = P/E_x ÷ g_y. P/E_x is one of TTM, FY1 estimate or FY2 estimate. g_y is one of: 5Y historical EPS CAGR [STMT], 3Y historical CAGR [STMT], FY1/FY0 or FY2/FY1 consensus growth [CONS], or 3–5Y consensus long-term growth (LTG) [CONS]. Optionally add dividend yield to g (Lynch). The screener should make P/E basis and growth basis explicit parameters, because the published screens disagree on both.
- Signals that only consensus data can provide: forward P/E (F1), forward PEG, projected quarterly and annual EPS growth, % change in F1 estimates over 4 weeks (revisions), earnings surprise %, and vendor ranks. Each needs a point-in-time consensus history for backtesting, not just current snapshots, or the backtest will have look-ahead bias. That is an inference about data design, not a sourced claim.
- Trailing building blocks remain: 3Y EPS CAGR and 3Y revenue (or SPS) CAGR, using S&P's |EPS_t−3| denominator; trailing PEG = TTM P/E ÷ 5Y EPS CAGR (the Zacks UIT definition); buyback ratio; CFO/price.
- A screener-friendly quality block for India can copy NSE: ROE, D/E (skipped for financials), and a 5-year EPS growth variability measure (e.g. the standard deviation of YoY EPS growth). Add ROCE if a value/quality blend is wanted, following NSE's Value Z.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full S&P methodology text, so the exact winsorization/z-score formula and the precise BVPS-negative rule are from extracts only. The 2019 blog describes winsorizing at the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles, and the 2024 paper describes ROE outliers being assigned the 2.5th-percentile z-score.
- NSE does not publish the precise formula for "EPS growth variability" in the extracted document; it names the parameter but does not define it (e.g. stdev of YoY growth vs coefficient of variation).
- No standard GARP *index* specified a positive operating-cash-flow or FCF filter (only the S&P Innovation GARP variant uses FCF ratios). Practitioner sources (SmartTrust, positive TTM FCF; Zacks UIT, CFO/price rank) do use cash-flow criteria.
- I found no authoritative data on analyst consensus coverage (number of covered stocks or analysts per stock) for the Nifty 500, from LSEG I/B/E/S, Bloomberg, FactSet, Trendlyne or others. Searches returned only low-reliability syndicated content.
- I found no published GARP rule that sets a numeric earnings-surprise threshold. Surprise is used as a ranking input (Zacks UIT) or a descriptor (Zacks articles).

## Q2. Commonly used thresholds and how they differ across sources

### Takeaway
The thresholds are not standardized. The PEG cutoff ranges from 0.5 (AAII's dividend-adjusted Lynch) to 1.0 (Lynch "fair", Zacks "undervalued"), with 1.5–2.0 as the upper warning zone. Growth bands cluster around Lynch's 20–25% "fast grower" range, with a hard ceiling of 50% in AAII. Modern products (Zacks, S&P, AAII P/E test) mostly use relative cutoffs (industry median, top-N rank) rather than absolute numbers.

**Indian valuation context**
- Nifty 500 trailing P/E was about 22–24 through 2026 (Jan 23.9, Mar 22.0, Aug ~23.0), with a 3-year average of 24.27 (range 20.96–28.04). Readings were much higher in 2018–21, e.g. 44.7 in Jan 2021. ### Inferences
- With the Nifty 500 at ~23x trailing, an absolute US-style P/E cap of 15 would exclude most of the universe. Lynch's PEG ≈ 1 logic is unit-free, and a 1.0 cap on 15–25% growers implies P/E caps of 15–25x. Relative rules (P/E below sector median, PEG in the bottom tercile of the growth-eligible set) transfer to India more robustly than absolute caps.
- Forward-based rules transfer the same way. Fidelity's forward P/E ≤ 15 is a US absolute number that would not fit India at ~23x. Its PEG ≤ 1 and growth floors (projected FY growth ≥ 10%, projected quarter ≥ 5%) are unit-free and can carry over. Zacks-style industry-median-relative forward P/E/PEG plus a positive 4-week F1 revision rule needs no recalibration for Indian valuation levels.
- A plausible India rule set from these sources: 3Y EPS CAGR between 15% and 40–50% (Lynch band widened toward AAII's 50% ceiling); trailing PEG ≤ 1.0–1.5; P/E < sector median; ROE ≥ some floor; D/E ≤ ~0.5 for non-financials; positive EPS in each of the last 5–6 years (the NSE quality condition is 6). These specific cutoffs are my synthesis; no single source states them for India.

### Gaps
- I found no published, India-specific GARP threshold set (absolute PEG/P/E/growth numbers) from NSE, BSE or an AMC.
- I did not get primary-text confirmation of Lynch's exact PEG bands beyond "P/E at or near growth rate".

## Q3. How formal GARP indices and products define their methodology

### Takeaway
S&P DJI's GARP family is the canonical rules-based GARP index. It works sequentially: rank by Growth (3Y EPS and SPS growth), keep the top slice, rank those by Quality+Value (ROE, leverage, E/P), and keep the top half or so. Weights tilt by growth score, with 5% stock and 40% sector caps and a semi-annual rebalance. MSCI's version is a value/quality tilt overlay. FTSE Russell has no GARP index that I could find. In India, NSE and BSE have **no GARP index**. The nearest products are NSE's Quality/Value/multi-factor indices (building blocks), the MarketGrader India Growth Leaders Index (VanEck GLIN), and active funds (e.g. Mirae Asset Flexi Cap) that describe GARP only qualitatively.

### Cited Findings
**S&P DJI**
- S&P 500 GARP Index: rank by growth z-score (3Y EPS and SPS growth) and keep the **top 150**; rank those by QV composite z-score (ROE, financial leverage, E/P) and select the **top 75**. Weights are proportional to growth score, capped at 5% per stock and 40% per sector. — [S&P DJI GARP methodology](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-garp-indices.pdf); [Indexology 2022](https://www.indexologyblog.com/2022/08/26/sp-500-garp-index-growth-at-a-reasonable-price-anyone/)
- The 2024 S&P paper describes generalized cutoffs: top 30% on growth, then the top half of those on QV, with a **20% selection buffer** for existing constituents. — [S&P DJI: A Systematic Approach to GARP](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf)
- S&P 500 GARP 100 Index (launched Nov 2024): top 200 by Growth Score, then top 100 by QV composite. Weight = Growth Score × free-float market cap. S&P's rationale for growth first: "to avoid selecting growth companies with relatively high valuations". — [S&P 500 GARP 100 brochure](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/brochure/brochure-sp-500-garp-100-index.pdf); [Indexology 2025](https://www.indexologyblog.com/2025/07/09/introducing-the-sp-500-garp-100-index/)
- S&P MidCap 400 and SmallCap 600 GARP launched 1 May 2023 with the same logic; earlier history is back-tested. — [Indexology 2023](https://www.indexologyblog.com/2023/06/07/the-sp-garp-index-series-expands-to-include-sp-midcap-400-and-sp-smallcap-600-versions/)
- S&P World ex Australia GARP (Global X "GARP" ETF): top 500 by Growth Score, then 250 by QV (ROE, leverage, P/E). Weight = Growth Score × float market cap. Rebalanced semi-annually in **June and December**. — [Global X AU: Inside GARP methodology](https://www.globalxetfs.com.au/insights/post/inside-garp-methodology-rules-based-strategy/)
- S&P/ASX 200 GARP (Global X "GRPA"): top 150 by Growth, then 50 by QV using **ROE and P/E only**. Leverage is excluded as "less meaningful" because of the large weight of banks. Weight = QV score × float market cap. Rebalanced semi-annually in April and October. — [Global X AU](https://www.globalxetfs.com.au/insights/post/inside-garp-methodology-rules-based-strategy/)

**India**
- No NSE "Nifty500 GARP" index was found. Nifty 500 itself has no growth or valuation screen. — [NSE Nifty 500 whitepaper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/nifty-500-whitepaper-pdf.pdf)

- Nifty Growth Sectors 15 is not a growth-rate index. It selects sectors by sector P/E and P/B relative to Nifty 50, then ranks stocks by EPS growth *frequency*. Stocks with negative EPS in the review period are excluded, with a 15% cap. — [NSE: Nifty Growth Sectors 15 methodology](https://www.niftyindices.com/Methodology/Method_Nifty_Growth_Sectors_15.pdf)
- MarketGrader India Growth Leaders Index (tracked by VanEck GLIN): an all-cap index of "fundamentally strong Indian firms with attractive growth potential at reasonable prices". Detailed rules were not retrieved. — [VanEck/ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/tactical-allocation-content-hub/indias-garp-investing-edge-overcome-valuation-hurdles/)
- Mirae Asset Flexi Cap Fund names GARP as a core approach, with a "BMV" (Business-Management-Valuation) framework, holding high-quality growth businesses "up to a reasonable price" over long periods. No quantitative thresholds are given. — [Mirae Asset Flexi Cap Fund guide, Dec 2023](https://www.miraeassetmf.co.in/docs/default-source/product-guides/mafcf-dec2023.pdf?sfvrsn=5f3b19aa_2)

### Inferences
- The S&P recipe ports directly to the Nifty 500. Rank by Growth z (3Y EPS CAGR and 3Y sales CAGR), keep the top ~30% (≈150), rank those by QV z (ROE, −leverage, E/P), and keep ~50–75. Weight by growth score (or equal-weight) with a 5% stock cap, rebalance semi-annually, and keep a 20% rank buffer. NSE's own conventions (5% cap, semi-annual reconstitution, top-N forced inclusion and rank-50 forced exclusion buffer) are compatible with this design.
- For Indian banks/NBFCs, follow the GRPA and NSE precedents: drop the leverage/D/E term and score them on ROE and P/E (or P/B).

### Gaps
- MarketGrader India Growth Leaders methodology details (metrics, weights, rebalance frequency) were not retrieved; the VanEck page failed with a redirect loop.
- I could not confirm the S&P 500 GARP rebalance months from the primary PDF. Global X documents June/December for the World ex-Australia version, and S&P extracts mention semi-annual.
- I found no BSE/Asia Index GARP index and no SEBI scheme document with quantitative GARP rules.

## Q4. Edge case handling (negative or low earnings, extreme growth, cyclicals, one-offs, financials)

### Takeaway
Practitioners handle the edge cases mechanically. They require multi-year positive EPS, cap the maximum growth rate, use |base EPS| in CAGR denominators, winsorize at the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles, and drop leverage for financials. I found no source that explicitly adjusts for cyclicality or one-off items beyond the earnings-variability and positive-history requirements.

### Cited Findings
- **Negative or low earnings.** AAII Lynch requires 5 years of positive earnings. NSE Quality requires positive EPS in each of the previous 6 FYs, and NSE Value requires positive PAT in the latest FY. Nifty Growth Sectors 15 excludes negative-EPS stocks. — [AAII](https://www.aaii.com/latest/article/27126-former-wall-street-investment-managers-screening-strategy); [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf); [NSE Growth Sectors 15 methodology](https://www.niftyindices.com/Methodology/Method_Nifty_Growth_Sectors_15.pdf)
- **Negative base-year EPS in growth calculations.** S&P uses the absolute value of the starting EPS in the CAGR denominator. The NSE excerpt applies a sign adjustment when prior EPS is negative. — [S&P DJI paper](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf); [Mirae index methodology](https://www.miraeassetmf.co.in/docs/default-source/marketing/index-methodology.pdf?sfvrsn=da1e13d5_2)
- **Very high growth and PEG distortion.** AAII excludes 5Y EPS growth above 50%. Lynch was wary above 25%. — [AAII](https://www.aaii.com/latest/article/27126-former-wall-street-investment-managers-screening-strategy); [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/the-one-up-on-wall-street-rules/)
- **Outliers.** S&P winsorizes each fundamental at the 2.5th/97.5th percentile before z-scoring (2019 description). The 2024 paper describes ROE outliers being set to the 2.5th-percentile z-score. — [Indexology 2019: Using GARP Strategies for Indices](https://www.indexologyblog.com/2019/03/25/using-garp-strategies-for-indices/); [S&P DJI paper](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf)
- **Negative book value.** S&P has special handling for negative BVPS in the leverage calculation; the exact rule was not retrieved. — [S&P DJI paper](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf)
- **Missing data.** If one growth or quality z-score is missing, S&P uses the other alone. — [S&P methodology](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-garp-indices.pdf)
- **Financials.** AAII excludes the financial sector entirely. NSE drops D/E for financial services, using 50/50 ROE and EPS variability. S&P/ASX 200 GARP drops leverage because of bank weight. — [AAII](https://www.aaii.com/latest/article/11683-aaii-stock-ideas-lynch-screening-strategy); [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf); [Global X AU](https://www.globalxetfs.com.au/insights/post/inside-garp-methodology-rules-based-strategy/)
- **Earnings stability.** NSE penalizes 5-year EPS growth variability, which serves as an implicit cyclicality and one-off filter. — [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf)
- **Changing growth rates.** Zacks says PEG cannot capture high near-term growth that decays later. — [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock-education/31/Understanding-PriceEarnings-to-Growth--PEG-)

### Inferences
- Suggested computable handling for the screener: require EPS > 0 in each of the last 5 FYs (or 6, per NSE); compute PEG only when 3Y EPS CAGR is between 5% and 50%; winsorize inputs at 2.5/97.5; use 3Y CAGR rather than 1Y growth to dampen one-offs; add an EPS-growth-variability penalty for cyclicals; drop D/E for banks, NBFCs and insurers (and optionally score them on P/B instead of P/E).
- In India, exceptional items are common in reported PAT. A screener that uses PAT before exceptional items, where the data allows, would align with the "one-off" concern. No source mandated this.

### Gaps
- I found no source specifying cyclical normalization (e.g. average-earnings P/E) or explicit one-off/exceptional-item adjustments in a GARP index.

## Q5. Ranking vs filtering, and concrete scoring formulas

### Takeaway
The leading index approach is **sequential ranking**, not hard cut-offs: growth rank first, then a quality+value composite rank within the growth set. S&P's research rejects a pure PEG sort because the lowest-PEG buckets carry the highest volatility. Retail screens (AAII, Zacks) use hard filters, increasingly expressed relative to industry medians. NSE provides an explicit weighted z-score composite template.

### Cited Findings
- S&P: "an index simply focusing on the PEG ratio might not be sufficient… that's why we take a two-step sorting approach". In US and Australian data, the lowest-PEG groups historically had the highest return volatility, which dragged down risk-adjusted returns. — [S&P DJI: Understanding GARP Strategies](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/index-tv/article/understanding-garp-strategies); [S&P DJI: Bridging Value and Growth: Australia](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/research/research-bridging-value-and-growth-designing-a-garp-strategy-for-australia.pdf)
- S&P scoring in formula form (extracts): GrowthZ = mean(Z(3Y EPS CAGR), Z(3Y SPS CAGR)). QVZ = mean of quality Z (Z(ROE), −Z(Leverage)) and value Z (Z(E/P)). Select the top N1 by GrowthZ, then the top N2 by QVZ. Weight ∝ GrowthZ (or GrowthScore × FMC), with 5% and 40% caps. The extract describes QV as averaging two quality factors and one value factor; whether the average is equal over three metrics or over the two pillars was not confirmed. — [S&P methodology](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-garp-indices.pdf); [S&P brochure](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/brochure/brochure-sp-500-garp-100-index.pdf)
- NSE composite: weighted z-scores, converted to factor score = 1+Z (Z>0) or 1/(1−Z) (Z<0), then percentile ranked. Multi-factor selection takes the top 30 by weighted average percentile score, with weights from the weighted average factor z-score. — [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf)
- AAII/Zacks: hard filters (PEG ≤ 0.5; P/E < industry median; PEG < industry median). — [AAII](https://www.aaii.com/latest/article/11683-aaii-stock-ideas-lynch-screening-strategy); [Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/4-garp-stocks-discounted-peg-190000701.html)
- Zacks GARP Composite 35 UIT: a sequential ranking tree of PEG (5Y historical), then buyback ratio, then CFO/price, then earnings surprise [CONS], ending with 35 stocks. — [SmartTrust white paper](https://www.smarttrustuit.com/content/uploads/2015/01/SmartTrust_The-World-According-to-GARP_White-Paper.pdf)
- Fidelity: pure hard filters, all criteria joined by AND. — [Fidelity GARP screen](https://research2.fidelity.com/fidelity/screeners/commonstock/strategy.asp?simpleStrategy=%7BF61E8D5C-32C9-499B-8F6D-DD6DAF53EB78%7D)
- MSCI: tilt multipliers on cap weights (0.25–3.5) based on value and quality score buckets. — [MSCI Quality GARP Select](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/48032710/MSCI-USA-Quality-GARP-Select-Index_ProductInsight.pdf/c74dd866-2897-46c6-48b0-4b599337740b)

### Inferences
- A hybrid design fits a plain-English rule compiler. First apply hard eligibility gates (positive EPS 5Y, growth 0–50%, D/E cap for non-financials, liquidity). Then rank sequentially (GrowthZ top 30%, then QVZ top 50–60 names), optionally adding a PEG ≤ 1.5 sanity gate.

### Gaps
- I found no published head-to-head of sequential vs single-composite GARP scoring, including turnover.

## Q6. Holding period, rebalance frequency and turnover

### Takeaway
Rules-based GARP and Indian factor indices rebalance **semi-annually** (S&P global: June/December; S&P/ASX: April/October; NSE multi-factor: semi-annual). Rank buffers limit churn: S&P uses a 20% selection buffer, and NSE forces inclusion of the top 10 and excludes a holding only when its rank falls below 50. Active GARP managers (Lynch, Mirae) describe multi-year holding. I found no published turnover figures.

### Cited Findings
- S&P World ex-Australia GARP: semi-annual, June and December. S&P/ASX 200 GARP: semi-annual, April and October. — [Global X AU](https://www.globalxetfs.com.au/insights/post/inside-garp-methodology-rules-based-strategy/)
- S&P GARP: 20% buffer for existing constituents (2024 paper). — [S&P DJI paper](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/education-a-systematic-approach-to-garp.pdf)
- NSE multi-factor: semi-annual reconstitution. Top 10 compulsorily included; existing constituent excluded if its rank falls below 50. Quarterly checks for SEBI concentration-norm compliance. Weights drift between rebalances. — [NSE Multi-Factor Methodology](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_Methodology.pdf)
- Zacks GARP Composite 35 Trust: fixed 35-stock portfolio held for a 15-month UIT term, with no intra-term rebalancing (UIT portfolios "generally remain fixed"). — [SmartTrust white paper](https://www.smarttrustuit.com/content/uploads/2015/01/SmartTrust_The-World-According-to-GARP_White-Paper.pdf)
- Mirae Asset Flexi Cap: holds high-quality growth businesses "over an extended period". — [Mirae MAFCF guide](https://www.miraeassetmf.co.in/docs/default-source/product-guides/mafcf-dec2023.pdf?sfvrsn=5f3b19aa_2)

### Inferences
- For NSE, semi-annual rebalancing aligned to post-results data availability is a natural default, using March-FY annual numbers by around June and H1 data by around November. A 20% rank buffer or an NSE-style hold-until-rank-falls-below-threshold rule would limit turnover. Annual data inputs (3Y CAGR) change slowly, so quarterly rebalancing would mostly respond to price moves through P/E.

### Gaps
- No turnover statistics for the S&P GARP indices or any Indian GARP-style product were retrieved; the relevant S&P documents were blocked (403).
- No typical holding period was quantified for Lynch-style or Indian active GARP funds.
