# Weekend Effect (Monday / Day-of-Week Effect): Literature, with India (NSE/BSE) Focus

Scope note: research done 2026-10-22 through web search. Many primary PDFs (Boston Fed Fortune 1998, Edinburgh thesis, MPRA 46805, IMFI Hsaio 2004) could not be read (compressed binary PDFs, 403 errors or timeouts), so several findings come from abstracts, RePEc pages and secondary summaries. These are marked. No numbers below were filled in from memory. Where a commonly quoted number could not be verified, it is listed under Gaps.

## 1. Classic US findings (pre-1990, older-only evidence): how large was the negative Monday, and is it in the weekend gap or in Monday trading?

### Takeaway
Classic US studies (data roughly 1928 to the early 1980s) found negative mean Friday-close-to-Monday-close returns for the S&P Composite and for firms of all sizes. Rogalski (1984) placed the loss mainly in the non-trading weekend gap (Friday close to Monday open), not in Monday trading hours. I could not verify the exact effect sizes (e.g. French's Monday mean) from accessible sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Cross (1973)** first documented the "non-random movements" that French (1980) later called the weekend effect. — [ASU / Smith & Robins summary](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20171204-research-debunks-myth-stock-market-weekend-effect)
- **French (1980)**, S&P Composite 1953–1977: Monday returns were significantly negative, and in each five-year sub-period the average Monday return was negative and lower than any other weekday. (Older-only, US.) — [Monday effect on the JSE (SAJBM), lit review](https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/download/1064/1005)
- **Gibbons & Hess (1981)**, *Journal of Business* 54, 579–596, is cited with French as a founding Monday-effect paper. The accessible sources gave no magnitudes. — [SAJBM reference list](https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/download/867/805)
- **Keim & Stambaugh (1984)**: Monday returns were consistently negative for the S&P Composite as early as 1928, for exchange-listed firms of all sizes, and for actively traded OTC stocks. The Friday–Monday correlation was positive for Dow stocks, which is inconsistent with general measurement-error explanations. A secondary source says they report a strong link between Friday returns and firm size. (Older-only, US.) — [summary via search of SAJBM / FMG DP568 lit reviews](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/publications/DP568.pdf)
- **Rogalski (1984)** split the weekend return into Friday-close-to-Monday-open and Monday-open-to-Monday-close. He found that the weekend effect is "really a Friday-close-to-Monday-open effect" (i.e. it sits in the non-trading period). — [SAJBM JSE study lit review](https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/download/1064/1005); [IMFI Hsaio 2004 (search snippet)](https://businessperspectives.org/images/pdf/applications/publishing/templates/article/assets/807/imfi_en_2004_01_Hsaio.pdf)
- **Smith & Robins (2017, ASU)** used US data back to 1926. Monday returns "averaged down by 18.1 points" in 1926–1974 but only about 5 points in 1975–2014, which is not statistically significant. They argue the effect faded after about 1975. (US. "Points" is the units used in the press release, probably basis points, but this is unconfirmed.) — [ASU news](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20171204-research-debunks-myth-stock-market-weekend-effect); [ASU news mirror](https://news.asu.edu/node/72641)
- **Lakonishok & Maberly (1990, JF 45(1))**: individuals trade relatively more on Mondays and raise sell transactions relative to buys. This may explain at least part of the weekend effect. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v45y1990i1p231-43.html); [PDF (AUEB mirror)](https://eclass.aueb.gr/modules/document/file.php/ODE212/%CE%91%CE%A1%CE%98%CE%A1%CE%9F%CE%93%CE%A1%CE%91%CE%A6%CE%99%CE%91/Lakonishok%20and%20Maberly.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Friday-close-to-Monday-close trade, the classic literature says most of the negative return came from the overnight weekend gap. An NSE study should therefore split Friday close to Monday open from Monday open to Monday close.

### Gaps
- I could not verify these commonly quoted magnitudes from an accessible source: French's (1980) Monday mean (often quoted as about -0.17% per day, or about -33% annualised), Gibbons & Hess's (1981) S&P 500 / CRSP Monday means, and Keim & Stambaugh's per-size numbers. The primary papers should be consulted before quoting them.
- I did not obtain the full text of Fortune (1998, Boston Fed WP 98-6, "Weekends Can Be Rough"). Only its abstract-level result is available (see section 2).

## 2. Did the effect disappear or reverse after the 1980s–90s? The "reverse weekend effect" and post-2000 US and global evidence

### Takeaway
US evidence: after about 1987 the negative Monday effect disappeared in large-cap indices and in several studies reversed to significantly positive Mondays (the "reverse weekend effect"). It persisted longer in small-cap and broad indices (Russell 2000, NASDAQ, CRSP) and after negative Fridays. By the mid-1990s to 2000s it was largely insignificant. Global studies report it has disappeared in the US, UK, France and Germany, with pockets of persistence in some emerging markets. Calendar anomalies in general decay after publication.

### Cited Findings
- **Mehdian & Perry (2001, JBFA 28(7-8))**, US 1964–1999, five indices: before 1987 Monday returns were significantly negative in all five indices. After 1987 the large-cap indices (NYSE, S&P 500, DJ Composite) showed a significant **reversal**, with significantly positive Monday returns. (US.) — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jbfnac/v28y2001i7-8p1043-1065.html)
- **Fortune (1998, Boston Fed)**: a large, statistically significant negative weekend return before 1987, and **no weekend drift after 1987**. Weekend volatility stayed higher than on ordinary weekdays. (US.) — [Boston Fed WP 98-6](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/research-department-working-paper/1998/weekends-can-be-rough-revisiting-the-weekend-effect-in-stock-prices.aspx); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedbwp/98-6.html)
- **Brusa, Liu & Schulman (2000, JBFA)**: the traditional weekend effect is weak in small firms, while the "reverse" weekend effect is strong and significant in large-firm portfolios. — [RePEc JBFA 27(5-6)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jbfnac/v27y2000i5-6p555-574.html)
- **Brusa, Liu & Schulman (2003, JBFA 30, 863–890)**, "The Weekend and 'Reverse' Weekend Effects": analysis by month, week of month and industry. — [reference list](https://etd.lib.ncu.edu.tw/detail/603bd5cdbd14a2623d55a45a7aab57f0)
- **Brusa, Liu & Schulman (2005, JBFA 32(7-8))**, 1988–1998: Monday returns were significantly positive and higher than on other weekdays. Large-firm Monday returns followed positive Fridays but did not follow negative Fridays. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jbfnac/v32y2005i7-8p1495-1517.html)
- **Brusa, Hernandez & Liu (2011, Managerial Finance 37(9))**: the reverse weekend effect is widely spread among large individual firms. Volume and illiquidity explain it only partly. — [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/03074351111153212/full/ris); [special issue on day-of-week anomalies](https://emerald.com/insight/publication/issn/0307-4358/vol/37/iss/9)
- **Cho, Linton & Whang (2006, LSE/FMG, stochastic dominance)**: the Monday effect reversed or weakened in the DJIA and S&P 500 after 1987, but was "still strong" in broader indices (NASDAQ, Russell 2000, CRSP). (US.) — [FMG](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/publications/discussion-papers/are-there-monday-effects-stock-returns-stochastic-dominance-approach); [LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/24520)
- A secondary review says the Monday effect had dissipated for all stock sizes by the mid-1990s, with Monday returns generally not different from zero. Pettengill (2003), as cited, finds small caps negative and significant, mid caps no effect, and large caps positive and significant on Mondays. (US; secondary.) — [search summary of Pace / SAJBM lit reviews](https://activityinsight.pace.edu/mmorey/intellcont/7117-1.pdf)
- **Kamara (1997)** (as cited): the weekend effect disappeared over time for the S&P 500. Transaction costs may keep it alive in small caps while institutions arbitrage it in large caps. — [MPRA 54185 summary](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/54185/)
- **CXO Advisory**, S&P 500 1981–2010 (practitioner): little support for exploitable day-of-week anomalies. A Monday reversal appeared in the latest five-year sub-period, but sub-period inconsistency undermines reliability. (US.) — [CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/any-recent-day-of-the-week-anomalies)
- A 2004 US study (IMFI) found the weekend effect still present in 1994–2000, concentrated in mid-size stocks and in Mondays after negative Fridays. — [IMFI Hsaio 2004 (search snippet)](https://businessperspectives.org/images/pdf/applications/publishing/templates/article/assets/807/imfi_en_2004_01_Hsaio.pdf)
- **Global, 15 exchanges (Eunomia journal)**: the weekend effect no longer exists in the USA. It has disappeared in France, Germany and the UK and in almost all Latin American markets studied, except Venezuela and Chile. (Global; sample period not visible in snippets.) — [Eunomia article 268](https://ojs.akademiarac.edu.pl/index.php/eunomia/en/article/view/268)
- **Cai, Kazemi, He & Zhai**, 60 indices: short sellers could explain the weekend effect "until about a decade ago", and this link is gradually dissipating in developed markets. (Global.) — [MPRA 54185](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/54185/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/pra/mprapa/54185.html)
- **Plastun, Sibande, Gupta & Wohar (2019)**, "Rise and fall of calendar anomalies over a century": by title, a long-run decline in calendar anomalies. I could not read the findings. — [Plastun RePEc page](https://ideas.repec.org/e/ppl82.html)
- **McLean & Pontiff (2016)**, 97 published predictors: post-publication returns are about 58% lower than in-sample (one summary gives 0.58% to 0.26% a month). Investors appear to learn from publication. (US; general anomaly decay, not weekend-specific.) — [arXiv 2105.01380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380); [arXiv 2209.13623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13623); [BC news](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/schools/carroll-school/news/2017/pontiff-paper.html)
- **Diminishing weekend effect in five G7 countries** (Gu): listed by title only. — [academia.edu](https://independent.academia.edu/AnthonyGu5)

### Inferences
- Under the "reverse weekend effect", large caps had positive Mondays after about 1987 while small caps kept weak Mondays. A Nifty 50 (large-cap) test and a smallcap-index test may therefore disagree.
- Large-cap US evidence after 2000 points to no reliable Monday effect. The prior for a persistent, tradable Nifty 50 weekend effect today should be low.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed US paper with a 2000–2026 sample that gives a specific Monday mean. The Motley Fool claim that the pattern "has since returned" after 1987–1998 could not be traced to the original Fed paper.
- Hou, Xue & Zhang's "Replicating Anomalies" covers cross-sectional anomalies. I found nothing showing that it tests the day-of-week effect.
- Sullivan, Timmermann & White (2001, data snooping and calendar effects) did not come up in searches. It is commonly cited as showing that calendar effects lose significance once data-mining is accounted for, but I did not verify this here.

## 3. Explanations: settlement, Friday bad news, short-selling, individual investors, mood, and overnight vs intraday

### Takeaway
No single explanation dominates. The evidence supports (a) firms releasing worse news after the Friday close, (b) individual investors' net selling on Mondays, (c) speculative short sellers closing positions before the weekend and reopening on Monday (contested), and (d) settlement-lag mechanics, which matter especially in India's former weekly settlement. The loss historically sat in the weekend gap (close to open).

### Cited Findings
- **Friday news timing**: Penman (1987) and Damodaran (1989) found support for firms releasing bad news after the Friday close. — [summary in Hsaio 2004 / EFMA 2007 lit review](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0263.pdf)
- **DellaVigna & Pollet (NBER w11683, 2005; JF 2009)**, US earnings announcements: Friday announcements have a 15% lower immediate response and a 70% higher delayed response. The delayed share is about 60% on Fridays vs 40% on other days. Abnormal volume is about 10% lower. Friday announcements have a 45% higher probability of a negative surprise and a 50 bp lower abnormal return. A long-short Friday-drift portfolio earns "substantial" abnormal returns. (US.) — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11683); [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11683.pdf); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2013/02/pay-attention-to-friday-earnings-announcements/)
- **Counter-evidence (Michaely et al.)**: the Friday underreaction is tied to after-hours Friday releases, and firm fixed effects remove it. Inattention is "not the reason" for the weaker Friday reaction. (Venue not confirmed.) — [search summary; see causal-claims page](https://causalclaims.trfetzer.com/paper/w11683.html)
- **Short-selling, Chen & Singal (2003, JF 58(2))**: speculative short sellers close positions on Friday and reopen on Monday. Supported by comparisons of high vs low short-interest stocks, optioned vs non-optioned stocks, IPOs and high-volatility stocks. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i2p685-705.html)
- **Challenge**: Christophe, Ferri & Angel found that speculative short selling explains no economically meaningful part of the Monday–Friday return gap, even among the most-shorted firms. — [JOIM](https://joim.com/do-short-sellers-cause-the-weekend-effect); [Creighton](https://researchworks.creighton.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Speculative-Short-Sellers-Put-Options-and/991005972435802656)
- **Hong Kong**: after short selling was allowed, individual stocks showed a stronger weekend effect. — [search summary, EFMA 2007 paper 0260](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0260.pdf)
- **Individual investors**: Lakonishok & Maberly (1990), plus Abraham & Ikenberry (1994) and Brooks & Kim (1997) as cited, find individuals more active and net sellers on Mondays. — [RePEc L&M](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v45y1990i1p231-43.html); [JFQA "The Individual Investor and the Weekend Effect"](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/individual-investor-and-the-weekend-effect/320824DC60F3ED5C68212A3DF5DC8574)
- **India, individual investors**: a study of Indian individual investors found significantly negative average Monday returns, attributed partly to dependence on broker calls rather than a simple sell-off. — [i-scholar JCAR 214851](https://www.i-scholar.in/index.php/jcar/article/view/214851/0)
- **Arbitrage / institutions**: one study argues that the timing and size of the change in US Monday returns are inconsistent with institutional arbitrage as the cause of its decline. — [Creighton "Arbitrage, Institutional Investors and the Monday effect"](https://researchworks.creighton.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Arbitrage-Institutional-Investors-and-the-Monday/991005972434702656)
- **Settlement (India)**: under fixed-day weekly settlement, Nifty returns were inflated on Wednesday, the first day of the settlement cycle. The effect vanishes once returns are adjusted for settlement lag (Badhani & Tripathy 2010). — [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/filter.php?aid=29742); [RePEc IJBEMA 2(1)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijbema/v2y2010i1p23-42.html)
- **Overnight vs intraday**: Rogalski (1984) puts the effect in the Friday-close-to-Monday-open gap (see section 1). Fortune (1998) finds weekend volatility stays higher even after the drift disappears. — [Boston Fed](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/research-department-working-paper/1998/weekends-can-be-rough-revisiting-the-weekend-effect-in-stock-prices.aspx)

### Inferences
- In India, the settlement-mechanics channel (weekly Wednesday settlement before 2001–02) was probably the main driver of pre-2002 day-of-week patterns, not the US-style Monday weakness.
- For NSE, Friday post-close corporate disclosures (results are often filed after market hours) are the plausible analogue of the DellaVigna–Pollet channel. A stock-level Monday-gap study conditioned on Friday filings would test it.

### Gaps
- No mood/weather-based weekend-effect evidence was retrieved.
- No recent (2020s) study splitting overnight vs intraday weekend returns was found for the US or India. The Lou, Polk & Skouras "tug of war" overnight/intraday work did not come up.

## 4. Cross-section: small caps, volatility, limits to arbitrage, and conditioning on the prior Friday/week

### Takeaway
The negative Monday effect was historically strongest in small caps and in hard-to-short or volatile stocks. After 1987 large caps flipped to positive Mondays. Conditioning on the prior Friday matters: Monday effects are concentrated after down Fridays, especially in small-cap indices.

### Cited Findings
- **International, 15 countries / 40 indices**: conditioning Monday returns on the sign of the previous Friday gave a significant difference for 11 of 13 small-cap indices but only 4 of 14 large-cap indices. — [Edinburgh ERA thesis (search snippet)](https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstreams/bbda7f15-25e0-43d4-91f6-162e14acde23/download)
- **Brusa, Liu & Schulman (2005)**: large-firm Monday returns follow positive Fridays but not negative Fridays. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jbfnac/v32y2005i7-8p1495-1517.html)
- A US 1994–2000 study found the weekend effect concentrated in mid-size stocks and after negative Fridays. — [IMFI 2004](https://businessperspectives.org/images/pdf/applications/publishing/templates/article/assets/807/imfi_en_2004_01_Hsaio.pdf)
- **Chen & Singal (2003)**: the effect is stronger in high short-interest, non-optioned, IPO and high-volatility stocks. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i2p685-705.html)
- Miller's view (as cited): the effect should be larger in small caps with wide spreads and less information. — [EFMA 2007 0263 lit review](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0263.pdf)
- **Australia (Sharif)**: the weekend effect shifted from small to large stocks, and was absent in equal- and value-weighted indices in the latest 10 years of data. — [JISR-MSSE](https://jisrmsse.szabist.edu.pk/index.php/szabist/article/view/96)
- **January**: Al-Rjoub, Hassan & Varela (2003) find significantly positive Monday returns in the first week of January, inversely related to size. — [UNO WP 2003-05](https://ideas.repec.org/p/uno/wpaper/2003-05.html)

### Inferences
- An NSE test should be split by size (Nifty 50 vs Midcap vs Smallcap) and conditioned on the Friday (or week) return sign. Those are the dimensions where effects survived longest elsewhere.

### Gaps
- Abraham & Ikenberry (1994) full results (Monday after down vs up Friday) were not retrieved. Only citations were found.
- No India-specific study conditioning Monday returns on Friday's sign was found, apart from a non-peer-reviewed practitioner note (section 6).

## 5. India: day-of-week effects in Nifty, Sensex and NSE stocks, and whether settlement, T+1 or expiry changes altered them

### Takeaway
Indian evidence is inconsistent and depends on the specification. Before rolling settlement (2001–02), a **Wednesday effect** tied to weekly settlement was common. After rolling settlement / T+2 (2003), most studies find no significant day-of-week effect in index returns. Where effects show up post-2000 they are often **positive Mondays** (not negative) or positive Wednesdays, with a negative or low Tuesday in some studies. The classic US-style negative Monday is not a robust feature of Nifty or Sensex. I found no study on the T+1 transition (2023) or on weekly-expiry-day changes.

### Cited Findings
**Settlement-regime studies**
- **Badhani & Kavidayal (2008)**, NSE 1995–2005: the Wednesday effect from the weekly-settlement era has disappeared. Monday and Tuesday returns remain low but are no longer significantly different. Monday has low volume and high volatility. — [search summary of IJFM / i-scholar](https://i-scholar.in/index.php/ijfm/article/view/40948)
- **Badhani & Tripathy (2010)**: Wednesday was inflated under fixed-day weekly settlement and vanishes after adjusting for settlement lag. No day-of-week effect after rolling settlement. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijbema/v2y2010i1p23-42.html)
- **Sen (2013), GARCH-M on Nifty**: a day-of-week effect existed before T+2 rolling settlement and **vanished after T+2**. A significant day effect remained in conditional volatility, mainly on Tuesday. — [IJFM "An investigation of the day-of-the-week effect on return and volatility of NSE Nifty"](https://publishingindia.com/archive/ijfm/an-investigation-of-the-day-of-the-week-effect-on-return-and-volatility-of-nse-nifty)
- A 1995–2007 study: after rolling settlement, no day-of-week effect in returns. — [Seasonality in NSE indices (inflibnet)](https://gnanaganga.inflibnet.ac.in:8443/jspui/bitstream/123456789/7901/1/Seasonality%20in%20National%20Stock%20Exchange%20Indices.pdf)
- **Khanna**, 203 NSE firms: no effect overall. A Friday effect before rolling settlement and a slight Wednesday effect after. — [IUP IJAF](https://www.iupindia.in/205/IJAF_Day_of_the_Week_Effect_5.html)
- A high-frequency study: before rolling settlement (January 2002), Monday and Friday were significant. After it, Friday became significant. — [publishingindia 1824](https://www.publishingindia.com/storage/PDFBrochures/1824.pdf)

**Index studies with post-2000 data**
- **Aziz & Ansari (MPRA 46805)**, Sensex and Nifty 1990–2013: the traditional (negative) Monday effect is "non-existent". There is a **positive Monday effect in Sensex** and a positive Wednesday effect in Nifty over the full sample. — [MPRA](https://ideas.repec.org/p/pra/mprapa/46805.html)
- **Srinivasan & Kalaivani**, Nifty and Sensex 1997–2012 (GARCH): positive Monday and Wednesday effects. — [inferred from search summary; see MPRA 46805 / publishingindia](https://publishingindia.com/storage/PDFBrochures/1795.pdf)
- **Gnanasekar & Rajesh**, Nifty 2005–2015: higher returns on Mondays, a "robust Monday effect" (positive). — [publishingindia 8019](https://publishingindia.com/storage/PDFBrochures/8019.pdf)
- **Kothari et al.**, BSE/NSE indices 2005–2014: only Nifty Junior was significant (t-test), and nothing was significant under ANOVA. — [search summary](https://www.indianjournalofentrepreneurship.com/index.php/ijrcm/article/download/112884/83033/286963)
- **Sharma**, Sensex/Nifty 2008–2009: no day-of-week effect. — [IJCMSS RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aii/ijcmss/v2y2011i6p25-30.html)
- **Panda (2018, Theoretical Economics Letters)**, Nifty 50 / Midcap 50 / Smallcap 50, April 2005–June 2018 (GARCH(1,1)): Tuesday returns are lower than Monday's. There is a strong positive weekend/Monday effect alongside a negative Tuesday effect in all three indices. — [SSRN 2981633](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2981633); [author page](https://independent.academia.edu/AjayakumarPanda7)
- **Kumar & Jawa (2017)**, Nifty 50 1995–2015: a significant Wednesday effect, plus a December effect. — [search summary, Inderscience / RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/intjaf/v11y2023i3p181-201.html)
- **Saxena, Purohit & Malhotra (2021)**, January 2011–January 2018: no day-of-week effect in any of four Nifty indices. — [RePEc gbusec 24(1)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/gbusec/v24y2021i1p43-58.html)
- **"Diminishing calendar anomalies: case of Indian equity markets" (Inderscience, 2020)**: by title, calendar anomalies in India are diminishing. Details not retrieved. — [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/offers.php?id=111999)
- **Elangovan, Gnanasekar & Parayitam (2023)**, Nifty 50 and Sensex, April 2011–March 2021: significant positive Wednesday coefficient. — [RePEc intjaf 11(3)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/intjaf/v11y2023i3p181-201.html)
- **Yadav & Kadian (December 2024)**, COVID period 11 March 2020–30 September 2021, Sensex / Nifty 50 / SX40, dummy regression: Monday returns negative, significant only for Sensex. — [JCAR](https://www.publishingindia.com/archive/jcar/day-of-the-week-effect-during-covid-19-pandemic-an-empirical-study-of-indian-stock-market)
- A broad review: "no general comment can be made" on the presence of a day-of-week effect in India. India's seasonality differs from patterns commonly seen elsewhere. — [NBU thesis](https://ir.nbu.ac.in/handle/123456789/2958)
- The settlement cycle is now T+1 for most equity trades (practitioner source). — [Jainam blog](https://www.jainam.in/blog/stock-market-settlement/)

### Inferences
- After 2003 (T+2) and especially after 2010, the weight of Indian index evidence is **no robust day-of-week effect**. The isolated significant findings (positive Monday, positive Wednesday, negative Tuesday, a COVID-era negative Sensex Monday) are not consistent across samples or methods. This pattern looks like data-mining noise or regime-specific effects.
- Indian findings run the opposite way from classic US results: where significant, Mondays were usually positive (Aziz & Ansari, Srinivasan & Kalaivani, Gnanasekar & Rajesh, Panda).
- Weekly index-option expiry (Nifty on Thursday for years, later moved, plus SEBI's 2024–25 changes limiting weekly expiries) could plausibly create expiry-day patterns that look like day-of-week effects. No published study tests this.

### Gaps
- I could not find or verify these named Indian papers: **Choudhary & Choudhary, Kumari & Mahendra, Sarma, Raj & Kumari, Kiran & Rao, and Nageswari**. Search found no matching abstracts. Choudhry (2000), on Asian emerging markets including India, is cited as a seasonality study with a misspecified conditional mean. The others need direct lookup (e.g. Vikalpa, Indian Journal of Finance, Journal of Emerging Market Finance).
- I found **no study on the effect of T+1 settlement (phased 2022–January 2023)** on day-of-week returns.
- I found **no study on weekly-expiry-day changes** (expiry-day shifts or the SEBI 2024 rationalisation) and day-of-week returns in the cash market.
- I found no NSE stock-level study of Friday-close-to-Monday-open gaps after 2015. Computing it directly from NSE bhavcopy / index OHLC data is the recommended route.
- Effect sizes (mean daily % by weekday) for most Indian studies were not visible in the abstracts. Only signs and significance were recovered.

## 6. Is it tradable after Indian costs (STT, brokerage)?

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed study showing a day-of-week strategy on Nifty/NSE that survives STT, brokerage, stamp duty and slippage. Given that post-2003 effects are mostly insignificant and inconsistent, and given US evidence that calendar effects are unstable and decay, it is very unlikely to be tradable net of costs today.

### Cited Findings
- **Practitioner (non-peer-reviewed)**: a "fade Friday's direction" Monday-reversal rule closed green in roughly 52–55% of Nifty weeks in 2021–2025 before costs. It reportedly improves with a filter for large, gapped Fridays. The source warns that STT on option sells and theta can consume most of the small gains. Methodology unverified. — [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/strategies/monday-reversal-strategy)
- **US**: the decline in the Monday effect is not well explained by institutional arbitrage after lower costs. Another study found a simple rule profitable after costs, but not reliably every week. — [Creighton](https://researchworks.creighton.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Arbitrage-Institutional-Investors-and-the-Monday/991005972434702656); [Otago Magazine](https://www.otago.ac.nz/otagomagazine/issue42/inbrief/monday-returns)
- **Kamara (1997)** (as cited): transaction costs can keep the effect alive in small caps, where it is not arbitraged. — [MPRA 54185](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/54185/)
- **CXO (US, 1981–2010)**: little support for exploitable day-of-week anomalies. — [CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/any-recent-day-of-the-week-anomalies)
- **NSE calendar anomalies** (Management Prudence Journal): abstract only. — [publishingindia MPJ](https://publishingindia.com/archive/mpj/the-calendar-anomalies-in-indian-stock-market-a-special-reference-to-nse)

### Inferences
- A Friday-buy / Monday-sell (or the reverse) delivery trade in Indian cash equities pays STT on both legs for delivery (0.1% each side under the current schedule, not verified in this research), plus stamp duty, exchange fees and slippage. That is roughly 20–30 bp per round trip, which exceeds any plausible post-2010 weekday mean difference in Nifty returns (reported effects are generally single-digit bp and insignificant). Index futures are cheaper but carry basis risk. Any test must be net of these costs.
- If an edge exists, it is more likely conditional (after large down Fridays, in small or illiquid stocks, or around Friday after-hours disclosures) than unconditional. But those are exactly the stocks where costs and spreads are highest.

### Gaps
- No academic Indian study that explicitly nets STT/brokerage against a day-of-week rule was found.
- Exact current STT rates and their changes (e.g. Budget 2024 changes for F&O) were not verified here.
